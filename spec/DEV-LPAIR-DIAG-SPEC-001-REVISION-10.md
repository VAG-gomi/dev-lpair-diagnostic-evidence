## 2. K-gate unevaluability, classification, and accounting — four states, separately defined (R10-01)

### 2.1 The four objects, defined before any rule (governing)

For any K-gate check G (KX2a/b/c/d-span/d-open/e/f, and any analogous gate check defined in REV-06 §2 / REV-07 §5, retained), a future report must state **four separate things**, never collapsed into one label:

1. **Gate-check state** — the result of evaluating G on the record: PASS (evaluated, no contradiction), FAIL (evaluated, contradiction demonstrated → defect outcome per §2.2), UNEVALUABLE-EXCEPTION (cannot be evaluated, but §3 comparison inputs are present and sufficient — §2.3), or UNEVALUABLE-PENDING (cannot be evaluated, and §3 comparison inputs are insufficient or the defect determination is outcome-determining without G — §2.4). UNEVALUABLE in either form is neither PASS nor FAIL (REV-09 §3.4, retained, now with the two forms distinguished).
2. **§3 permission** — whether the step may receive a §3 component outcome: permitted (PASS, or UNEVALUABLE-EXCEPTION with flag) or not permitted (FAIL → defect outcome instead of §3; UNEVALUABLE-PENDING → no §3 outcome).
3. **Winning tree outcome** — the step's single I0 outcome (REV-07 §4 single-counting, retained): defect outcome (FAIL), §3-derived outcome (PASS or UNEVALUABLE-EXCEPTION), FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN or independently determined outcome (UNEVALUABLE-PENDING, per §2.4), or an earlier-exit outcome (per §4 tree order, where applicable).
4. **Auxiliary flag** — the flag recording the unevaluability, where applicable: `GATE-UNEVALUABLE-<check>` (exception form, §2.3) or `DATA-PENDING-<attribution>` (pending form, §5.2). A flag is an attribute (zero additional I0 summands, per single-counting), never a second outcome, never a waste bucket by itself.

A report that states only "gate unevaluable" without stating all four objects (or, where an object is not applicable, stating why) has not defined the step's status under this revision and must be corrected before its counts are evaluated.

### 2.2 Gate FAIL (evaluated contradiction) — unchanged, restated for completeness

If G evaluates and fires (contradiction demonstrated on present data — e.g., KX2a True + K = 0; KX2d-open est False + open_len NONE-GENUINE + K = 1; KX2f AVAIL/VAL contradiction), the step outcome is the defect outcome per defect precedence D1–D4 (INVALID-K, INVALID-FEAT-RECORD, or INVALID-EST-STATE via EST-PROV where applicable, retained). No §3 component outcome is assigned for that step's K attribution (defect routing precedes §3, REV-08 §2 / REV-09 §2, retained). Waste follows the defect outcome's bucket (unassigned for defect/status-line outcomes, per REV-05 §6 / REV-07 §4, retained). No flag is needed for a FAIL gate (the defect outcome itself records the contradiction); a FAIL gate must never be relabelled UNEVALUABLE to soften it.

### 2.3 GATE-UNEVALUABLE as an exception-flagged classification (governing; same type as E-TUPLE, different check)

**Definition (governing).** `GATE-UNEVALUABLE-<check>` is an **exception-flagged classification**, formally the same **type** as E-TUPLE (REV-09 §3.2, retained) and formally **distinct** from DATA-PENDING (§5.2). The type is defined by three properties, all required:

- (i) **Outcome assigned:** the step receives a §3 component outcome (and, via §4.1 precedence where applicable, a step-level label) on the basis of classification inputs that are present and sufficient for the applicable §3 comparison (recorded values for comparison, K flag recorded, and, for the computable branch, required partial provenance present). The gate's inputs are **additional** provenance/availability fields whose omission does not prevent the comparison itself (e.g., AVAIL flag omitted while VAL present and K flag present; tuple omitted while True flag present — the E-TUPLE analogue).
- (ii) **Check not run:** the named gate check was not evaluated (its required provenance/availability inputs are omitted or Unknown), so its result is UNEVALUABLE. The flag records the check name and the omitted input(s). The flag must never be dropped in summaries of the outcome, and the outcome must never be cited as evidence that the gate passed, that no gate contradiction exists, or that the K record is gate-verified.
- (iii) **Claim scope limited (§2.5):** the outcome supports descriptive counting and descriptive waste/frequency totals, but **not** accounting-sufficient assigned-pattern numerators or waste sums (see §2.5). This limitation is part of the exception type, not an optional annotation.

**Relationship to E-TUPLE (governing).** E-TUPLE (`EST-PROV-(a)-UNEVALUABLE` under True, REV-09 §3.2, retained) and GATE-UNEVALUABLE are **instances of the same exception type** (outcome assigned + contradiction check unevaluable + flag carried + UNEVALUABLE ≠ PASS/FAIL), differing only in **which check is unevaluated** (EST-PROV history check vs K-gate check) and therefore in **which claim is limited**: E-TUPLE limits history-correctness claims (cannot claim history contradiction absent, cannot claim full-estimate state verified against history); GATE-UNEVALUABLE limits gate-verification claims (cannot claim gate contradiction absent, cannot claim K record gate-verified). Both instances share the §2.5 accounting-sufficient exclusion (see below): an exception-flagged outcome, whether E-TUPLE or GATE-UNEVALUABLE, is excluded from accounting-sufficient assigned-pattern numerators and waste sums, with flagged steps retained in denominators. This shared exclusion is the formal relationship the forensic review asked to be stated; it does not make the two flags interchangeable (a report must name the check that is unevaluated, not a generic "exception flag").

**When the exception does NOT apply (governing).** The exception applies **only** when (i) holds (classification inputs present and sufficient). If classification inputs are insufficient, the case is UNEVALUABLE-PENDING (§2.4), not an exception, even if the gate's inputs are also omitted. In particular, a missing K flag (§2.7) is never an exception case (K flag is itself a classification input); a missing recorded value for the governing comparison is never an exception case; missing partial provenance where the matrix requires it for the computable branch is never an exception case for that branch (the incomputability branch applies instead, per REV-08 §3, retained). A report that labels a comparison-input-missing case as GATE-UNEVALUABLE (exception) instead of DATA-PENDING misclassifies the step and must be corrected to §2.4.

### 2.4 UNEVALUABLE-PENDING (governing; distinct from the exception)

**Definition (governing).** `UNEVALUABLE-PENDING` (flag form: `DATA-PENDING-<attribution>`, §5.2) applies when a K-gate check cannot be evaluated **and** the step cannot receive a defensible §3 outcome without it — either because a classification input is missing (Category (M) in REV-09 §3.4, retained as redefined here), or because the defect determination itself is outcome-determining and cannot be determined without the gate (e.g., K flag present but its consistency with availability/slots cannot be determined and no §3 comparison can be performed defensibly without that determination — the evaluator must state which condition holds; "gate unevaluable" alone is insufficient). Consequences (retained from REV-09 §3.5, renamed per §5):

- No §3 component outcome is assigned for the pending attribution (the attribution has no value).
- Winning tree outcome is determined independently (FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN where a missing field is the cause, otherwise the independently determined outcome per §4 tree order — see §4.3 for the missing-phase interaction, where "independently determined" means determined by an earlier exit).
- Pending flag is a secondary attribute (zero additional I0 summands).
- The pending attribution supports **no** frequency, waste, pattern, or accounting claim of any kind (descriptive or accounting-sufficient) — there is no outcome to claim with. This is **stronger** than the exception's limitation (§2.5): exception-flagged outcomes support descriptive totals; pending attributions support no totals for that attribution at all (the step contributes to descriptive totals only via its independently determined tree outcome, e.g., FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN counts, not via any §3 pattern).

### 2.5 Claim scope — descriptive vs accounting-sufficient, P3/P4 alignment (governing)

**Two totals, always distinguished (governing).** Every frequency and waste total in a future report must be stated as either:

- **Descriptive total:** includes all steps with the relevant outcome, flagged or unflagged, with flagged sub-counts shown separately. Descriptive totals answer "what was recorded?" They never, by themselves, support an accounting-sufficient claim.
- **Accounting-sufficient total:** includes **only unflagged** steps with the relevant outcome (no E-TUPLE flag, no GATE-UNEVALUABLE flag, no DATA-PENDING flag for that attribution). Flagged steps are retained in **denominators** (n_pre, n_pre-all, episode denominators as applicable) but contribute **zero** to accounting-sufficient numerators (assigned-pattern pre-step counts, trigger counts where the trigger attribution is flagged, and assigned waste sums SEL/HEAD/K3-FULL). Accounting-sufficient totals answer "what is established well enough to count toward a threshold?" A threshold (T_pre/Y_pre/T_trig, Rule C/T/CT, when prescribed — decisions 6, retained as open) evaluated on descriptive totals that include flagged outcomes is invalid for an accounting-sufficient claim; it must be re-evaluated on accounting-sufficient totals, or the claim must be marked INCONCLUSIVE on flagged-evidence grounds.

**Waste treatment, stated precisely (governing; supersedes REV-09 §7 row T14's unqualified "waste follows outcome bucket").** For an exception-flagged step (GATE-UNEVALUABLE or E-TUPLE) with a §3-derived or pattern outcome: its waste is **reportable** in descriptive waste totals under its outcome bucket (with flagged sub-total shown separately), and **not eligible** for accounting-sufficient assigned waste sums (it is excluded from `waste_SEL`, `waste_HEAD`, `waste_K3-FULL` accounting-sufficient sums, even though its descriptive bucket is the same label). For a pending step (no §3 outcome): its waste follows its independently determined tree outcome's bucket descriptively (e.g., FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN waste unassigned per bridge, retained), and contributes zero to any assigned-pattern waste sum (it has no assigned-pattern outcome). In neither case is waste double-counted (one step, one descriptive bucket, plus at most one flagged sub-total marker within that bucket) and in neither case is flagged waste silently included in an accounting-sufficient total.

**Frequency treatment (governing).** Contributor frequency (pre steps with pattern P / n_pre) and trigger frequency (episodes with at-class P / non-success episodes in scope) have descriptive and accounting-sufficient forms on the same principle: descriptive numerators include flagged pattern steps/episodes (flagged sub-count shown); accounting-sufficient numerators include only unflagged pattern steps/episodes. Denominators (n_pre, non-success episodes in scope) include flagged steps/episodes in both forms (flagged steps are counted steps/episodes, not missing steps/episodes). A frequency that meets a threshold descriptively but not on accounting-sufficient totals does **not** meet the threshold for an accounting-sufficient claim.

**P3/P4 alignment (governing).** Flagged or pending steps do **not** by themselves exclude an episode from P3-final (P3 eligibility is determined by Stage-A/B/C, REV-07 §2 / REV-09 §4 / REV-10 §3, retained) and do **not** by themselves block P4 (P4 gate is zero Stage-A FAIL exclusions, zero Stage-A UNRESOLVED exclusions, zero P3-INVALID, plus prescribed frequency/threshold conditions when set — decisions 4, 6, retained). What flagged/pending status **does** affect is the **claim** that can be made about a P3-final episode or a P4-passing job: (i) an episode's waste accounting result must show flagged waste separately and must not present flagged waste as accounting-sufficient assigned waste; (ii) a job-level accounting-sufficient claim (per-episode-all rule, decision 4, retained) must be evaluated on accounting-sufficient totals (unflagged only) — if the claim meets its threshold only by including flagged outcomes, the claim is INCONCLUSIVE (flagged-evidence grounds), not met, even if P4's Stage-A conditions are satisfied. P4 passing (Stage-A conditions) and accounting-sufficient claim meeting (thresholds on unflagged evidence) are therefore **separate** conditions, both required for a job-level accounting-sufficient claim; neither implies the other. A report must state both separately and must not present P4 passing alone as an accounting-sufficient claim.

**REV-09 §3.5 P4 sentence, clarified (governing).** REV-09 §3.5's statement that pending does not by itself block P4 is retained **with the §2.5 qualification**: pending does not block P4's Stage-A conditions, but pending (and exception-flagged) attributions cannot contribute to the threshold evidence for an accounting-sufficient claim. The sentence must henceforth be cited with that qualification; citing it without the qualification to imply that pending evidence can support a threshold claim is invalid.

### 2.6 Table C instructions for flags (governing)

Table C (REV-07 §4.4 / REV-08 §4.4, retained in form) gains the following flag instructions, which are part of the governing reporting standard (not optional annotations):

- Every outcome count row (I0/I1) and every waste bucket row (I3) that includes exception-flagged steps must show a flagged sub-count column (`n_flagged` for counts, `waste_flagged` for waste) alongside the total, with the flag type(s) named (E-TUPLE, GATE-UNEVALUABLE-<check>). The accounting-sufficient subtotal (`total − flagged`, for assigned-pattern rows only) must be shown alongside the descriptive total where an accounting-sufficient claim is contemplated; where no such claim is contemplated, the flagged sub-count must still be shown, so a future claim cannot mistake the descriptive total for an accounting-sufficient total.
- DATA-PENDING steps appear in Table C only under their independently determined tree outcome (e.g., FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN row), with a pending-flag sub-count column on that row (naming the pending attribution). No separate "pending" outcome row exists in Table C (per §5.2, DATA-PENDING is not an outcome).
- APPROVAL-HOLD steps (§5.3) appear in Table C only under HOLD-APPROVAL (governing holding outcome, §5.3) or under their approved-rules outcome where one exists (§5.3), with candidate outcomes in a **separate candidate table** (never in Table C governing rows). Candidate-table totals must never be added to Table C totals.

### 2.7 Missing K flag — explicit governing route (governing; resolves FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN vs K-STATUS-UNKNOWN)

**Route (governing; exhaustive for R-K-FLAG).** R-K-FLAG has three relevant states for routing (recorded 1, recorded 0, NOT-RECORDED). A fourth state, "recorded but provenance unknown", is not a flag value — it is a provenance condition on a recorded flag (see below). The route is:

- **R-K-FLAG = 1 (recorded):** step is a K_rec = 1 candidate for §3 (subject to gates, defects, T0, and tree order, retained). If a gate input is omitted (e.g., AVAIL omitted while VAL present), §2.3/§2.4 apply (exception if classification inputs sufficient, pending if not). If VAL is also missing, §2.4 pending applies (comparison input missing), not the exception.
- **R-K-FLAG = 0 (recorded):** step is a K_rec = 0 candidate for availability-state routing (§2.2 of REV-08, retained, as qualified by REV-09 §2.2 KX2a exception, retained). Availability-state outcome is determined by estimate/tuple status: KBLANK / KNEVER / KUNK / phase-only sub-states as applicable, or **K-STATUS-UNKNOWN** when the flag is recorded 0 but its timing/status provenance (estimate tuple / first-estimates timing) is unknown so that blank/never/unk cannot be distinguished. K-STATUS-UNKNOWN is therefore an **availability-state outcome for a recorded-0 flag with unknown provenance** — it presupposes a recorded flag value (0) and asserts that the status timing is unknown. It is counted in I0 as an availability-state outcome (one summand), with no P- attribute for either component (REV-08 §2, retained).
- **R-K-FLAG = NOT-RECORDED (field absent):** step **cannot** be routed as K_rec = 1 or K_rec = 0, because there is no recorded flag value to route on. Tree outcome is **FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN** (missing-field bridge, D1, retained), subject to §4 exit order in the analogous sense (E0/E1 earlier exits win over a missing-field outcome — see §4, where E2 is stated for recorded phase; the flag-field case follows the same earlier-exit principle). The step is **not** K-STATUS-UNKNOWN (which requires a recorded 0 flag), **not** an availability-state outcome, and **not** a §3 candidate. Pending flag: `DATA-PENDING-K-status` (attribution "K status", cause "R-K-FLAG NOT-RECORDED", tree outcome FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN or earlier-exit outcome per §4). I0: one summand to FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN (or to the earlier-exit outcome, per §4), zero summands to any availability-state count, zero summands to any K-class count.

**Never equated (governing).** FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN (flag field absent) and K-STATUS-UNKNOWN (flag recorded 0, provenance unknown) are different outcomes for different records, counted in different I0 buckets, with different downstream consequences (FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN: denominator in P2, unassigned waste in P3 per bridge; K-STATUS-UNKNOWN: availability-state waste unassigned per REV-03 §3 / REV-08 §2, retained, with no P- attribute in either case — the shared "no P- attribute" consequence does **not** make them the same outcome). A report that counts a NOT-RECORDED-flag step as K-STATUS-UNKNOWN, or a recorded-0-unknown-provenance step as FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN, misroutes the step and must be corrected to §2.7. A step is never both: if the flag is NOT-RECORDED, K-STATUS-UNKNOWN does not apply; if the flag is recorded 0, FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN does not apply on K-flag grounds (another missing field could still give FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN via D1 precedence — see §4.3, where the winning outcome is single and the other condition is secondary/pending as applicable).

## 3. A4-C comparison domain — who is compared, who is skipped, and what PASS means (R10-02)

REV-09 §4.2's A4-C definition is amended by this section. REV-09 §4.2's aggregation (FAIL-if-any-inconsistency, else UNRESOLVED-if-any-missing, else PASS) is retained **only within the A4-C domain defined here**; outside that domain, the aggregation does not apply, because no comparison is performed.

### 3.1 Two domains, kept distinct (governing)

For an episode, define:

- **A4-P domain:** all steps in the episode that are candidates for T0 comparison in the REV-09 §4.2 scope sense — i.e., steps not already excluded by step-type exclusion (CRUSH-ND, TERMINAL, UNREACH, REV-02 §2 / REV-05 §5, retained) before T0. A4-P is evaluated over every A4-P-domain step: does the step have all T0 fields present (T0 presence set per REV-09 §4.1, retained: excluding recorded phase, `door_open_pre`, and `position_after`, each handled by its own check)? If any A4-P-domain step lacks a T0 field, A4-P **fails** for the episode (FAIL tier contribution, per §3.3).
- **A4-C domain:** the subset of A4-P-domain steps that **pass A4-P** (T0 fields present for that step). Only A4-C-domain steps are eligible for phase comparison. A step that fails A4-P is **not** in the A4-C domain, for any purpose: its phase is not compared, its phase condition does not contribute to A4-C status, and it does not contribute an A4-C reason (see §3.3 for what is recorded instead).

**Why the split is necessary (governing rationale, stated so the domain cannot be collapsed silently).** Phase comparison requires both a recorded phase value **and** a comparison target derived from `t_pre` (true phase under the stable 20/10 schedule, REV-01 §3, retained). If `t_pre` (or another T0 field required to place the step and derive its target) is missing, the target cannot be derived, so consistency cannot be demonstrated and inconsistency cannot be demonstrated either. Comparing a present recorded phase against an absent target would be inventing the target; comparing it against a derived-from-elsewhere target would violate the no-substitution rule (REV-07 §2.1 / REV-08 §4.4, retained). The only defensible treatment is to skip the comparison for that step and record the skip (see §3.3), while the A4-P failure records the missing field as a FAIL reason. Skipping is not PASS, not UNRESOLVED, and not FAIL **on A4-C grounds** — it is a distinct per-step state, `NOT-EVALUATED-SKIPPED (A4-P failed)`, which contributes to the episode's A4 status only via A4-P (FAIL), never via A4-C.

### 3.2 Four sub-cases, each defined (governing)

For a single candidate step (A4-P-domain step), exactly one of the following holds. The table states, for each, its A4-P contribution, its A4-C treatment, and its ledger treatment. "T0 fields present" means all T0 fields for that step are present (per-step A4-P passes); "T0 field missing" means at least one T0 field for that step is missing (per-step A4-P fails).

| Sub-case | A4-P contribution (per step) | A4-C treatment (per step) | Ledger treatment (per step) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| (i) T0 field missing, recorded phase present | FAIL reason (field named, step ID). Episode A4-P fails (see §3.3). | **SKIPPED** — NOT-EVALUATED-SKIPPED (A4-P failed). Phase comparison not performed. Present phase is **not** evidence of consistency and **not** evidence of inconsistency. | A4-P FAIL reason recorded (step ID, field named). Descriptive note recorded: "recorded phase present, comparison not performed (T0 field missing: <field>)". Descriptive note is **not** an A4-C reason, **not** counted as an A4-C PASS/FAIL/UNRESOLVED reason, and **not** summed into any A4-C count. |
| (ii) T0 field missing, recorded phase also missing | FAIL reason (T0 field named, step ID). Episode A4-P fails. | **SKIPPED** — NOT-EVALUATED-SKIPPED (A4-P failed). Phase comparison not performed (both target and value absent for comparison purposes; value absence alone would have given A4-C UNRESOLVED if T0 fields were present, but they are not). | A4-P FAIL reason recorded (step ID, T0 field named). Descriptive note recorded: "recorded phase also missing; comparison not performed (T0 field missing)". Descriptive note is **not** an A4-C UNRESOLVED reason (see §3.3: missing-phase A4-C reasons arise only for A4-C-domain steps). Step-level tree outcome per §4 (E1: INVALID-RECORD, since T0-P fails — see §4.2), not FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN on phase grounds (see §4.3 for the winning-outcome rule). |
| (iii) T0 fields present, recorded phase missing | No A4-P reason (per-step A4-P passes). | **In A4-C domain.** A4-C condition: missing phase → contributes UNRESOLVED to A4-C aggregation (§3.4). | A4-C UNRESOLVED reason recorded (UNRESOLVED-INVALID-RECORD, step ID), subject to aggregation (§3.4) and episode two-tier rule (§3.4). Step-level tree outcome FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN **only** if no earlier exit won (E2, §4.2); if an earlier exit won, see §4.3 (missing phase as secondary/descriptive, not winning outcome, not A4-C-domain change — A4-C domain membership is by A4-P pass, not by tree outcome, so an E0 step is not in A4-C domain at all (type-excluded), while an E2 step is). |
| (iv) T0 fields present, recorded phase present | No A4-P reason. | **In A4-C domain.** A4-C condition: consistent with `t_pre`-derived true phase → PASS contribution; inconsistent → FAIL contribution (demonstrated inconsistency). | If inconsistent: A4-C FAIL reason recorded (step ID, demonstrated inconsistency). If consistent: no A4 reason recorded for that step. Step-level tree outcome per §4 (T0-C PASS → subsequent routing; T0-C inconsistency → INVALID-RECORD). |

**Multi-condition episodes (governing).** An episode's A4-P status, A4-C status, and A4 status are determined from the per-step table above by §3.4 (aggregation) and §3.3 (A4 status composition), not by applying REV-09 §4.2's aggregation to all candidate steps indiscriminately. In particular, skipped steps (i)/(ii) never vote in A4-C aggregation (they have no A4-C condition), and their phase conditions never convert an A4-C PASS into FAIL/UNRESOLVED or vice versa — they affect the episode only via A4-P (FAIL) and via descriptive notes.

### 3.3 A4 status composition — A4-P, A4-C, and episode final status kept distinct (governing)

- **A4-P status (episode):** FAIL if any A4-P-domain step lacks a T0 field; else PASS (every A4-P-domain step has T0 fields present). A4-P has no UNRESOLVED status (presence is decidable from the record: a field is present or it is not; "field presence unknown" is itself a missing-field case → FAIL, not UNRESOLVED, per REV-07 §2.1 presence rule, retained).
- **A4-C status (episode, domain-restricted):** computed **only** over A4-C-domain steps (A4-P-passing steps) by REV-09 §4.2 aggregation, retained **within the domain**: FAIL if any domain step demonstrates inconsistency; else UNRESOLVED if any domain step has a missing recorded phase; else PASS **provided the domain is non-empty** (see empty-domain rule below). Skipped steps do not affect A4-C status in any direction.
- **A4 status (episode, the check status passed to the two-tier rule):**
  - If A4-P = FAIL → **A4 = FAIL** (code INVALID-RECORD, unless an earlier check's FAIL code takes precedence as final code per two-tier rule — see below). A4-C status in this case is recorded as **NOT-EVALUATED-SKIPPED for the skipped steps** and, for any A4-C-domain steps that exist alongside A4-P-failing steps, as computed over those domain steps — but the **check** status is FAIL regardless of the domain computation, because A4-P failure dominates within A4 (presence failure is a FAIL, not an UNRESOLVED, and FAIL dominates UNRESOLVED within the check as across checks). Ledger records both: A4-P FAIL reason(s) **and** A4-C domain reasons (if any domain steps have FAIL/UNRESOLVED conditions), with A4-C domain reasons marked **underlying** (not final) when A4 = FAIL. A4-C domain reasons are never dropped because A4-P failed; they are retained as underlying reasons per REV-09 §4.2 ledger rule, retained and extended here to domain reasons alongside A4-P reasons.
  - Else (A4-P = PASS) → **A4 = A4-C status** (FAIL / UNRESOLVED / PASS, per domain aggregation, with empty-domain rule below).
- **Empty-domain rule (governing; prevents PASS without comparison).**
  - If A4-C domain is **empty** and A4-P did **not** fail: this occurs when the episode has no A4-P-domain steps at all (e.g., all steps type-excluded, or the episode has no candidate steps in scope) — A4-P vacuously passes (no step to fail it), but no phase comparison was performed for any step. → **A4 = UNRESOLVED** with new code **UNRESOLVED-A4C-NO-ELIGIBLE-STEPS**. Rationale: eligibility for P3-final requires established conformance; with zero comparisons performed, conformance is not established, so PASS is impermissible and FAIL would overclaim a demonstrated violation. The code is added to the UNRESOLVED code set for I5 (see §3.5); it is counted in I5 as an UNRESOLVED term (final status), never as a FAIL term.
  - If A4-C domain is **empty because A4-P failed for every step** (no step passes A4-P): A4 = FAIL (A4-P dominates, above); A4-C is recorded as NOT-EVALUATED-SKIPPED (all steps skipped), **not** as UNRESOLVED-A4C-NO-ELIGIBLE-STEPS (the empty-domain UNRESOLVED code applies only when A4-P did not fail; otherwise the FAIL already excludes the episode, and adding an UNRESOLVED code would double-count the episode's exclusion). Ledger records A4-P FAIL reason(s) and the skipped-step notes; it does not record an A4-C UNRESOLVED reason for skipped steps.
- **Episode final status (two-tier, retained, with A4 composition above).** Final status is determined from all six check statuses (A1–A6, with A4 as composed above) by REV-07 §2.3 two-tier rule, retained: any check FAIL → final FAIL (first failing check in A1→A6 order as final code); else any check UNRESOLVED → final UNRESOLVED (first unevaluable check in A1→A6 order as final code); else PASS. A4-C domain reasons and A4-P reasons that are not the final code are retained as underlying reasons in the ledger (REV-09 §4.2, retained). A4-P FAIL reasons, A4-C reasons, descriptive skipped-step notes, and final codes are four distinct ledger elements and must not be merged into a single "A4 reason" count.

### 3.4 A4-C aggregation, restated for the domain (governing; REV-09 §4.2 retained within domain)

Within the A4-C domain (non-empty, A4-P passed for domain steps):

- A4-C = FAIL if any domain step demonstrates phase inconsistency.
- Else A4-C = UNRESOLVED if any domain step has a missing recorded phase.
- Else A4-C = PASS (every domain step has recorded phase present and consistent with `t_pre`).

**PASS meaning, corrected (governing).** A4-C = PASS now means, exactly: "the A4-C domain is non-empty, and every step in it has a recorded phase present and consistent with the phase derived from `t_pre`." It does **not** mean "every step in the episode is consistent" (skipped steps and type-excluded steps are not in the domain and were not compared), and it does **not** mean "no step has a missing T0 field" (that is A4-P's claim, and A4-C = PASS in an episode with A4-P = FAIL is impossible as a check status, because A4 = FAIL in that case — see §3.3). A report that cites A4-C = PASS as evidence about skipped or type-excluded steps misrepresents the domain and must be corrected to the domain-restricted meaning.

### 3.5 I5 code set update and count definitions (governing)

- The UNRESOLVED code set for I5 (REV-07 §2.4 / REV-08 §5 / REV-09 §5, retained in form) gains **UNRESOLVED-A4C-NO-ELIGIBLE-STEPS** as a seventh UNRESOLVED code (for the empty-domain case in §3.3). I5's UNRESOLVED sum includes this code as a final-status term on the same basis as the other six UNRESOLVED codes (final status UNRESOLVED with that code). The code must never be used for skipped steps in an A4-P-failing episode (those episodes are FAIL, §3.3), and never for type-excluded steps alone (type exclusion is recorded by its own reason, not by this code, unless the episode has no A4-P-domain steps at all and A4-P did not fail — the §3.3 empty-domain case).
- Count definitions in REV-09 §5 are updated by §§3–4 of this revision as follows (see §4.3 for the full restatement): (a) counts winning FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN steps due to missing recorded phase only (E2 steps, §4.2); (b) counts episodes containing at least one such winning step (E2 step, hence A4-C-domain missing-phase step); (c) counts episodes whose final status is UNRESOLVED-INVALID-RECORD (unchanged in meaning, now explicitly requiring A4-C-domain missing phase + no FAIL + no earlier UNRESOLVED code); (d) counts FAIL-final episodes retaining UNRESOLVED-INVALID-RECORD as an underlying reason (now explicitly including A4-C-domain missing-phase reasons retained underlying when A4 = FAIL via A4-P or via A4-C inconsistency on another step, per §3.3); (e) counts episodes recording UNRESOLVED-INVALID-RECORD in any ledger position (final or underlying, A4-C-domain reasons only — skipped-step descriptive notes do **not** count toward (e), because they are not A4-C reasons). The identity (e) = (b) when ledger-complete (REV-09 §5.1, retained) now holds **for A4-C-domain reasons**: every episode in (b) records an A4-C reason (final or underlying), and no episode outside (b) records one (skipped-step notes are not reasons for this identity). Episodes with only secondary/descriptive missing-phase steps (E0/E1, §4) are outside (b) and outside (e) for A4-C-reason purposes, and are counted descriptively only (§4.3).

## 4. Missing-phase precedence against other tree outcomes — exit order governs (R10-03)

### 4.1 Governing tree exit order for missing-phase interaction (governing)

REV-09 §3.5(b)'s phrase "when another tree rule independently determines the outcome, that outcome stands" is clarified by the following exit order. "Earlier" means earlier in this order; an earlier exit's outcome **wins** over a later exit's outcome for the same step, and the later condition (if present) is recorded as a secondary attribute (where determinable on present fields) or as a DATA-PENDING flag (where undeterminable), never as a second winning outcome (single-counting, REV-07 §4, retained).

- **E0 — Step-type exclusion (earliest).** If the step is CRUSH-ND, TERMINAL, or UNREACH (REV-02 §2 / REV-05 §5, retained), the step outcome is the exclusion outcome. Missing recorded phase (if present on such a step) does **not** change the outcome, does **not** make the step FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN, and does **not** contribute to `n_steps_missing-phase` (a) or to A4-C-domain counts (type-excluded steps are not in A4-P/A4-C domains, §3.1). Missing phase on an E0 step is recorded descriptively in the step record only ("missing phase, step type-excluded") and must not be summed into any FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN count.
- **E1 — T0-P failure.** If any T0 identity/ordering field for the step is missing (T0-P fails, REV-09 §4.1, retained), the step outcome is **INVALID-RECORD**, regardless of recorded-phase presence/absence. Missing recorded phase (if also present) does **not** change the outcome to FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN, does **not** contribute to (a), and does **not** contribute an A4-C reason (the step is skipped for A4-C, §3.2 sub-case (ii); its missing phase is a descriptive note only, per §3.2). This is the precedence stated in REV-09 §2.1 (T0-P dominates), now placed explicitly in exit order.
- **E2 — T0-C reached (the missing-phase rule's scope).** If E0/E1 do not apply (step not type-excluded, T0 fields present), the tree reaches T0-C. **Only here** does the missing-phase rule apply: if recorded phase is missing → tree outcome **FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN** (D1, retained — see §4.2 for its precedence over later defects). If recorded phase is present → T0-C outcome applies (PASS → subsequent routing E3; inconsistency → INVALID-RECORD, which is itself the T0-C outcome, not a later defect).
- **E3 — Subsequent routing (latest).** If T0-C passes (phase present and consistent), subsequent routing applies in retained order: OPT routing, availability-state assignment, defect precedence D1–D4 for overlapping defects, §3 comparison, pattern classification (REV-05 §4 / REV-06 §2.3, retained). Missing phase cannot arise at E3 (phase was present at T0-C by E2's condition), so no missing-phase precedence question arises at E3. OPT and availability outcomes at E3 therefore **never** win over a missing phase, because a missing-phase step never reaches E3 as a missing-phase step — it exits at E2 as FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN. REV-09 §3.5(b)'s examples of OPT/availability as "independently determined outcomes" are henceforth read as **E3 outcomes for steps whose phase was present** (or, for availability, for steps that reached availability routing with phase present), not as outcomes that override E2 missing phase. Any reading of §3.5(b) in which OPT/availability wins over E2 missing phase is superseded by this section.

### 4.2 Missing phase at E2 vs later defects (governing; D1 precedence within E2)

At E2 (T0-P passed, recorded phase missing), FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN is the winning tree outcome by defect precedence D1 (FIELD-MISSING > D2 EST-STATE > D3 INVALID-K > D4 INVALID-FEAT-RECORD, REV-06 §2.3, retained). Consequences:

- Later defects (D2/D3/D4) that are **determinable on present fields** for the same step are recorded as **secondary attributes** (zero additional I0 summands, per single-counting), not as winning outcomes and not as additional I0 summands. Example: a step with missing recorded phase (E2, FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN wins) and a determinable INVALID-K condition on a present field (e.g., K flag and availability fields present and contradictory) carries INVALID-K as a secondary attribute (flagged in the attribute cross-tabulation, REV-07 §4, retained), not as its tree outcome.
- Later defects that are **undeterminable** because the missing phase (or another missing field) prevents their evaluation are recorded as **DATA-PENDING flags** (attribution named, cause named, tree outcome FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN, per §5.2), not as defect outcomes and not as assumed-passed checks.
- FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN at E2 is therefore **not** overridden by any later defect, and no later defect is hidden by it (secondary attributes and pending flags preserve both, in their correct roles). A report that counts an E2 missing-phase step as INVALID-K (or any D2–D4 outcome) as its winning outcome violates D1 precedence and §4.1 exit order and must be corrected to FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN (winning) + defect as secondary/pending as applicable.

### 4.3 Redefined counts and F/Q instructions (governing)

- **`n_steps_missing-phase` (a) redefined (governing; supersedes REV-09 §5.1(a) as read without exit order).** (a) = number of steps whose **winning** tree outcome is FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN **and** whose missing field includes recorded phase (i.e., E2 steps, §4.1). Steps with missing recorded phase as a secondary/descriptive condition (E0 type-excluded steps, E1 T0-P-failure steps) are **not** in (a). A separate descriptive count is defined for audit completeness (never used in I0/I5 reconciliation): `n_steps_with-missing-phase-secondary` = number of steps with recorded phase missing whose winning outcome is **not** FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN (E0/E1 winners). This descriptive count must be reported separately from (a) where missing-phase steps exist, so that (a) + secondary count = total steps with missing recorded phase (a checkable identity for the step population, not an I0 identity). (a) + secondary count must never be presented as (a) itself.
- **`n_episodes_with-missing-phase` (b) redefined (governing; supersedes REV-09 §5.1(b) as read without exit order).** (b) = number of episodes containing at least one (a) step (winning FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN due to missing recorded phase, hence A4-C-domain missing-phase step). Episodes containing **only** secondary missing-phase steps (E0/E1) are **not** in (b). A separate descriptive count, `n_episodes_with-any-missing-phase` (episodes containing at least one step with recorded phase missing, winning or secondary), may be reported descriptively but must never be used in place of (b) in any I5 or ledger reconciliation, and must never be equated with (e) (see §3.5: (e) counts A4-C reasons, which arise only for (b) episodes when ledger-complete).
- **F/Q instructions (governing).** F/Q rows are winning outcomes only (REV-07 §4, retained). E2 missing-phase steps appear in F/Q under FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN row (F/Q column by Q presence for that step, per REV-06 §4 / REV-07 §4, retained). E0/E1 missing-phase steps appear in F/Q under their winning outcome's row (exclusion / INVALID-RECORD row, respectively), **not** under FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN row, and their missing-phase condition appears nowhere in F/Q (it is not a winning outcome and not a Q-availability condition). Secondary defect attributes and DATA-PENDING flags for E2 steps appear in the attribute cross-tabulation (REV-07 §4, retained), never as F/Q rows/columns. APPROVAL-HOLD steps (§5.3) appear in F/Q only under HOLD-APPROVAL row where HOLD-APPROVAL is the governing outcome (see §5.3); candidate outcomes never appear in F/Q.
- **I0/I1 instructions (governing).** I0 counts winning outcomes only (one summand per counted step, REV-07 §4, retained). E2 steps: one summand to FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN. E0 steps: one summand to the exclusion outcome. E1 steps: one summand to INVALID-RECORD. I1 status-line breakdown for FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN must show (a) as a sub-count (winning missing-phase steps) and, separately, the secondary descriptive count (where present), so that I1 can be checked against §3.5/§4.3 without conflating winning and secondary conditions.

## 5. Data-level pending vs approval-level hold — two statuses, distinct names and consequences (R10-04)

REV-09 §3.5's single term "unclassified-pending" is replaced by two distinct statuses. The replacement is not a renaming for its own sake: the two statuses have different **sources** (missing evidence vs undecided authority), different **evaluability** (check cannot be run vs check can be run under a candidate rule), and different **counting** (auxiliary flag vs governing holding outcome with candidate table). Using one term for both, as REV-09 §3.5 and decisions 22–23 did, allowed a known contradiction to be misread as missing data. That misreading is now impossible by construction: the flag name itself states the source.

### 5.1 Definitions (governing)

- **DATA-PENDING (data-level unevaluability).** Source: required evidence is missing, or the check genuinely cannot be evaluated on the record (e.g., comparison input missing, gate input missing where §2.4 applies, provenance field NOT-RECORDED where the check needs it). The check's result is **unknown because the data to run it are absent**. Flag form: `DATA-PENDING-<attribution>` (attribution named, cause field named, tree outcome named — REV-09 §3.5 flag requirements, retained). Counting: auxiliary attribute only (REV-09 §3.5 treatment, retained and renamed): step retains exactly one independently determined tree outcome (FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN where a missing field is the cause, otherwise the earlier-exit outcome per §4), flag adds zero I0 summands, and the pending attribution supports no frequency/waste/pattern claim of any kind (§2.4). DATA-PENDING never asserts that the underlying condition is present, absent, passed, or failed — it asserts only that the data to determine it are missing.
- **APPROVAL-HOLD (approval-level hold).** Source: the underlying record **can** be evaluated under a candidate rule (the data are present and sufficient to run the candidate check), but the candidate rule's **authority for use is undecided** (owner approval pending, per decisions 18, 22, 23, and any future decision that states an interim hold — see §5.3). The check's result under the candidate rule may be known (including a demonstrated contradiction), but it is **not a governing result** until the rule is approved. Flag/table form: candidate outcome shown in a **separate candidate table only** (see §5.3), with the candidate rule named, the candidate result stated, and the governing outcome stated separately. APPROVAL-HOLD never asserts missing data, never asserts PASS/FAIL under governing rules, and never asserts that a demonstrated candidate contradiction is absent — the candidate contradiction, where present, is shown in the candidate table as a candidate result, explicitly labelled "candidate (rule not approved)".

**Prohibited conflation (governing).** A DATA-PENDING flag must never be used for an approval-level hold, and an APPROVAL-HOLD (or candidate-table entry) must never be used for a data-level unevaluability. In particular: (i) a step with VERIFIED data demonstrating an EST-PROV contradiction under the candidate EST-PROV rule is **not** DATA-PENDING (the data are present; the check can be run) — it is APPROVAL-HOLD with candidate outcome INVALID-EST-STATE (see §5.3, decision 22); (ii) a step with a missing gate input that prevents running the gate is **not** APPROVAL-HOLD (no candidate result can be computed without the input) — it is DATA-PENDING (or GATE-UNEVALUABLE exception where §2.3 applies, with classification inputs present); (iii) a step whose governing rule is approved but whose data are missing is DATA-PENDING, not APPROVAL-HOLD, even if the rule was recently approved (approval status and data status are independent dimensions, and the flag must name the dimension that actually holds).

### 5.2 DATA-PENDING counting and reporting (governing; REV-09 §3.5 retained, renamed)

DATA-PENDING treatment is REV-09 §3.5, retained in full under the new name, with the following clarifications (which do not change the treatment, only its labelling):

- Flag name: `DATA-PENDING-<attribution>` (e.g., `DATA-PENDING-K-defect-status`, `DATA-PENDING-§3-phase-outcome`). The generic label "unclassified-pending" without a DATA- prefix and without a named attribution is superseded and must not be used in governing reports (it appears in REV-09 §3.5 and decisions 22–23 as superseded wording — see §9 audit).
- Counting: auxiliary attribute, zero additional I0 summands, tree outcome independently determined per §4 exit order (FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN where a missing field is the cause at E2, otherwise the earlier-exit outcome). DATA-PENDING steps contribute to I0, P2 denominators, and descriptive totals only via their tree outcome, never via a pending attribution total.
- Claims: a DATA-PENDING attribution supports no frequency, waste, pattern, or accounting claim (descriptive or accounting-sufficient) for that attribution (§2.4). P3/P4 consequences per §2.5 (pending does not by itself exclude from P3-final or block P4's Stage-A conditions, but cannot contribute threshold evidence).
- Distinction from GATE-UNEVALUABLE: DATA-PENDING means **no outcome for that attribution**; GATE-UNEVALUABLE means **outcome assigned with gate flag** (§2.3). A step can carry both flags for **different** attributions (e.g., DATA-PENDING for a §3 component whose comparison input is missing, GATE-UNEVALUABLE for a different gate check whose inputs are omitted but whose comparison inputs are present) — in that case, each flag's consequences apply to its own attribution only, and the step's tree outcome is determined by §4 exit order / D1–D4 precedence as applicable, with both flags recorded as attributes.

### 5.3 APPROVAL-HOLD counting, candidate table, and interim rules (governing)

**Governing outcome rule (governing).** For a step whose classification **depends** on a candidate (unapproved) rule — i.e., approved rules alone do not determine a governing outcome for the relevant attribution — the governing tree outcome is the dedicated holding outcome **HOLD-APPROVAL**. HOLD-APPROVAL is a **formal governing I0 outcome** (one summand per such step, counted in I0 under HOLD-APPROVAL row, with F/Q column by Q presence for that step per §4.3, and waste unassigned/descriptive only, never assigned-pattern waste). HOLD-APPROVAL explicitly means: "classification withheld because the governing rule is not approved; no governing PASS, FAIL, contradiction, or pattern outcome is asserted for the held attribution." It is **not** a data-missing outcome (data may be complete), **not** a defect outcome (no governing defect is asserted), and **not** a pattern outcome (no governing pattern is asserted). Its existence as a formal outcome is intentional and limited to approval holds: it preserves exactly-one-governing-summand (every counted step has exactly one governing I0 outcome, including held steps) without forcing a governing outcome under an unapproved rule and without hiding held steps from denominators (held steps are counted steps, in denominators, with zero contribution to governing pattern/defect numerators).

**Approved-rules-determined case (governing).** If approved rules alone **do** determine a governing outcome for the step (e.g., T0-P failure → INVALID-RECORD under approved rules, regardless of what an unapproved rule would also say), that approved outcome **governs** and APPROVAL-HOLD does **not** apply to the step's governing outcome. The candidate rule's result (if any) for that step is recorded as a **candidate secondary note** only (in the candidate table, labelled candidate, never as a governing attribute that changes counts, never as a second outcome). APPROVAL-HOLD therefore applies only where the governing outcome genuinely depends on the unapproved rule; it is not a blanket hold on any step that merely mentions an unapproved rule.

**Candidate table (governing; required format).** Every APPROVAL-HOLD step (and every candidate secondary note, where present) must appear in a **separate candidate table**, never in governing Table C rows (except HOLD-APPROVAL row itself, which is governing). The candidate table states, per step: step identifier, candidate rule named (e.g., "EST-PROV as proposed in REV-06 §2.1, approval pending per decision 22"), candidate inputs (present data, cited), candidate result (e.g., "candidate INVALID-EST-STATE (contradiction demonstrated under candidate rule)"), governing outcome (HOLD-APPROVAL, or approved-rules outcome where §5.3 approved-rules case applies), and the decision under which approval is pending (decision number). Candidate-table totals must **never** be added to governing I0/I1/F/Q/waste totals, and candidate results must **never** be cited as governing outcomes, governing defects, governing contradictions, or governing PASS/FAIL. A report that merges candidate totals into governing totals invalidates its governing counts and must be corrected by separating the tables.

**Interim rules rewritten (governing; supersedes REV-09 decisions 18, 22, 23 interim wording).**

- **Decision 22 interim (EST-PROV / True-before-first/NEVER, approval pending):** until decision 22 is approved, True-before-first/NEVER steps whose governing outcome depends on EST-PROV are **APPROVAL-HOLD** (governing outcome HOLD-APPROVAL; candidate outcome INVALID-EST-STATE shown in candidate table where the candidate rule demonstrates the contradiction on VERIFIED data; candidate outcome KF1/KF2 shown where the candidate rule would permit classification — each candidate result labelled candidate, never governing). They are **not** DATA-PENDING (data are present; check is evaluable under the candidate rule) and **not** governing INVALID-EST-STATE and **not** governing KF outcomes. The known candidate contradiction must be shown as a candidate contradiction, never hidden as "unevaluable data" and never upgraded to a governing contradiction.
- **Decision 23 interim (KX2d-open gate, approval pending):** until decision 23 is approved, span-Unknown + open-length-absent + K = 1 steps whose governing outcome depends on KX2d-open are **APPROVAL-HOLD** (governing outcome HOLD-APPROVAL; candidate outcome INVALID-K shown in candidate table where the candidate rule demonstrates it). They are **not** DATA-PENDING on gate grounds alone (gate inputs for the candidate rule are present in the stated case: span Unknown, open_len absent as NONE-GENUINE or as recorded value as applicable — the evaluator must state which; if a required input for the candidate rule is itself missing, the step is DATA-PENDING for that attribution **in addition to** or **instead of** APPROVAL-HOLD as the facts require, with both flags recorded where both hold for different attributions — see §5.2). They must not be classified via the retired narrow KX2d form or via §3 as governing outcomes while decision 23 is pending.
- **Decision 18 interim (gate checks generally, approval pending):** until decision 18 is approved, steps whose governing outcome depends on an unapproved gate check follow the same APPROVAL-HOLD rule (governing HOLD-APPROVAL or approved-rules outcome; candidate gate result in candidate table). Steps whose gate check is approved but whose gate **inputs** are missing follow DATA-PENDING / GATE-UNEVALUABLE rules (§2), not APPROVAL-HOLD — approval status and data status are independent (§5.1).

**What APPROVAL-HOLD does not do (governing).** APPROVAL-HOLD does not assert that the candidate rule is correct or incorrect, does not prejudge the owner's eventual approval/amendment/rejection, does not convert a candidate contradiction into a governing contradiction (or vice versa), and does not remove held steps from denominators (held steps are counted in I0 under HOLD-APPROVAL, in P2 denominators where their tree outcome so provides — HOLD-APPROVAL steps' pre-step status for P2 follows the same pre/at/post rules as other outcomes, with HOLD-APPROVAL treated as a non-pattern, non-defect holding outcome for denominator purposes and zero contribution to pattern numerators). A future approval of the candidate rule does **not** retroactively reclassify governing HOLD-APPROVAL counts without a further revision that states the reclassification rule explicitly (approval of a rule and reclassification of prior governing counts are separate decisions; the candidate table is the record from which any future reclassification would be derived, not itself a reclassification).

## 10. Bounded conclusion — what this revision fixes, what remains unresolved, and what is still not validated

**Answer: REVISION-10 corrects the five identified formal contradictions and counting ambiguities and is ready for owner approval as a specification — with the remaining limits stated below, which are limits of authorisation, owner approval, code verification, and empirical validation, not hidden inconsistencies in the corrected rules as far as the edge cases enumerated in §§2–8 are concerned. This conclusion is about the specification's readiness, not about any diagnostic having succeeded. No diagnostic has succeeded, no mechanism has been established, no trajectory has been collected under this revision, and no identity in REV-05 §6 / REV-06 §§4–6 / REV-07 §§2–4 / REV-08 §§2–5 / REV-09 §§2–5 / REV-10 §§2–5 has been empirically validated.**

**Specification consistency — what is now established (formally, within the text, subject to owner approval):**
- Gate unevaluability is now four separate facts, not one label (§2): gate state (PASS / FAIL / UNEVALUABLE-EXCEPTION / UNEVALUABLE-PENDING), §3 permission, winning tree outcome, and flag are each stated, and the two unevaluable forms have opposite §3 consequences (exception: outcome assigned with flag; pending: no outcome for that attribution). GATE-UNEVALUABLE is formally the same exception type as E-TUPLE (outcome + check-not-run + flag + UNEVALUABLE ≠ PASS/FAIL), differing only in which check is unevaluated and therefore which claim is limited, with a shared accounting-sufficient exclusion for both. Waste and frequency now have two totals everywhere (descriptive including flagged, accounting-sufficient excluding flagged, flagged steps in denominators), so T14's unqualified "waste follows outcome bucket" can no longer be misread as accounting-sufficient eligibility. P3/P4 are aligned to the claim actually supported: flagged/pending steps do not by themselves exclude from P3-final or block P4's Stage-A conditions, but thresholds for accounting-sufficient claims must be met on unflagged evidence alone — P4 passing and threshold meeting are separate, both required.
- Missing K flag has one route, not two (§2.7): flag absent → FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN (not K-STATUS-UNKNOWN); flag recorded 0 with provenance unknown → K-STATUS-UNKNOWN (availability outcome, not FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN). The shared "no P- attribute" consequence no longer conflates them, because their I0 buckets, P2 treatment, and downstream consequences are stated separately and a step is never both.
- A4-C can no longer PASS without a comparison (§3): the comparison domain is exactly the A4-P-passing steps; A4-P-failing steps are skipped (NOT-EVALUATED-SKIPPED, descriptive note only, never an A4-C reason, never a vote in aggregation); A4-C PASS requires a non-empty domain with every domain step present and consistent; an empty domain with A4-P not failed gives the new UNRESOLVED-A4C-NO-ELIGIBLE-STEPS (not PASS, not FAIL); an empty domain because A4-P failed gives A4 FAIL (A4-P dominates, A4-C recorded as skipped, not as an additional UNRESOLVED code). All four single-step sub-cases (T0 missing + phase present; T0 missing + phase missing; phase missing + T0 present; phase present + inconsistent) and multi-condition episodes are defined, with A4-P reasons, A4-C reasons, descriptive notes, and final codes kept as four distinct ledger elements.
- Missing phase no longer overrides earlier exits (§4): exit order E0 (type exclusion) → E1 (T0-P failure) → E2 (T0-C reached, missing-phase rule applies) → E3 (subsequent routing) governs every combination. E0/E1 winners stand over missing phase (descriptive/secondary only, not in (a)/(b), no A4-C reason for type-excluded steps, no A4-C reason for skipped A4-P-failing steps). At E2, FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN (D1) wins over later defects (secondary attributes where determinable, DATA-PENDING flags where undeterminable) and over later OPT/availability routing (which are E3 outcomes for present-phase steps only). Counts (a)/(b) are redefined to winning steps/episodes only, with separate descriptive counts for secondary cases, so no report can silently count every missing-phase record as a FIELD-MISSING-PATTERN winner.
- Pending now names its source (§5): DATA-PENDING (evidence missing / check cannot be run; auxiliary flag, tree outcome independently determined, no claim for that attribution) vs APPROVAL-HOLD (record evaluable under a candidate rule, authority undecided; governing outcome HOLD-APPROVAL or approved-rules outcome, candidate result in a separate candidate table only, never in governing totals). Known candidate contradictions (decision 22 EST-PROV, decision 23 KX2d-open) are shown as candidate contradictions, neither hidden as missing data nor upgraded to governing contradictions, and approval alone does not retroactively rewrite governing counts without a further revision stating the reclassification rule.
- The prior-collection claim is now bounded (§6): this revision relabels rules, not prior outputs; DEV-LPAIR-001 remains existing, incomplete and unofficial evidence, unchanged by this text-only review, with its records not inspected. The overbroad "no prior collection exists" sentence is superseded verbatim, so no future reader can cite REV-10 for non-existence of the pilot.
