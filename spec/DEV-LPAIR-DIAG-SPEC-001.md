# DEV-LPAIR-DIAG-SPEC-001 — Specification-Only Diagnosis of L-SHARED / L-SVA Held-Out Failure

**Work order:** DEV-LPAIR-DIAG-SPEC-001
**Status: AUTHORISED FOR SPECIFICATION DRAFTING ONLY — FOR OWNER REVIEW. NOT EXECUTED.**
**What this document is:** A diagnostic specification. It defines what would count as evidence for four candidate failure categories, what the existing records can and cannot establish, and what minimum additional evidence would be required.
**What this document is NOT:** A diagnosis, a cause, a winner, a rerun, an implementation, a repository change, or a change to EXP1 / EXP1-COMB / TBM gates, bars, or completeness rules. No code was written, modified, or executed for this specification beyond reading existing records and code to interpret them. No trajectory was collected. No missing job was resumed. No record was altered.

**Direct answer to the work-order question (§8), stated first:**

> **Can the existing DEV-LPAIR-001 evidence distinguish discovery, knowledge, decision and execution failures, or is additional trajectory-level evidence necessary?**
>
> **Additional trajectory-level evidence is necessary.** The existing evidence establishes the *outcome* — zero held-out successes in every completed job, with every completed held-out episode running to the 200-tick cap — and it rules out one narrow execution sub-cause (crushing in a door cell) as the sole explanation for most held-out jobs. It does **not** distinguish discovery from knowledge from decision from other execution failures, and for the missing L-SVA W20 seed 2 job it establishes nothing at all. Any statement that names one of the four categories as *the* cause of the held-out zeros on the basis of the existing records alone would be an overclaim. §3–§6 show, category by category and measurement by measurement, exactly where the discrimination breaks down, and §7 states the minimum trajectory schema that would be required to discriminate it.

**Evidence labels used throughout, as required:**
- **OBSERVED** — directly present in a retained record or in code read for this specification.
- **INFERENCE** — supported by observed records/code, but not directly recorded (for example, a deduction from an aggregate total).
- **HYPOTHESIS** — a candidate explanation that remains to be tested.
- **PROPOSAL** — a specification element, threshold rule, or future schema element for owner consideration, not an established fact.
- **UNKNOWN** — a necessary record, definition, or provenance element is unavailable; no value is reconstructed from memory or assumption.

**Outcome vocabulary (§3C), used exactly as defined here:**
- **SUPPORTED** — the specified discriminating evidence is present and the stated exclusion criterion for the main alternatives is met.
- **NOT_SUPPORTED** — a stated counter-observable is present that excludes the candidate *as specified* (for example, crush count zero excludes "crush as sole cause" for that job). This is not proof that no variant of the candidate applies.
- **INCONCLUSIVE** — evidence exists but cannot distinguish the candidate from at least one plausible alternative.
- **UNKNOWN** — a necessary record, measurement definition, or provenance element is unavailable, so no outcome can be assigned.
- Failure to support a cause is **not** proof that the cause is absent. That distinction is the reason most cells in §4 are INCONCLUSIVE rather than NOT_SUPPORTED.

**Scope discipline carried into this specification:**
- Pilot status is **INCOMPLETE**, unofficial, and not an EXP1-COMB result. It must never be pooled with SWEEP / SPIKE / EXP1-COMB records, and it must never be presented as a winner.
- ACT-0, ACT-1, ACT-2 have **no predefined semantics** (owner decision, 2026-10-28; L design memo). An act label alone therefore never establishes that an act was "suitable" or "unsuitable". Suitability can only be judged from a trajectory outcome (reaching the goal, approaching and crossing the correct door at an open time, or failing at a named point), not from the label.
- No numerical threshold is imported from URCP or PROTOCOL_SINGULARITY, per the work order. Where the existing evidence provides no defensible threshold, this specification uses a qualitative rule or marks the threshold as requiring an owner decision (§3D). No retrospective threshold is applied to agreement rates, margins, head differences, or act coverage.
- Implementation evidence (what code *can* calculate) is distinguished from experimental evidence (what was *actually recorded* in the pilot). A field that `lpair.py` could have saved, but `run_pilot.py` discarded, is **missing evidence**, not available evidence.

## 1. Executive conclusion

### 1.1 What is established

**OBSERVED (from `records_dev_lpair_001.jsonl`, recomputed for this specification — see §2):**
- Completed held-out evaluations: L-SHARED **0/120** (6 jobs × 20 episodes); L-SVA **0/100** (5 jobs × 20 episodes). Matched held-out pairs (5 pairs, both constructions present): **0/100 vs 0/100**.
- Completed trained evaluations: L-SHARED **77/120** (6 jobs); L-SVA **99/100** (5 jobs). Matched trained pairs (5 pairs): **57/100 vs 99/100**, descriptive only.
- Every completed held-out evaluation job has `ticks_total = 4000` and `ticks_mean = 200.0`. Since each episode is capped at 200 ticks and none reached the goal, **INFERENCE:** every completed held-out episode ran to the cap. This is a deduction from the aggregate total (20 × 200 = 4000, with per-episode ticks ≤ 200), not a recorded per-episode "ended by cap" field — no such field exists in the records.
- Crush totals in completed held-out jobs: L-SHARED **0** in all 6 jobs; L-SVA **83** in total, all in one job (L-SVA W10 seed 1 held-out, 0/20, ticks_mean 200.0), and **0** in the other 4 completed SVA held-out jobs. Crush therefore cannot be the sole explanation for Shared held-out failure, nor for SVA held-out failure in 4 of 5 completed jobs. It remains a possible contributor in the single SVA W10 seed 1 job, but the records cannot show whether crushes were the first divergence, a repeated consequence of looping, or incidental — see §3, Execution.
- Training reached totals (descriptive, exploration-influenced, not a learned-policy measure): L-SHARED W10 1889/3000, W20 1321/3000; L-SVA W10 1980/3000, W20 seeds 0–1 880/2000. Training episodes used ε exploration (1.0 → ~0.05095 at episode 999), so a training reach does not establish that the *greedy* policy discovered or retained the same behaviour.

### 1.2 What is not established — and why the four categories cannot be separated yet

For every completed held-out job, the retained records answer "did it reach the goal?" (no) and "how many ticks and crushes in total?" (4000 ticks; crushes as above) and "which acts, in total, were used, and what would each head have chosen on the visited states, in aggregate?" (act_steps, per-head counts, pairwise agreement, and for SVA, selector-vs-head-value agreement). They do **not** answer, for any step: where the agent was, whether it approached the held-out door row, whether the door was open, whether the ScheduleModel knew the schedule at that step, which act the selector actually chose, which action the chosen head actually took, what happened next, or where the trajectory first diverged from a successful one. Without that step-level chain, the same zero can be produced by all four categories, and no retained measurement separates them:

- A model that never visits the new door row (discovery) and a model that visits it but whose values do not encode the timing (knowledge) and a model whose values encode it but whose selector picks another act (decision) and a model that picks the right act but arrives when the door is closed or is blocked by the wall (execution) can all produce 0/20, 4000 ticks, and similar aggregate act counts. The records do not contain the step at which these stories diverge.
- The one partial exception proves the rule: for L-SHARED W20 seed 1, trained and held-out act_steps are identical ([289, 3518, 193]) and the diagnostic counts are identical, which the pilot report notes as consistent with the agent never reaching the wall, so that the door-row difference never matters. That is an **INFERENCE** from identical aggregates, not a recorded position, and it applies to one job only. It does not generalise to the other held-out jobs, whose trained and held-out aggregates differ.

### 1.3 The missing job changes every pooled denominator

**OBSERVED:** Planned pilot: 12 trainings + 24 evaluations + 24 diagnostics = 60 records. Completed: 11 trainings + 22 evaluations + 22 diagnostics = **55 records, 0 duplicates**. Missing, exactly: training `DEV-LPAIR-001:train:L-SVA:W20:2`, evaluations `:eval:L-SVA:W20:2:W20` and `:eval:L-SVA:W20:2:W20h`, and their two diagnostics, plus the weights file `weights_L-SVA_W20_2.npz`.
Consequences, stated in §6: SVA totals must use denominators 100 (trained and held-out evaluations), not 120; matched comparisons must use 5 pairs (100 episodes), not 6; the missing seed's held-out result is **UNKNOWN, not zero**; no pooled L-SHARED-vs-L-SVA held-out rate can be computed on a common 120-episode denominator; and no diagnostic statement about SVA on W20 can be made for seed 2.

### 1.4 What would change this conclusion

Only step-level trajectory evidence of the kind specified in §7, collected under a separate owner authorisation with a stated label, scope, and pooling rule, and validated against the existing aggregates (see §3G, progression condition P-VAL). Until then, the correct diagnostic verdict for the four categories, on existing evidence, is **INCONCLUSIVE** for discovery, knowledge, and decision, and **INCONCLUSIVE with one narrow NOT_SUPPORTED sub-case** for execution (crush-as-sole-cause, §3, Execution). The missing seed is **UNKNOWN** across all categories.

### 2.2 What each record type actually contains — and what it discards

**Training record** (`job_type = "training"`, 11 records). Fields: construction, world, seed, episodes = 1000, wall_s, reached (sum over 1000 episodes), ticks_total, ticks_mean, crushes_total, updates, updates_act, n_params, mean_loss_sel, mean_loss_act, act_steps (3 counts, summed over all training steps), act_episodes_used (count of episodes in which each act was used, out of 1000).
Discarded by `run_pilot.py` aggregation: per-episode ticks/reached/crushes/loss, per-step act/action/reward/state, per-episode start tick, replay contents, and any position or door-approach information. Training `act_steps` are exploration-mixed (ε from 1.0 down to ~0.05095), so they are not a measure of the greedy policy's act usage.

**Evaluation record** (`job_type = "evaluation"`, 22 records). Fields: construction, train_world, eval_world, eval_kind (trained / held-out), seed, episodes = 20, wall_s, reached (sum over 20), ticks_total, ticks_mean, crushes_total, act_steps (3 counts, summed over all eval steps in the job), act_episodes_used (count of episodes in which each act was used, out of 20).
Discarded: per-episode breakdown (including per-episode start_phase 0..19, ticks, crushes, reached, return), per-step position, door_open, ScheduleModel K/phase/remaining/contradiction, selector Q values, head Q values, selected action, next position, reward, and episode-end reason. The job record does not say *which* episodes reached, *which* steps used which act, or *in what order* acts were used.

**Diagnostic record** (`job_type = "diagnostic"`, 22 records). Fields: construction, train_world, eval_world, eval_kind, seed, diagnostic = {n_states, per_head_action_counts, pairwise_agreement, plus for L-SVA only selector_vs_headvalue}.
How it was produced (**OBSERVED, `lpair.py:eval_job` + `diagnostic`)**: during the eval job, every encoder vector `x` was appended to an in-memory `states` list; after the job, `diagnostic(agent, S)` was computed **offline** from those vectors, with no environment step and no weight update. `run_pilot.py` then discarded the `states` list as `_states` and saved only the diagnostic summary in the JSONL. Raw states are **not** persisted as separate files.
What the diagnostic therefore is: for each *visited* state, the greedy action each head *would* have chosen (argmax over 5 actions, per head), counted and compared pairwise; for SVA only, whether the selector argmax equals the head-value argmax (max over heads of max action value), plus mean selector margin and mean head-value margin (top-two Q gap, averaged over visited states).
What the diagnostic is **not**: the action actually taken at each state (that depends on the selected head, which is not saved per state); a Q-value table; a position map; a schedule-knowledge measure; or a statement about unvisited states. Its denominator is *visited steps* (`n_states` = ticks_total for that job), not episodes, not world states, and not possible states. For a held-out job with 0/20, `n_states` is always 4000 (20 × 200); for a trained job it is smaller when episodes reach (for example, Shared W10 seed 0 trained: 570 states).

### 2.3 Weights: why they do not count as existing trajectory evidence

**OBSERVED:** 11 weights files exist, one per completed training; the 12th (L-SVA W20 seed 2) does not. The pilot report states that, given frozen weights and a fresh encoder, a fresh eval run would reproduce the same eval trajectory, but that reproduction was **not performed and is not claimed** in the pilot.
**INFERENCE / boundary:** Re-running evaluation from saved weights would be a *new execution* that generates *new* step-level data. It is not "existing evidence", even if it is deterministic in principle, because (a) it has not been performed, (b) its agreement with the existing aggregates has not been verified, and (c) the work order for this specification authorises drafting only, not execution. §7 therefore treats "trajectory from saved weights" as a *proposed future diagnostic step* (with a validation gate in §3G), not as a source that retroactively fills the existing-evidence gaps. Any future collection must be separately authorised, with a stated label that distinguishes it from the original DEV-LPAIR-001 records.

### 2.4 Evaluation protocol facts that constrain interpretation (OBSERVED, from code)

- Eval is **greedy** (ε = 0, `agent.act_greedy`), weights frozen, no training step, no replay addition during eval.
- Eval job: **20 episodes, start phases 0..19 in order** (`w.reset(start_tick=start)`), one fresh encoder per job, encoder **persists across the job's 20 episodes** and is reset per episode. ScheduleModel `new_episode()` clears only the pending previous observation and marks the span gapped; **events and estimates persist across episodes** (sibling `wrapper.py:new_episode`). Early episodes in a job therefore start with a blank schedule (K = 0, phase/remaining = 0.0), later episodes may inherit estimates — but the records do not split any measurement by episode or by K, so early-vs-late schedule effects cannot be separated from existing records.
- Training: start ticks drawn from `Random(seed+1000)`, range 0..19; encoder persists across training episodes, reset per episode, same ScheduleModel persistence.
- Environment: shared 20/10 schedule (door open iff `tick % 20 < 10`); episode cap 200 ticks; rewards step −0.01, goal +1.0, crush −1.0; crush returns the agent to start and the episode **continues** (it does not end the episode). A job with ticks_mean 200.0 therefore does not imply "no crushes" — SVA W10 seed 1 held-out proves the point (83 crushes, ticks_mean still 200.0).
- Input: grid²+5 — one-hot position, door bit D, phase/20.0, remaining/10.0, known flag K, prev-door bit. Forbidden inputs (per `lpair.py` header): world period/open_len, goal, geometry, next_door, episode counter, tick as direct input, safe-to-cross flag, fixed act-selection rule. The network therefore cannot "know" the held-out door row except through the position one-hot and the door bit it observes while visiting positions.
- Training geometry vs held-out geometry: W10 trained door (5,5), held-out (5,2); W20 trained door (10,10), held-out (10,5). The held-out door row was **never present in training** for either world. That is a design fact, not a diagnostic finding — it makes "the model never saw the held-out door during training" true by construction, and it makes the eval question "did it visit the new door row during greedy eval?" the relevant discovery question, not "did training contain it?".

## 3. Four-category operational definitions

Each category is defined in the same five parts required by the work order: (A) operational claim, (B) measurement, (C) decision rule, (D) thresholds, (E) earliest distinguishable failure point. §3F (stop conditions) and §3G (progression conditions) apply across categories and are stated once, after the four categories, to avoid repeating identical stop rules four times with drift. A category is **provisional diagnostic category, not an established cause**, until its SUPPORTED criterion is met and the main alternatives are excluded by their stated criteria.

### 3.1 Category 1 — Discovery failure

**A. Operational claim.**
- *What it would mean:* The mechanism did not encounter, or did not adequately explore, the information or states needed to discover a successful behaviour. Two loci must be kept separate, because they have different evidence and different remedies:
  - **D1 — Training discovery:** during training, the agent never (or rarely) visited the states, door approaches, or timing conditions that a successful held-out policy would need, so no learning signal for them existed.
  - **D2 — Eval discovery:** during greedy held-out evaluation, the agent never (or rarely) visited the held-out door row, approached it, or entered a state from which crossing was possible, so no learned behaviour could be exercised, regardless of what the weights contain.
- *Evidence that would support it:* For D2, a step-level record showing that, across a held-out job, the agent's visited positions never include the held-out door cell or its approach cells, or never include a position from which the door cell is reachable at an open time, while the job still runs to the cap. For D1, a step-level training record showing that the corresponding trained-world approach states were never or rarely visited during training (with "rarely" defined before the diagnostic, not after — see §3D).
- *Evidence that would contradict it:* For D2, a step-level record showing the agent *did* visit the held-out door cell or approach it at an open time and still did not cross or reach the goal — that moves the failure downstream to knowledge, decision, or execution, and D2 as the *sole* cause is NOT_SUPPORTED for that job. For D1, a training record showing frequent visits to the trained door approach and successful crossings during training (in the greedy sense, not merely under exploration) would contradict D1 as the sole cause.
- *Other categories that could produce the same observation:* Knowledge (agent visits the door but values do not encode timing), decision (agent visits the door but selector picks another act), execution (agent visits the door at a closed time, is blocked, or loops). Aggregate act counts and zero success cannot separate D2 from any of these, because none of them is recorded.

**B. Measurement.**
- **M-D1 (available, weak):** Training reached / 1000 per training job, and training act_steps / act_episodes_used. Unit: training job. Denominator: 1000 episodes (reached) or all training steps (act_steps). *What it measures:* whether training episodes ended at the goal at all, under exploration. *What it does not measure:* which positions were visited, whether the door was approached, or whether the greedy policy would visit them. **Availability: available from existing records, but non-discriminating for D1/D2.**
- **M-D2 (available, weak, one-job only):** Identical trained-vs-held-out act_steps and diagnostic counts within a job (Shared W20 seed 1). Unit: eval job pair. Denominator: 4000 visited steps each. *What it measures:* that the visited-state distributions, as summarised by head-action counts, coincided. *What it does not measure:* positions directly. **Availability: available for one job as an INFERENCE (see §1.2), not as a recorded position.**
- **M-D3 (required, not available):** Visited-position set per held-out job: set of (x, y) visited, count of visits to held-out door cell, count of visits to approach cells (defined in §7 as the cells adjacent to the door cell on the start side, plus the door cell itself), and earliest tick at which the door cell was first visited (or "never", if absent). Unit: step, aggregated to episode and job. Denominator: steps in the job (4000 for a 0/20 job) and episodes (20). **Availability: NOT available from existing records. Requires trajectory-level data (§7).**
- **M-D4 (required, not available):** Training visited-position set per training job, same definitions, plus per-episode start-phase breakdown. **Availability: NOT available. Training records contain no position information at all.**

**C. Decision rule (for a single held-out job; the missing job is UNKNOWN, not INCONCLUSIVE).**
- **SUPPORTED (D2):** M-D3 shows the held-out door cell was never visited in the job, AND the job ran to the cap in all episodes, AND no alternative downstream failure can have occurred (because the downstream state was never reached). This is the only form in which D2 can be SUPPORTED alone.
- **NOT_SUPPORTED (D2 as sole cause):** M-D3 shows the held-out door cell was visited at least once at an open time (door_open = True at the visit step) in the job, and the job still reached 0/20. The failure must then be at or after that visit (knowledge, decision, or execution).
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** M-D3 is unavailable (the case for every job on existing evidence), OR M-D3 shows visits only at closed times (which confounds D2 with timing/execution — see §8, ambiguity A-3), OR training discovery (D1) is invoked without M-D4. In all these cases the job's discovery status cannot be assigned from existing records.
- **UNKNOWN:** The job does not exist (L-SVA W20 seed 2 held-out), or the visited-position definition (approach cells) has not been agreed by the owner before the diagnostic.

**D. Thresholds.**
- No numerical threshold for "adequate exploration" is set in this specification, and none is imported. "Never visited" is a qualitative, exact criterion (count = 0), not a threshold. Any future "rarely visited" criterion (for example, visits below X% of steps) would require an owner decision *before* the diagnostic is run, with a stated justification (for example, a comparison to the trained-world visit rate for the corresponding door cell), because a retrospective cut-off chosen after seeing the counts would be data-dredging. **Threshold status: exact-zero rule adopted; any non-zero rarity threshold = OWNER DECISION REQUIRED.**
- The trained-world visit rate, if ever used as a reference, must use the same denominator (steps, not episodes) and the same approach-cell definition as the held-out measurement, or the comparison is invalid (§8, confounding C-3).

**E. Earliest distinguishable failure point.**
- With existing records: **none**. Aggregate act_steps and diagnostic counts do not contain a position, so the earliest point at which the trajectory diverged from a successful one (first failure to approach, first approach at a closed time, first wrong act, first blocked move) cannot be located. The only job-level hint is the Shared W20 seed 1 identical-aggregate INFERENCE, which suggests "never reached the wall" for that job, but does not locate a tick, a position, or a first divergence.
- With trajectory data (§7): the earliest distinguishable point is the first step at which the agent's position set stops containing any path to the held-out door at an open time — in practice, the first episode tick at which the agent is in a position from which it cannot reach the door before it closes, or the end of the episode with the door cell never visited. That point is definable only once position + door_open + tick are recorded per step.

### 3.2 Category 2 — Knowledge failure

**A. Operational claim.**
- *What it would mean:* The mechanism's learned representations or values did not retain or express the information required for successful behaviour, even if the relevant states were visited. Sub-forms, kept separate because they imply different remedies:
  - **K1 — Representation failure:** the held-out states are not distinguishable in the model's input or hidden representation from states for which a different behaviour was learned (for example, the new door row's position one-hot is untrained, or the schedule features are blank/misleading).
  - **K2 — Value failure:** the learned Q-values (selector Q and/or head Q) do not rank the successful act/action highest on the relevant states, even though the states are represented.
  - **K3 — Retention / schedule-knowledge failure:** the ScheduleModel's estimates (phase, remaining, known flag) are absent, stale, or wrong at the relevant steps, so the timing information the policy needs is not present in the input. Note: ScheduleModel estimates persist across episodes within a job (§2.4), so K3 can differ between early and late episodes in the same job.
- *Evidence that would support it:* For K1, a step-level record showing that on held-out states the model's hidden or Q outputs are near-identical to those on trained states for which a different action is correct, or that the held-out door-row position inputs were never updated during training (by construction, they were not — but that alone does not prove K1 caused failure in eval, because the agent might still succeed via other inputs). For K2, per-state Q values showing that the successful act/action is not argmax on states the agent actually visited, *where "successful" is defined by a successful trajectory* (for example, the action that would move toward the door at an open time), not by a head's own argmax. For K3, per-step K/phase/remaining showing K = 0 or phase/remaining inconsistent with the true schedule at the steps where crossing was possible.
- *Evidence that would contradict it:* Per-state Q values showing that, on visited held-out states, the successful act/action *is* argmax, and the agent still fails — that moves the failure downstream to decision (if a different act was chosen despite its Q) or execution (if the argmax action was taken but did not succeed). A diagnostic showing only that heads differ from each other does **not** contradict or support K2, because head differences are counterfactual argmaxes on visited states, not a measure of whether any head's values encode the successful behaviour.
- *Other categories that could produce the same observation:* Discovery (values are never exercised because the state is never visited — then K2 cannot be assessed on that state at all); decision (values are correct but the selector's argmax differs from the head-value argmax, or the selector picks a different head); execution (values and choice are correct but timing/blocking prevents success). Mean losses and aggregate agreement rates cannot separate these.

**B. Measurement.**
- **M-K1 (available, non-discriminating):** Mean training losses (mean_loss_sel, mean_loss_act) per training job, averaged over training updates. Unit: training job. Denominator: updates in the job (not stated per job in the record beyond updates count). *What it measures:* average masked-MSE loss during training, under exploration, on replayed transitions. *What it does not measure:* loss or Q accuracy on held-out states, on greedy eval states, or at the door-approach steps. **Availability: available, but not a knowledge measure for held-out behaviour.**
- **M-K2 (available, non-discriminating):** Diagnostic per-head action counts and pairwise agreement, per eval job. Unit: visited state. Denominator: n_states (visited steps). *What it measures:* whether frozen heads would choose the same greedy action on the same visited state, and what action distribution each head implies. *What it does not measure:* whether any head's argmax is the *successful* action, what the Q values or margins were for the successful action, or whether the state is represented distinctly from trained states. Head differences alongside 0/20 (present in most held-out jobs) are a direct example: differences exist, success does not — differences therefore do not establish usefulness, and agreement does not establish correctness.
- **M-K3 (available, non-discriminating):** For SVA only, selector_vs_headvalue agreement (agree / n_states) and mean selector / head-value margins, per eval job. Held-out pooled SVA: 8041/20000 = 40.2% agreement; per-job 25.8%–56.6%; mean selector margins 0.0024–0.0136, mean head-value margins 0.0046–0.0097 (held-out jobs). Unit: visited state. Denominator: n_states. *What it measures:* how often selector argmax coincides with the argmax of best-head values, and how large the top-two Q gaps are on average. *What it does not measure:* whether either argmax is the successful act, whether head values are comparable across heads (heads were trained on different state distributions, so a cross-head max is not a calibrated comparison — see §8, confounding C-5), or whether the selector is "weak" in any causal sense. Agreement near 33% (chance for 3 acts) is descriptive, not a proof of weakness, usefulness, success, or failure of value alignment. For L-SHARED, no selector_vs_headvalue measurement exists at all.
- **M-K4 (required, not available):** Per-step Q values (selector Q[3], all head Q[3×5] or at least selected-head Q[5]) on visited held-out states, plus per-step K/phase/remaining/contradiction and true schedule (door open/closed, ticks to next close/open) at the same steps. Unit: step. Denominator: visited steps in the job, split by episode and by K. **Availability: NOT available. No Q value, K, phase, or remaining value was saved per step or per job.**
- **M-K5 (required, not available):** A representation-distinctness measure, defined before collection: for example, the distribution of Q vectors on held-out door-approach states vs on trained door-approach states, with a stated distance and a stated reference. **Availability: NOT available, and the distance/reference themselves require an owner decision before collection (§9).**

**C. Decision rule.**
- **SUPPORTED (K2):** M-K4 shows, on a defined set of visited held-out states from which crossing was possible (door approach, open time, correct side of wall), that the successful act/action is not argmax in selector/head Q, AND discovery is excluded for those states (they were visited), AND the decision rule (§3.3) is applied to the same states and shows the selector did not override a correct value (otherwise the failure is decision, not knowledge). All three conditions must hold; M-K4 alone is not sufficient.
- **NOT_SUPPORTED (K2 as sole cause):** M-K4 shows the successful act/action *is* argmax on the relevant visited states, and the job still fails. The failure is then downstream (decision or execution).
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** M-K4 unavailable (all jobs on existing evidence); OR M-K4 available but the "successful action" for a state is not defined independently of a head's argmax (circular); OR K1/K2/K3 cannot be separated because K/phase/remaining were not recorded alongside Q.
- **UNKNOWN:** The job does not exist (L-SVA W20 seed 2), or the representation-distinctness measure (M-K5) has not been defined and agreed before collection.

**D. Thresholds.**
- No threshold for agreement rate, margin size, or head-difference rate is set, and none is imported. Reasons, stated explicitly: (1) there is no pre-registered diagnostic threshold for these quantities; (2) agreement with head-value argmax is not ground truth for "correct act" (see confounding C-5); (3) margins are in Q units whose scale depends on reward, discount, and training, and no calibration to success exists in the records; (4) a retrospective threshold chosen after seeing 25.8%–56.6% agreement would be data-dredging. **Threshold status: no numerical threshold adopted; any future threshold for agreement/margin/head-difference = OWNER DECISION REQUIRED, to be set before the diagnostic run, with a stated justification and a stated reference (for example, the same measurement on trained jobs, with the same denominator, as a descriptive reference — not as a pass/fail bar).**
- The only exact rule adopted for knowledge is the argmax rule itself: on a defined relevant state, either the successful action is argmax or it is not. That rule still requires an independent definition of "successful action" for that state (§7, field: suitable_action_reference), which must be defined from trajectory outcome (for example, "the action that moves the agent one step closer to the held-out door along a shortest path, at a time when the door is open or will be open on arrival"), not from any head's argmax.

**E. Earliest distinguishable failure point.**
- With existing records: **none** for knowledge. Mean losses are training aggregates, diagnostic counts are visited-state aggregates without Q values, and neither is split by position, door approach, or K. The point at which values first fail to rank the successful action cannot be located.
- With trajectory data (§7): the earliest step at which, on a visited state from which crossing was possible, the successful action is not argmax, with K/phase/remaining recorded at the same step so that K1/K2/K3 can be separated (for example, K = 0 at that step points toward schedule-knowledge, while K = 1 with correct phase/remaining and still wrong argmax points toward value/representation).

### 3.3 Category 3 — Decision failure

**A. Operational claim.**
- *What it would mean:* The necessary information or useful learned values may have been available, but the selector or action policy chose an unsuitable act or action. Sub-forms:
  - **Dec1 — Selector failure:** the selector chose an act whose head's action is unsuitable, while another act's head would have chosen a suitable action on the same state.
  - **Dec2 — Head-action failure:** the selector chose a suitable act, but that head's greedy action on that state is unsuitable (this overlaps K2; the distinction is whether the *head's* values are wrong (knowledge) or the *choice among heads* is wrong (decision) — see ambiguity A-4 in §8).
  - **Dec3 — Coordination failure (SVA only):** selector and action networks disagree systematically, so the act that the selector predicts to be best is not the act whose head values are highest, and the executed act is the selector's, not the head-value's.
- *Evidence that would support it:* Per-step records showing, on the same visited state, (a) the selector's chosen act, (b) the action the chosen head actually took, (c) the action each other head would have taken, and (d) an independent suitable-action reference for that state — with (b) unsuitable and (c) suitable for at least one other head (for Dec1), or (b) suitable in value but not taken because of a different selector choice (for Dec3). A single aggregate agreement rate cannot support this, because it does not show what was actually taken vs what would have been suitable.
- *Evidence that would contradict it:* Per-step records showing that, on the relevant visited states, the selector chose the act whose head's action *was* the suitable action, and the job still failed — then the failure is downstream (execution) or the suitable-action reference is wrong, not decision.
- *Other categories that could produce the same observation:* Knowledge (no head's action is suitable on that state, so no selector choice could have succeeded — then decision cannot be the sole cause); discovery (the relevant state was never visited, so no decision was ever made on it); execution (the suitable action was chosen and taken, but blocked/timing prevented success). Aggregate act_steps — for example, ACT-2 used least in many trained jobs, or act_steps skewed in held-out jobs — cannot separate these, because act_steps count *steps* per act, not *suitability*, and they are not split by position, door approach, or outcome.

**B. Measurement.**
- **M-Dec1 (available, non-discriminating):** Eval act_steps (3 counts per job) and act_episodes_used (count of episodes in which each act was used, out of 20). Unit: step (act_steps) / episode (act_episodes_used). Denominator: ticks_total for the job (act_steps) or 20 episodes (act_episodes_used). *What it measures:* how often each act was selected in total, and in how many episodes it was selected at least once. *What it does not measure:* which action was taken, on which state, in what order, or whether it was suitable. Act_steps in training are exploration-mixed and must not be compared directly to greedy eval act_steps as if they were the same policy.
- **M-Dec2 (available, non-discriminating):** Diagnostic per-head counts + pairwise agreement + (SVA only) selector_vs_headvalue agreement/margins — same measurements as M-K2/M-K3, same limits. *What they do not measure for decision:* the actually selected act per state. The diagnostic is computed offline from recorded states for *all* heads, regardless of which head was selected at that step in the live job. It therefore cannot show, per state, whether the selected head's action differed from a suitable action.
- **M-Dec3 (required, not available):** Per-step selected act, selected-head action, all-head counterfactual actions, and suitable-action reference, on the same state, with position and door_open. Unit: step. Denominator: visited steps in the job, split by episode and by door-approach vs non-approach states. **Availability: NOT available.**
- **M-Dec4 (required, not available):** Decision-error rate, defined before collection: number of steps on relevant states (door approach, open time) where the taken action differs from the suitable-action reference, divided by the number of relevant steps. **Availability: NOT available, and the suitable-action reference itself requires an owner-agreed definition (§7).**

**C. Decision rule.**
- **SUPPORTED (Dec1, per job):** M-Dec3 shows, on a defined set of relevant visited states, that the taken action was unsuitable while at least one other head's counterfactual action was suitable, AND knowledge is excluded for those states in the sense that a suitable head action existed (otherwise the failure is knowledge, not decision). The proportion of such states must be stated with its denominator; a single example does not support a job-level decision-failure diagnosis.
- **NOT_SUPPORTED (Dec1 as sole cause):** M-Dec3 shows that on relevant visited states the taken action *was* the suitable action (or no head's action was suitable), and the job still fails. Then decision, as defined, is not the sole cause.
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** M-Dec3 unavailable (all jobs on existing evidence); OR suitable-action reference not defined independently of head argmax; OR Dec1/Dec2/K2 cannot be separated because per-step Q values were not recorded alongside the taken action.
- **UNKNOWN:** The job does not exist (L-SVA W20 seed 2), or the suitable-action reference has not been agreed before collection.

**D. Thresholds.**
- No threshold for "how many decision errors constitute decision failure" is set. A single wrong act on a single step does not establish a job-level diagnosis, and a retrospective percentage (for example, "more than 50% of relevant steps") would be arbitrary without a reference. **Threshold status: no numerical threshold adopted; any future decision-error-rate threshold = OWNER DECISION REQUIRED, to be set before collection, with a stated reference (for example, the same rate on trained jobs, same denominator, as a descriptive reference — not as a pass/fail bar).** The qualitative rule adopted is: decision failure is SUPPORTED only if unsuitable taken actions on relevant states are *systematic* (present across episodes and across start phases, not confined to a single episode) — "systematic" itself must be operationalised before collection if it is to be used as a criterion (for example, present in at least X of 20 episodes, with X set by owner decision, not by this specification).

**E. Earliest distinguishable failure point.**
- With existing records: **none**. Act_steps are job totals without order, position, or action, so the first unsuitable decision cannot be located.
- With trajectory data (§7): the earliest step on which the taken action differs from the suitable-action reference on a relevant state (door approach, open time), with the selector Q, head Q, and counterfactual head actions recorded at the same step, so that Dec1/Dec2/Dec3 can be separated at that point.

### 3.4 Category 4 — Execution failure

**A. Operational claim.**
- *What it would mean:* The selected act or action was appropriate, but its execution, transition, environment interaction, or episode handling prevented success. Sub-forms, kept separate because the records treat them differently:
  - **Ex1 — Crush execution:** the agent is in a door cell when the door closes, is returned to start, and loses progress. Episode continues; crush does not end the episode.
  - **Ex2 — Blocked / wall execution:** the chosen action attempts to move into a wall, obstacle, or closed door cell and the position does not change (or changes differently from intended). No crush necessarily occurs.
  - **Ex3 — Timing execution:** the agent arrives at the door when it is closed, or arrives too late in the open window to cross, or waits/loops until the window closes. The action may be directionally correct but temporally wrong.
  - **Ex4 — Looping / non-progress execution:** the agent cycles among a small set of positions, or stays, without approaching the door, despite choosing actions that are locally plausible. This overlaps discovery (never approaching) and decision (repeatedly choosing a looping act) — see §8, ambiguity A-5.
  - **Ex5 — Episode-handling execution:** start-phase, cap, or ScheduleModel persistence effects prevent success (for example, an episode starts at a phase from which the door cannot be reached before it closes, or the cap truncates a trajectory that would otherwise succeed). This is an environment/protocol effect, not a policy error.
- *Evidence that would support it:* For Ex1, per-step records showing crush events at door cells, with position, tick, and door_open at the crush step, and a trajectory that would otherwise have progressed. For Ex2, per-step records showing attempted action vs position_before vs position_after, with position_after = position_before despite a move action, at a wall/closed-door boundary. For Ex3, per-step records showing arrival at the door cell's approach at a closed time, or arrival during the open window but too late to cross before closing. For Ex4, per-step position sequences showing cycles or a bounded visited set over many ticks. For Ex5, per-episode start_phase vs outcome, showing that success is impossible from certain start phases under the cap/schedule (a protocol fact, to be checked against the world, not assumed).
- *Evidence that would contradict it:* For Ex1 as sole cause, crush count zero in the job (see below). For Ex2/Ex3/Ex4 as sole cause, per-step records showing the agent never approached the door (then discovery, not execution, is the earlier failure) or never chose a suitable action (then knowledge/decision is earlier). Execution can only be the *first* failure if the earlier chain (visit → value → choice) is shown to have succeeded up to the execution step.
- *Other categories that could produce the same observation:* Discovery (agent loops in the start area because it never discovers the door — same position trace as Ex4, different cause); decision (agent repeatedly chooses a looping act — same trace, different level); knowledge (values do not encode the door timing, so the agent arrives closed — same trace as Ex3, different level). Position traces alone, without Q and choice data at the same steps, cannot separate Ex3/Ex4 from knowledge/decision.

**B. Measurement.**
- **M-Ex1 (available, discriminating for one sub-case only):** Crushes_total per eval job. Unit: job. Denominator: 20 episodes (crushes per episode is not recorded; only the job total). *What it measures:* total crush events in the job. *What it does not measure:* at which step, position, tick, or door, or whether the crush was the first divergence. **Availability: available, and it is the only retained measurement that can exclude a sub-cause (see §3C).**
- **M-Ex2 (available, non-discriminating):** Ticks_total / ticks_mean per job. Unit: job. Denominator: 20 episodes. *What it measures:* total steps to cap or goal. *What it does not measure:* whether steps were progress, looping, blocked, or waiting, because position is not recorded. A job with 4000 ticks could be looping, blocked, waiting, or approaching-and-missing repeatedly — the aggregate cannot tell.
- **M-Ex3 (required, not available):** Per-step position_before, attempted action, position_after, blocked flag, door_open, tick, and crush flag, plus per-episode crush count and episode-end reason. Unit: step, aggregated to episode and job. Denominator: steps in the job/episode. **Availability: NOT available.**
- **M-Ex4 (required, not available):** Per-episode start_phase vs reached, and per-episode visited-position set, to test Ex5. Unit: episode. Denominator: 20 episodes per job, split by start_phase 0..19. **Availability: NOT available — start_phase is in code (`eval_job` loops 0..19 in order) but the per-episode breakdown was discarded by `run_pilot.py` aggregation.**

**C. Decision rule.**
- **SUPPORTED (Ex1 as sole cause, per job):** Crushes_total is high *and* per-step records show crushes at the door cell are the first divergence from a trajectory that otherwise approached and would have crossed, *and* discovery/knowledge/decision are excluded up to that point by M-D3/M-K4/M-Dec3 on the same steps. Crush count alone, however high, does **not** support Ex1 as sole cause, because crushes can be a consequence of looping or of arriving at the wrong time.
- **NOT_SUPPORTED (Ex1 as sole cause, per job):** Crushes_total = 0 in the job. This excludes crush-as-sole-cause for that job. **It does not exclude execution in general** (Ex2/Ex3/Ex4/Ex5 remain possible), and it does not exclude crush as a contributor in a different job. On existing evidence, this NOT_SUPPORTED applies to: all 6 Shared held-out jobs, and 4 of 5 completed SVA held-out jobs. It does **not** apply to SVA W10 seed 1 held-out (83 crushes), where Ex1 remains INCONCLUSIVE (not SUPPORTED) because per-step crush position/timing are missing.
- **SUPPORTED (Ex2/Ex3/Ex4/Ex5):** Per-step records (M-Ex3/M-Ex4) show the relevant pattern (blocked moves, closed-time arrivals, cycles, or start-phase impossibility) *and* the earlier chain (visit, value, choice) is shown to have succeeded up to that point. Without that earlier-chain evidence, the pattern is INCONCLUSIVE, not SUPPORTED, because the same pattern can be produced by earlier failures.
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** M-Ex3/M-Ex4 unavailable (all jobs on existing evidence, for Ex2–Ex5); OR crush count present but per-step position/timing missing (SVA W10 seed 1, Ex1).
- **UNKNOWN:** The job does not exist (L-SVA W20 seed 2).

**D. Thresholds.**
- No threshold for "how many crushes constitute execution failure" is set. Crush count zero is an exact exclusion for Ex1-as-sole-cause; any non-zero crush count is **not** by itself a threshold for Ex1-as-cause, because crushes can be consequential. **Threshold status: exact-zero exclusion adopted for Ex1-as-sole-cause only; any non-zero crush threshold or blocked-move / cycle threshold = OWNER DECISION REQUIRED, to be set before collection, with a stated definition of "cycle" (for example, revisiting the same position at least N times within M ticks, with N/M set by owner decision) and "blocked" (position unchanged despite a move action, at a wall/closed-door boundary).**
- Episode-end reason is not a threshold: "cap" is inferred from ticks_total = 4000 and reached = 0 (see §1.1), but it is not a recorded reason code, and it must not be presented as one in any future record without being recorded as a field (§7).

**E. Earliest distinguishable failure point.**
- With existing records: **none** for Ex2–Ex5, and **none** for Ex1's first crush (only the job total is known). The first divergence from a successful trajectory cannot be located from job totals.
- With trajectory data (§7): the earliest step at which the executed transition differs from the intended transition (blocked move, crush, or closed-door arrival) *after* the visit/value/choice chain has been shown to succeed up to that step. If the chain has not succeeded up to that step, the earliest failure is earlier (discovery/knowledge/decision), and the execution event is a consequence, not the first failure. The specification therefore requires the trajectory schema to record visit, value, choice, and transition at the *same* step, so that "earliest" is defined by step order, not by category preference.

### 3.5 Earliest distinguishable failure point — summary across categories (work-order §3E)

| Failure point | Locatable from existing records? | Why / what is missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First failure to visit the held-out door row (D2) | No, except one-job INFERENCE (Shared W20 seed 1, identical aggregates) | No position per step; no visited set |
| First failure to visit the trained door approach during training (D1) | No | No training position per step; training records are aggregates only |
| First step where values fail to rank the successful action (K2) | No | No Q values per step; no suitable-action reference; no K/phase/remaining per step |
| First step where schedule knowledge is absent/wrong (K3) | No | No K/phase/remaining per step or per episode; ScheduleModel persistence across episodes not split in records |
| First unsuitable selector/head choice (Dec1/Dec2) | No | No selected act/action per step; diagnostic is counterfactual for all heads, not the taken action per state |
| First crush, and whether it was the first divergence (Ex1) | No (job total only) | Crush count per job only; no step, position, tick, or door state at crush |
| First blocked move, closed-time arrival, or cycle (Ex2/Ex3/Ex4) | No | No position_before/after, attempted action, door_open, or position sequence per step |
| First episode-level divergence (which start_phase fails, and why) | No | Per-episode breakdown discarded; start_phase loop (0..19 in order) is in code, but outcomes per start_phase are not in the JSONL |
| Job-level outcome (reached / ticks / crushes / act totals) | Yes | Directly in evaluation records; this is the *only* level at which existing records locate anything, and it locates the outcome, not the failure point |

**Conclusion on §3E:** Aggregate success counts and saved diagnostic summaries **cannot** distinguish discovery, knowledge, decision, and execution failures without per-step trajectories. The diagnostic summaries add one thing aggregates alone do not — that heads differ on visited states and, for SVA, that selector and head-value argmax coincide only 25.8%–56.6% of the time on visited held-out states — but that thing is itself non-discriminating, because it does not show what was actually taken, whether it was suitable, or where the agent was.

### 3.6 Stop conditions (§3F) — when diagnosis must stop and return INCONCLUSIVE or UNKNOWN

Diagnosis must stop (that is, no category may be assigned SUPPORTED or NOT_SUPPORTED beyond the narrow exclusions already stated) if **any** of the following holds for the job or question at hand. These are stop conditions for the *diagnostic assignment*, not for reading the specification.

1. **Missing job:** The job does not exist in the records (L-SVA W20 seed 2 training and both evaluations, plus diagnostics). Return **UNKNOWN** for all categories for that job. Do not impute its result from other seeds, other worlds, or the other construction.
2. **Missing step-level chain:** The question requires a per-step fact (position, door_open, K/phase/remaining, selector Q, head Q, taken act/action, transition) and that fact is not in the records. Return **INCONCLUSIVE** (if the job exists) — do not substitute a code-computable value, a weights-based recomputation, or a plausible story for the missing fact.
3. **Overlapping causes:** Two or more categories are consistent with the same retained observation and no retained measurement separates them. Return **INCONCLUSIVE** for each, and list the overlap explicitly (see §8). Do not choose the "most likely" category by narrative preference.
4. **Incompatible denominators:** The comparison requires a common denominator that does not exist (for example, SVA pooled held-out on 120 episodes, or diagnostic agreement pooled across jobs with different n_states without weighting by the stated denominator, or training act_steps compared to greedy eval act_steps as if they were the same policy). Return **INCONCLUSIVE** for that comparison, and restate the comparison on the correct denominators or not at all.
5. **Insufficient provenance:** The measurement's source, definition, or version is unavailable (for example, a diagnostic number whose n_states, construction, or eval_kind is not stated, or a weights-based value whose weights hash is not stated). Return **UNKNOWN** for that measurement.
6. **Circular suitable-action reference:** The "suitable" action for a state is defined as a head's argmax, or as the action that the model took, or as the action that would have succeeded *assuming* the model already knew the door timing. Return **INCONCLUSIVE** — the reference must be defined independently (trajectory outcome / shortest-path-to-door at an open time, §3.2D/§7) before it can be used.
7. **Retrospective threshold:** A threshold is proposed *after* seeing the diagnostic numbers (agreement, margins, head differences, act coverage, crush counts) and is then used to assign SUPPORTED/NOT_SUPPORTED. Stop and return **INCONCLUSIVE** until the threshold is set before the diagnostic run, with a stated justification, or replaced by an exact qualitative rule (for example, crush count = 0 excludes crush-as-sole-cause).
8. **Incomplete-job pooling:** A pooled rate or pooled diagnostic is computed across completed and missing jobs as if the pilot were complete. Return **UNKNOWN** for the pooled quantity; report completed-job quantities separately with their denominators (§6).

### 3.7 Progression conditions (§3G) — what would justify a subsequent diagnostic step

A subsequent diagnostic step is justified only if it meets **all** of the following, in order. Meeting one condition alone (for example, "trajectories would be interesting") does not justify collection.

- **P-DEF — Definition first:** The diagnostic specification (§3, §7) is approved by the owner, including the suitable-action reference, approach-cell definition, and any thresholds that will be used. No data is collected before the definitions are fixed, or the data will be uninterpretable.
- **P-LABEL — Label and scope stated:** The new collection has a stated label distinct from DEV-LPAIR-001 (for example, a new DEV-LPAIR-DIAG identifier), a stated scope (which constructions, worlds, seeds, and episodes), and a stated pooling rule (whether new trajectories are pooled with, compared to, or kept separate from the original 55 records, and how the missing SVA W20 seed 2 job is handled — excluded, or separately labelled if it is ever collected).
- **P-VAL — Validation against existing aggregates:** If trajectories are generated from saved frozen weights (the cheapest candidate next step, because it requires no retraining), the new trajectories' job-level aggregates (reached, ticks_total, crushes_total, act_steps, diagnostic counts) must match the existing JSONL aggregates for the same job **exactly** before the new step-level data is accepted as representing that job. If they do not match, stop: the new data represent a *different* run, not a diagnosis of the original pilot, and must be labelled as such. This validation gate is a **PROPOSAL** for the owner to approve or amend; it is not already an authorised procedure.
- **P-MIN — Minimum data only:** Only the fields in §7 marked Essential are collected in the first trajectory step. Optional fields are added only if the Essential fields leave a named ambiguity unresolved (stated in the collection authorisation, not added by momentum).
- **P-STOP — Stop rule carried forward:** The §3F stop conditions apply to the new diagnostic as well. In particular, if the new trajectories still cannot separate two categories (for example, because the suitable-action reference remains circular, or because a new confound appears), the diagnostic must return INCONCLUSIVE for those categories, not choose one by preference.
- **P-NOWIN — No winner, no official status:** The new diagnostic, like the pilot, is unofficial and incomplete unless it is separately specified as a complete, preregistered experiment. It must not be presented as an EXP1-COMB result, and it must not select a winning architecture on the basis of diagnostic counts alone.

If existing records cannot resolve the categories (the conclusion of this specification), the minimum additional evidence required is stated in §5 (last column) and specified as a schema in §7. That statement is a *specification of what would be needed*, not an authorisation to collect it.

### 6.2 Denominator rules — mandatory for any use of this specification

1. **Never use 120 as an SVA denominator.** SVA trained = 99/100, SVA held-out = 0/100 (completed). Shared trained = 77/120, Shared held-out = 0/120.
2. **Matched comparisons use 5 pairs, 100 episodes.** Matched trained: Shared 57/100, SVA 99/100. Matched held-out: Shared 0/100, SVA 0/100. The missing pair (W20, seed 2) is excluded from matched comparisons, not counted as zero for SVA.
3. **Diagnostic denominators are visited steps, not episodes.** Shared held-out diagnostic pooled denominator = 24000 steps (6 × 4000); SVA held-out = 20000 steps (5 × 4000). Pooled agreement rates on different denominators must be reported with their denominators, not compared as if they were on a common base. Per-job diagnostic denominators for trained jobs vary with ticks_total (for example, 570 to 4000), because reached episodes contribute fewer steps.
4. **Training denominators are not eval denominators.** Training reached is per 1000 episodes under exploration; eval reached is per 20 episodes under greedy policy. A training rate and an eval rate are not comparable as "the same policy improved/worsened".
5. **Act_steps denominators are steps, act_episodes_used denominators are episodes.** Do not convert one to the other. Act_steps pooled held-out: Shared 24000 steps, SVA 20000 steps. Act_episodes_used pooled: Shared 120 episodes, SVA 100 episodes (per act, counts sum to more than episodes because an act can be used in multiple episodes — the three act_episodes_used counts for a job are not a partition of 20).
6. **Mean losses and mean margins are averages over different populations.** Mean loss: over training updates in a job. Mean margin: over visited eval states in a job. Neither is an average over episodes, and neither is comparable across training/eval or across jobs with different n_states without stating the population.
7. **The missing job is UNKNOWN, not zero, not "assumed similar".** Any sentence of the form "SVA held-out was 0/120" is false. Any sentence of the form "SVA would also have been 0 on seed 2" is UNKNOWN, not an inference.

### 6.3 What "incomplete" does and does not license

- **Does license:** reporting completed-job results with their denominators; stating that the pilot did not establish a winner; stating that held-out failure on completed jobs is 0 on the stated denominators; using the incompleteness itself as a stop condition (§3F-1, §3F-8).
- **Does NOT license:** pooling the missing job as zero; pooling a future rerun with the interrupted records without a stated labelling/pooling/disclosure authorisation; treating the 5 matched pairs as if they were the planned 6; treating diagnostic summaries as if they were step-level data; or treating the pilot as a complete basis for an EXP1-COMB decision.

## 7. Minimum trajectory schema — PROPOSAL ONLY, not implemented, not authorised for collection

**Status of this section: PROPOSAL.** The schema below is a specification for owner review. It has not been implemented, no trajectory has been collected under it, and collecting under it requires a separate owner authorisation with a stated label, scope, and pooling rule (§3G, P-LABEL). Fields are marked **Essential** (required for the four-category discrimination in §3) or **Optional** (useful for a named ambiguity, to be added only if the Essential fields leave that ambiguity unresolved, per §3G P-MIN).

**Unit of analysis:** one row per step, grouped by (run, construction, seed, train_world, eval_world, episode). Episode-level summary rows are derived from step rows, not recorded instead of them. A schema that records only episode summaries would reproduce the current gap at a smaller scale and must not be substituted.

**Validation gate for any future collection from saved weights (PROPOSAL, §3G P-VAL):** before step-level data from a new collection is accepted as representing an original pilot job, its job-level aggregates (reached, ticks_total, crushes_total, act_steps, diagnostic counts recomputed from the new states) must match the existing JSONL aggregates for that job exactly. If they do not match, the new data are a different run and must be labelled as such.

## 8. Contradiction, ambiguity and confounding map

### 8.1 Ambiguities — same observation, multiple categories

| ID | Ambiguity | Why it cannot be resolved from existing records | What would resolve it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A-1 | Zero success + 4000 ticks + skewed act_steps | Consistent with discovery (never approached), knowledge (approached but values wrong), decision (approached but wrong act), execution (approached but blocked/closed/looping). No position, Q, choice, or transition per step. | §7 trajectory: position + Q + choice + transition at the same step, in step order. |
| A-2 | Head differences on visited states + 0/20 | Differences show heads would choose differently, not that any head would succeed, nor that the selected head caused failure. | Per-step taken act/action + suitable-action reference (§7, T19/T21/T27). |
| A-3 | Visit only at closed times | Is this discovery (never visited at a usable time), timing execution (arrived closed), or knowledge (values do not encode the schedule)? The visit exists, but its timing makes it unusable. | Per-step door_open + K/phase/remaining + position at the visit (§7, T12–T15), plus a definition of "usable time" (door open on arrival, or open window long enough to cross) set before collection. |
| A-4 | Selector vs head distinction (Dec vs K) | If the selected head's action is unsuitable, is that because the head's values are wrong (knowledge) or because the selector chose the wrong head (decision)? Both produce the same taken action. | Per-step selector Q + all-head Q + taken act + suitable-action reference, so that "a suitable head existed but was not selected" (Dec) can be distinguished from "no head was suitable" (K). |
| A-5 | Looping / non-progress trace | Same position sequence can be discovery (never found the door), decision (repeatedly chose a looping act), knowledge (values rank looping actions highest), or execution (blocked moves look like loops). | Position sequence + Q + choice + blocked flag at the same steps (§7, T10/T11/T18/T19/T21/T22). Without Q/choice, a loop is a description, not a diagnosis. |
| A-6 | Crush as cause vs crush as consequence | Crushes can cause loss of progress, or can result from looping into the door cell at the wrong time, or from arriving at the door cell without knowing it will close. Job crush totals do not show order. | Per-step crush position/tick/door_open + the trajectory before the crush (§7, T10/T12/T24), with earliest-failure-point defined by step order (§3.4E). |
| A-7 | Early-episode vs late-episode failure | ScheduleModel blank-start (early episodes) vs learned-value failure (late episodes) cannot be separated, because records are not split by episode or by K. | Per-episode split + per-step K/phase/remaining (§7, T7/T13–T15). |
| A-8 | "Suitable act" without act semantics | ACT-0/1/2 have no predefined meaning, so an act count cannot be judged suitable/unsuitable from its label. | Suitable-action reference defined from trajectory outcome / shortest path (§7, T27), agreed before collection. |

### 8.2 Confounds — factors that vary with the comparison and are not controlled in the records

| ID | Confound | Effect on existing comparisons | Control required in any future diagnostic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C-1 | Incomplete pilot / missing seed | SVA denominators differ from Shared; matched pairs are 5, not 6; W20 SVA seed 2 is absent entirely. Any pooled or matched statement that ignores this is wrong (§6). | Report completed jobs separately; state denominators; never impute the missing job; label any future collection separately (§3G, P-LABEL). |
| C-2 | ScheduleModel persistence across episodes within a job | Episodes in a job are not independent: later episodes inherit schedule estimates from earlier ones. Start-phase comparisons (0..19 in order) are confounded with episode order and with schedule learning within the job. | Per-episode + per-step K/phase/remaining; do not treat episodes in a job as independent samples for any statistical claim (this specification makes no statistical claim, and neither should a future diagnostic without a stated model). |
| C-3 | Different denominators across measurements | Diagnostic agreement denominators are visited steps (4000 for 0/20 held-out jobs, variable for trained jobs); reached denominators are episodes (20 per job); act_steps denominators are steps; act_episodes_used denominators are episodes. Cross-measurement comparisons without denominator conversion are invalid. | State denominator for every number; convert only by a stated operation (for example, per-episode rates from step rows, not from job totals). |
| C-4 | Exploration training vs greedy eval | Training act_steps, reached, and losses are under ε exploration; eval act_steps and reached are under greedy policy. A training-vs-eval difference is not, by itself, evidence of learning, forgetting, or policy change. | Compare greedy-to-greedy (eval jobs) for policy statements; use training data only for training-process description, not as a policy baseline, unless a greedy training-policy measurement is separately collected and labelled. |
| C-5 | Cross-head value comparability (SVA) | Heads were trained on different state distributions (because the selector's choices during training determine which head's transitions are sampled for which states, via replay). A cross-head max (head-value argmax) is therefore not a calibrated "best act" measure. Selector_vs_headvalue agreement is descriptive, not a correctness measure. | Suitable-action reference defined independently of head values (§7, T27); do not use head-value argmax as ground truth for "correct act" in any future diagnostic. |
| C-6 | Shared-network interference (L-SHARED) | In Shared, selector and heads share a hidden representation, so a Q difference cannot be attributed to selector vs head without additional analysis. In SVA, networks are separate, so the same Q difference has a different meaning. Cross-construction Q comparisons must state this structural difference. | Record construction + network identity with every Q value (§7, T2/T18/T20); do not pool Q-based measures across constructions without stating the structural difference. |
| C-7 | Identical aggregates ≠ identical trajectories (Shared W20 seed 1) | Identical act_steps and diagnostic counts for trained and held-out in that job are *consistent with* never reaching the wall, but identical aggregates do not prove identical step sequences. | C-8 | Frozen weights, evolving encoder | Weights are frozen during eval, but the encoder/ScheduleModel state evolves across episodes within a job. ### 8.3 Contradictions in the existing evidence — checked, not smoothed over

- **No contradiction in the headline counts.** Recomputed JSONL aggregates match the pilot report's headline numbers (Shared held-out 0/120, SVA held-out 0/100 completed, matched trained 57/100 vs 99/100, matched held-out 0/100 vs 0/100, 55 records, missing SVA W20 seed 2 job set). Where the report and the JSONL could differ (for example, the report's interpretation of identical aggregates), this specification labels the interpretation INFERENCE and keeps the aggregate itself OBSERVED.
- **Apparent contradiction: "diagnostic shows heads differ, yet success is zero" — not a contradiction.** Head differences are counterfactual argmax differences on visited states. They do not imply that any head would succeed, that the selected head was wrong, or that differences caused failure. The pilot report states this limit explicitly, and this specification preserves it (§3.2B, M-K2).
- **Apparent contradiction: "SVA trained higher, held-out tied at zero" — not a contradiction.** Trained and held-out are different tasks (same door row vs moved door row), different denominators for SVA (100 vs 100 completed, but different jobs), and trained success is not the primary outcome. A trained difference with a held-out tie does not imply a construction difference on held-out behaviour, and it does not imply a winner.
- **Apparent contradiction: "crushes 83 in one SVA job, yet ticks_mean 200.0" — not a contradiction.** Crush returns the agent to start and the episode continues to the cap; crushes do not shorten an episode. Ticks_mean 200.0 is consistent with many crushes.
- **No contradiction was found between `lpair.py` code and `run_pilot.py` aggregation** on the fields that exist in the JSONL: `eval_job` returns episodes + states + diagnostic, `run_pilot.py` sums episodes into a job record and saves the diagnostic, discarding states and per-episode breakdowns. That discarding is the *cause* of the diagnostic gap, not a contradiction in the records.
