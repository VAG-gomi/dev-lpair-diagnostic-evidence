**What this revision changes, in one paragraph:**
REVISION-01 left six defects that this revision corrects. First, its step-pattern table contained two labels for one condition (P-BOTH was logically identical to P-HEAD), so the partition was not a partition at all; REVISION-02 removes P-BOTH and replaces the table with a priority-ordered, provably mutually exclusive and exhaustive classification. Second, REVISION-01 still treated expected environment events (crush, blocked, late arrival, looping) as if observing them were evidence of execution failure, even though its own reference transition function already *predicts* those events under the specified rules; REVISION-02 separates the observed event from a conformance mismatch, and reserves "execution failure" for the mismatch only. Third, REVISION-01 reported patterns pooled across whole episodes and required only a frequency threshold for job-level claims, so patterns that occurred *after* the episode was already lost could have been counted as causes; REVISION-02 splits every failed episode at its earliest inevitability point, excludes post-inevitability patterns from causal denominators, and requires an exact waste-based link between a pattern and the loss of recoverability, with frequency kept as necessary but not sufficient. Fourth, REVISION-01's "first_estimates_tick" was a single world tick, which is ambiguous across episode resets; REVISION-02 replaces it with a job-order tuple plus a monotonic job_step_ordinal. Fifth, REVISION-01's P-VAL language allowed aggregate matching to be read as proving trajectory identity; REVISION-02 separates aggregate consistency, model/configuration provenance, deterministic reproduction, and original-trajectory identity, and labels any new step data a reconstruction/replay, never the original trajectory. Sixth, REVISION-01 counted door-cell occupancy without distinguishing before- from after-occupancy, and did not state that a crush-forced step has no world-computed movement target; REVISION-02 defines both occupancies with their own ticks/phases and marks crush-step targets NOT_APPLICABLE, with any hypothetical target under a distinct field name.

**Evidence labels, as in the original and REVISION-01:** OBSERVED, INFERENCE, HYPOTHESIS, PROPOSAL, UNKNOWN. New definitions in this revision are PROPOSAL until the owner approves them. Code-derived facts are OBSERVED from the code (world step order re-checked for this revision, see §6.3). No DP value, no trajectory, and no threshold is computed or invented in this revision.

## 2. Revised step-pattern partition — P-BOTH removed, partition proved (supersedes REV-01 §3.5 in full, and REV-01 §3.4 K-class definitions as refined in §5)

### 2.1 Eligible populations and excluded states — stated before any pattern is assigned

A step-pattern classification is valid only for a stated population. REVISION-02 defines two populations and three excluded classes. A step belongs to exactly one of the following, before any pattern label is assigned:

- **Population F (full-Q population):** steps for which (a) the episode has not terminated before the step (position_before is not the goal — see terminal note below), (b) the step is not crush-forced (position_before ∉ D, or door_open_pre is true), (c) the reference value V(s) is finite (reference computable), and (d) full head Q is recorded (selector Q + all three heads' 5 Q-values at the step's encode). For Population F, the SEL/HEAD split in §2.3 is available.
- **Population Q (Q-missing population):** steps satisfying (a)–(c) but **not** (d) (selected-head Q only, or no Q). For Population Q, the reference outcome (OPTIMAL/SUBOPTIMAL) and the K classes are available, but the SEL/HEAD split is **not** available (suitable_act_set cannot be computed). Population Q steps must never be assigned SEL or HEAD; they receive SUBOPT-UNCLASS instead (§2.4).
- **Excluded class CRUSH-ND (crush-forced steps):** position_before ∈ D and door_open_pre is false. The world ignores the action at such a step (§6.3, code order). Such steps are counted separately (n_CRUSH-ND), excluded from all Dec/K denominators, and contribute zero waste (§4.1, crush-waste theorem); any causal role of a crush event lies in the preceding placement steps (entry into D, stays in D at open phases), whose waste is counted in their own pattern classes (§4.2), not through a pattern label at the crush step itself.
- **Excluded class TERMINAL (goal-terminal steps):** a step whose position_before is the goal. **Such steps do not occur under the pilot protocol**, because an episode terminates immediately after the step that reaches the goal, so no subsequent step starts at the goal. If a future record contains a TERMINAL step, that record is a protocol deviation (see §3.2), not a diagnostic step, and its classification is UNKNOWN, not OPT/SEL/HEAD. The goal step itself (the step whose position_after is the goal) **is** an ordinary eligible step: its position_before is not the goal, and it is classified by §2.3 like any other step (it will be OPTIMAL if its action is in R(s), which it is whenever the goal step is on a shortest path — the usual case for a reaching step, but not assumed; it must be checked, not asserted).
- **Excluded class UNREACH (reference-uncomputable steps):** V(s) = ∞ for the step's state (possible only in future worlds with obstacles; not expected in the pilot's W10/W20 worlds, where V is finite — INFERENCE from REVISION-01 §3.2, to be verified by the reference computation before use). Such steps receive no Dec/K label; their classification is UNREACH, and they are excluded from Dec/K denominators. If UNREACH steps occur in a pilot-world collection, that indicates a reference-computation error, and the collection's reference version must be investigated before any Dec/K claim is made.

No step may carry two population/exclusion labels. A step that is both crush-forced and Q-missing is CRUSH-ND (crush exclusion takes precedence, because the action is ignored regardless of Q availability). A step that is both TERMINAL and crush-forced cannot occur (the goal is not a door cell in the pilot worlds); if it occurred in a future world, it would be TERMINAL (protocol deviation) and reported as such.

### 2.2 Definitions used by the partition (unchanged from REVISION-01, except where §5 refines K terms)

- **s = (position_before, phase)** with phase = t_pre mod 20, as in REVISION-01 §3.2.
- **R(s)** = time-expanded optimal-action set from REVISION-01 §3.2. **taken_action** = the taken act's head argmax action at the step's encode. **OPTIMAL** iff taken_action ∈ R(s); **SUBOPTIMAL** iff taken_action ∉ R(s) (and the step is not crush-forced, terminal, or unreachable).
- **suitable_act_set** = {act ∈ {ACT-0, ACT-1, ACT-2} : that act's head argmax action at the step's encode ∈ R(s)}. Requires full head Q (Population F only). **taken_act** = selector argmax at the step's encode.
- **Timing-critical step** = position_before ∈ {E, D} OR the taken action is a move into D or out of D (REVISION-01 §3.4, retained). Note: a D step at a closed phase is crush-forced and therefore excluded (CRUSH-ND), not timing-critical for pattern purposes; a D step at an open phase is timing-critical (exit decision).
- **K attributes (recorded on every eligible step, whether or not they determine its class):** K_rec (0/1 at encode), K-mismatch flag (K_rec = 1 AND (phase_rec ≠ true_phase OR remaining_rec ≠ true_remaining), exact comparison, REVISION-01 §3.4), K-correct flag (K_rec = 1 AND no mismatch), and ordinal relation to first estimates (before / at-or-after / never, §5). Attributes are **not** classes: a step can carry a K-mismatch attribute and still be class OPT (if its action was optimal despite the mismatch), and a step can carry a K_rec = 0 attribute on a non-timing-critical step and still be class SEL or HEAD (schedule is not timing-critical there, so K status does not suspend attribution — see §2.3, step 2–5 scope note).

### 2.3 Priority-ordered classification for Population F (the partition)

For each Population F step, apply the following tests **in order**, and assign the **first** class whose condition holds. The order is part of the definition: changing the order changes the classification, so any future analysis must state that it used this order.

1. **OPT** — if taken_action ∈ R(s). *Interpretation:* no Dec/K error at this step, regardless of Q or K attributes. (K-mismatch, if present, is recorded as an attribute, not as a K3 error at this step, because the step's action was suitable despite the mismatch.)
2. **K3** — else (SUBOPTIMAL) AND timing-critical AND K-mismatch holds. *Interpretation (SVA):* schedule-knowledge candidate, preceding (REVISION-01 §3.4 precedence retained). Value/selector attribution is suspended for this step. *Attribute:* if the step's ordinal is before first_estimates_ordinal (partial-knowledge period, §5), record sub-attribute K3-PARTIAL; otherwise K3-FULL. K3-PARTIAL and K3-FULL are sub-counts of K3, reported separately (§5.4); they are not separate classes for the partition identity in §2.5 (n_K3 = n_K3-PARTIAL + n_K3-FULL).
3. **KBLANK** — else AND timing-critical AND K_rec = 0 AND first estimates exist for the job AND the step's ordinal < first_estimates_ordinal. *Interpretation:* protocol (blank-start) step, not a failure. Excluded from K3 denominators and from policy-error denominators for causal purposes (its waste, if any, goes to the protocol bucket in §4.3, not to a policy pattern bucket).
4. **KNEVER** — else AND timing-critical AND K_rec = 0 AND first estimates **never** occur in the job (first_estimates = NEVER, §5). *Interpretation:* schedule estimates never became available in this job (trajectory consequence — the agent never observed the conditions for full estimates — not a schedule-knowledge error at this step in the same sense as KUNK). Reported separately from KBLANK; excluded from K3 denominators.
5. **KUNK** — else AND timing-critical AND K_rec = 0 AND first estimates exist AND the step's ordinal ≥ first_estimates_ordinal. *Interpretation:* schedule unknown at a step where estimates had previously become available (estimates lost, contradicted, or otherwise unavailable). Attribution suspended for this step; its waste goes to the unattributed bucket in §4.3.
6. **HEAD** — else AND suitable_act_set is empty. *Interpretation (SVA):* K2 (head-value) step error — no head's action was suitable, so no selector choice could have succeeded at this step. (Original Dec2, reclassified as K2 in REVISION-01, retained.) Note that SUBOPTIMAL follows from suitable_act_set being empty (the taken action cannot be in R(s) if no head's action is), so no separate "taken action unsuitable" condition is needed.
7. **SEL** — else (i.e. suitable_act_set is non-empty). *Interpretation (SVA):* Dec1 (selector-ranking) step error — the selector chose an act whose head action is unsuitable, while a suitable act existed. Note that taken_act ∉ suitable_act_set follows from the earlier steps (see Lemma 2, §2.6), so it need not — and must not, to avoid redundant conditions — be listed as a separate conjunct that could drift from the proof.

**Scope note on steps 2–5:** K precedence applies **only** to timing-critical steps. A non-timing-critical SUBOPTIMAL step with K-mismatch, K_rec = 0 before first estimates, or K_rec = 0 after first estimates falls through steps 2–5 (their timing-critical condition fails) to step 6 or 7 (HEAD or SEL). Its K status remains recorded as an attribute, but it does not suspend value/selector attribution, because the step's outcome does not depend on schedule timing in the direct sense defined in REVISION-01 §3.4. Any analysis that applies K precedence to non-timing-critical steps is using a different partition and must state so.

**L-SHARED interpretation (partition identical, mechanism interpretation differs — REVISION-01 rule retained):**
The same seven classes are *computed* for Shared (selector Q = first 3 outputs, head Q = last 15 outputs reshaped to 3×5), but SEL and HEAD are labelled **SEL-PATTERN** and **HEAD-PATTERN** (descriptive only), not Dec1/K2 as separate mechanisms, because selector and heads share a hidden representation. OPT, K3, KBLANK, KNEVER, KUNK, and CRUSH-ND retain their meanings for Shared (OPT is model-independent; K classes concern input features, not network separation). Any Shared report that presents SEL-PATTERN counts as a Dec1 mechanism claim, or HEAD-PATTERN counts as a K2 mechanism claim, is invalid under this revision.

**Dec3 (descriptive coordination — retained, clarified):**
Dec3 at a step = selector argmax ≠ argmax over heads of (max action value per head). Dec3 is an **attribute**, not a class: it may be true or false on steps of any class above. Dec3 coincides with SEL on some steps and with other classes on others; it becomes causally relevant **only** when it coincides with SEL, in which case the step is reported as SEL with Dec3 noted as a correlate, not as a separate cause. Dec3 without SEL (for example, on an OPT step, or on a HEAD step where no head is suitable) is **not** a decision error. Dec3 must never appear as a row in the partition identity in §2.5 (it would double-count steps).

### 2.4 Classification for Population Q (Q-missing steps)

For each Population Q step, apply steps 1–5 of §2.3 unchanged (OPT, K3, KBLANK, KNEVER, KUNK — none of these requires full Q; K3 requires only K features + reference + timing, and OPT requires only taken_action + reference). For the remaining steps (SUBOPTIMAL, not K-precedence), assign:

- **SUBOPT-UNCLASS** — SUBOPTIMAL, not K-precedence, suitable_act_set unknown (full Q missing). *Interpretation:* a policy error occurred at this step (action unsuitable by the reference), but whether it was a selector error, a head-value error, or both **cannot be determined** from the available data. It must not be split by guesswork, by selected-head Q alone (selected-head Q shows only the taken head, not the counterfactual heads), or by the taken act's identity.

Population Q steps must never be pooled with Population F SEL/HEAD counts. Aggregation reports population F and population Q **separately**, with separate denominators (§2.5). A job-level Dec1/K2 claim requires Population F data in the claimed region; Population Q data alone cannot support it, regardless of how many SUBOPT-UNCLASS steps occur.

### 2.5 Aggregation fields reconciled to the new partition (supersedes REV-01 §4.4 aggregation fields)

For each region (E/D, W, START, GOAL-SIDE, as in REVISION-01 §4.4) and each segment (pre / at / post, §4; descriptive pooled, §4.6), report:

- **Population F:** n_F = n_OPT + n_K3 + n_KBLANK + n_KNEVER + n_KUNK + n_HEAD + n_SEL, with n_K3 = n_K3-PARTIAL + n_K3-FULL shown as a sub-split. Every Population F step in the region/segment is counted exactly once in this identity. If the identity fails, the aggregation is invalid and must be recomputed, not adjusted by an "other" bucket.
- **Population Q:** n_Q = n_OPT-Q + n_K3-Q + n_KBLANK-Q + n_KNEVER-Q + n_KUNK-Q + n_SUBOPT-UNCLASS, where the -Q suffix denotes Population Q counts (same class definitions, Q-missing population). Population Q OPT/K classes are counted in n_Q, **not** in n_F. A combined "n_OPT (all populations)" may be reported descriptively only if its F/Q composition is shown.
- **Excluded counts (reported separately, never in n_F or n_Q):** n_CRUSH-ND, n_UNREACH, n_TERMINAL-DEV (protocol-deviation steps, §3.2), n_NONCONF (non-conformant steps, §3.2 — note: a step can be both non-conformant and otherwise classifiable by reference/Q, but for causal aggregation it is removed to the non-conformant bucket and its waste is undefined, §4.3; its reference/Q class may still be shown descriptively with a NONCONF flag, never counted in n_F/n_Q causal denominators).
- **Total steps check:** total steps in region/segment = n_F + n_Q + n_CRUSH-ND + n_UNREACH + n_TERMINAL-DEV (with non-conformant steps counted in their descriptive class bucket for the total check only, and flagged — see §8.2 for the exact total identity, which must be stated in any future aggregation report so that the check can be audited).

**Deleted fields:** n_P-BOTH, and any "P-BOTH" row, column, or exclusion list, are deleted. REV-01 §4.4(d) ("for Dec1, P-HEAD and P-BOTH must not account for the same steps") is superseded by mutual exclusivity itself: no step carries two classes, so no exclusion list is needed or permitted as a substitute for the partition proof.

### 2.6 Proof of mutual exclusivity and exhaustiveness (for Population F; Population Q follows by the same argument with SUBOPT-UNCLASS replacing HEAD/SEL)

- **Lemma 1 (reference dichotomy):** For any eligible step, taken_action ∈ R(s) or taken_action ∉ R(s), exclusively (R(s) is a set; membership is a dichotomy). Step 1 (OPT) therefore captures exactly the optimal steps, and steps 2–7 capture exactly the suboptimal steps. No step is both, and no eligible step is neither.
- **Lemma 2 (suitable-set entailment):** If suitable_act_set is non-empty and taken_act ∈ suitable_act_set, then taken_action ∈ R(s) (by definition of suitable_act_set: the taken act's head action is in R(s)). Contrapositive: if taken_action ∉ R(s) (SUBOPTIMAL) and suitable_act_set is non-empty, then taken_act ∉ suitable_act_set. Therefore step 7 (SEL) needs no separate "taken_act ∉ suitable_act_set" condition — it follows — and step 7 cannot overlap step 1. If suitable_act_set is empty, taken_action ∉ R(s) follows immediately (no head's action is in R(s), including the taken head's), so step 6 (HEAD) steps are necessarily suboptimal and cannot overlap step 1.
- **Lemma 3 (K-precedence partition):** For a suboptimal timing-critical step, exactly one of the following holds: (a) K_rec = 1 and mismatch (step 2); (b) K_rec = 1 and no mismatch (falls through to steps 6–7); (c) K_rec = 0 and first estimates exist and ordinal < first_ordinal (step 3); (d) K_rec = 0 and first estimates never occur (step 4); (e) K_rec = 0 and first estimates exist and ordinal ≥ first_ordinal (step 5). Cases (a), (c), (d), (e) are mutually exclusive by (K_rec, mismatch, first-existence, ordinal comparison), and case (b) is the only K case that falls through. For a suboptimal non-timing-critical step, steps 2–5 do not apply (timing condition fails), so it falls through to steps 6–7 regardless of K status.
- **Theorem (partition):** Every Population F step receives exactly one class from {OPT, K3, KBLANK, KNEVER, KUNK, HEAD, SEL}, by Lemma 1 (step 1 vs rest), Lemma 3 (steps 2–5 partition the timing-critical suboptimal K cases, and non-timing-critical steps fall through), and Lemma 2 + suitable-set dichotomy (steps 6–7 partition the remainder by suitable_act_set empty/non-empty). The identity in §2.5 is therefore an identity, not an approximation: any violation indicates a classification or counting error, not a modelling choice.

## 3. Transition conformance and the redefinition of execution (supersedes REV-01 §4.3 transition-type use for execution claims, and REV-01 §6 Ex1–Ex5 rows)

### 3.1 Specified transition T_spec(s, a) — the conformance baseline (PROPOSAL, derived from REVISION-01 §3.2/§4.1)

For a step with state s = (position_before, phase), action a, and (for the reached field) the pilot protocol's episode-termination rule (episode ends on reaching the goal, so no step starts on the goal — see §2.1 terminal note), define T_spec(s, a) by the coded transition rules exactly as in REVISION-01 §3.2:

- **open_spec** = (phase < 10). **crush_spec** = (position_before ∈ D AND NOT open_spec).
- If crush_spec: **pos_after_spec** = (0, 0) [start, by reset]; **blocked_spec** = False (no blocked event is logged on a crush step — the world returns before movement); **reached_spec** = False; **tick_next_spec** = t_pre + 1.
- Else: **target_spec** = clip(position_before + delta(a), grid bounds); **blocked_spec** = (is_blocked_cell(target_spec) OR (target_spec ∈ D AND NOT open_spec)) AND (target_spec ≠ position_before); **pos_after_spec** = position_before if blocked_spec else target_spec; **reached_spec** = (pos_after_spec == goal); **tick_next_spec** = t_pre + 1.
- **door_open_spec** = open_spec. (The world's door schedule is the true schedule by definition in the pilot; a recorded door_open_pre that differs from open_spec is itself a conformance mismatch, §3.2.)

T_spec is a function of (position_before, phase, action) and the world's geometry only. It does **not** use the model's Q, the taken act, or any later trajectory data. It is the same function that defines R(s) in REVISION-01 §3.2 (V is computed from T_spec), so conformance and reference are coherent by construction: a step cannot be both conformant and reference-incoherent in its transition fields, because both use the same T_spec.

### 3.2 Conformance check — the only execution-failure test at step level (PROPOSAL)

For each recorded step, compare the observed fields to T_spec(s, a_taken), where a_taken is the recorded taken action:

| Observed field | Specified counterpart | Match condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| position_after (R-POS-AFTER) | pos_after_spec | Equal as grid cells. |
| crushed (R-CRUSH) | crush_spec | Equal as booleans. |
| blocked_log (R-TRANS blocked flag) | blocked_spec | Equal as booleans. Note: blocked is the world's logged event, not position equality (REVISION-01 §4.3, retained). |
| reached (R-REACHED) | reached_spec | Equal as booleans. |
| Next step's t_pre (or episode end) | tick_next_spec | Next step's t_pre = t_pre + 1; for the episode's last step, no next step is required, but the episode must end by the protocol in §3.2 (goal or 200 steps). |
| door_open_pre (R-DOOR-PRE) | door_open_spec | Equal as booleans. A mismatch here indicates the recorded door observation does not match the true schedule for that tick/phase — a recording or world-schedule deviation, to be investigated, not a policy error. |

- **CONFORMANT step:** all compared fields match. The observed transition is the specified transition for the action taken. No execution-failure inference may be drawn from this step's transition, however unfortunate its outcome (crush, blocked, cap) — the outcome was the specified consequence of (state, action) under the rules.
- **NON-CONFORMANT step (Ex-DEV step):** any compared field mismatches. Record the step as Ex-DEV with the **list of mismatching fields** (e.g. "pos_after mismatch: observed (5,2), specified (4,2)"; "crush mismatch: observed True, specified False"). A step with a mismatch in one field but matches elsewhere is still NON-CONFORMANT; partial matching does not rescue it. Its policy class (OPT/SEL/HEAD/…) may still be computed descriptively from recorded Q/reference (with a NONCONF flag), but it is **excluded from causal denominators and from waste accounting** (§4.3), because its actual next state is not the specified next state, so V(next_actual) and waste are not defined by the reference.
- **Episode/job protocol conformance (separate from step transition conformance):** an episode is protocol-conformant iff (a) its first step's position_before = (0, 0); (b) its start_phase S ∈ 0..19 and every step's t_pre = S + s; (c) its length is ≤ 200 steps, it ends after a reached step or at exactly 200 steps, and it does not continue after a reached step; (d) episodes in a job occur in the stated order with the stated start_phases (pilot eval: 0..19 in order). A job is protocol-conformant iff all its episodes are. Any violation is a **PROTOCOL-DEVIATION** at episode/job level: affected episodes' policy claims are INCONCLUSIVE (not merely their deviation steps), because start/tick/ordering errors undermine the reference and K assignments for the whole episode. Protocol deviations are Ex-DEV evidence at job level, even if every individual step transition happens to match T_spec for its recorded (possibly wrong) tick.

**Limit of the conformance check (stated explicitly, to prevent overclaiming):**
T_spec is derived from the coded transition rules (`Exp1World.step`, OBSERVED). A conformance pass therefore checks **recording + episode handling against the coded rules**; it does **not** prove that the coded rules match an independent written specification document, because no separate written transition specification exists for the pilot beyond the code. A world-implementation deviation (code differing from an intended written spec) is **UNKNOWN** from conformance alone and would require a separate written spec to compare against. Conversely, a conformance failure does not by itself prove the world is buggy: it may indicate a recording error, a schema error, or an episode-handling error in the collection code. The failure must be investigated and its source stated before any "world bug" or "execution bug" label is used.

### 3.3 Redefined Ex taxonomy — events, not execution failures (supersedes REV-01 §6 Ex1–Ex5 rows)

| Old label (REV-01) | New status under REVISION-02 | What it shows | What it does NOT show |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ex1 — Crush | **Ex1-EVENT (observed crush event).** Count crush steps, with position_before, phase, door_open_pre, and preceding placement chain (§4.2). | That a crush occurred under the specified rules (crush-forced state) or, if non-conformant, that a crush was logged where T_spec predicts none (Ex-DEV, §3.2). | Execution failure, by itself. A conformant crush is the specified consequence of being in D at a closed tick; its *cause* (if any) lies in the placement chain (policy/schedule on earlier steps), not in the crush step. |

Cap-failure *contribution* (cumulative waste leading to cap-fatal) is handled by §4 (inevitability/waste), not by Ex5. Execution failure only if cap handling deviates (episode > 200 steps, episode ends < 200 without goal, start_phase outside 0..19 — PROTOCOL-DEVIATION, §3.2). |

**Restated crush-exclusion rule (replaces REV-01 §4.4 Ex1 rule and §6 Ex1 row, same content, narrower label):**
Crushes_total = 0 for a job ⇒ Ex1-EVENT-as-sole-cause is NOT_SUPPORTED for that job (no crush events occurred, so a crush cannot be the sole cause). This does **not** exclude Ex-DEV, Ex2-EVENT, Ex3-EVENT, Ex4-EVENT, Ex5-EXCLUSION, or any policy cause. Crushes_total > 0 ⇒ Ex1-EVENT occurred; it does **not** establish Ex-DEV, and it does **not** establish that crush caused the job's failure (see placement chain + inevitability, §4.2).

### 3.4 Alternative definition Ex-ALT — stated explicitly, not adopted (work-order requirement)

If the owner intends "execution" to include the *expected consequences* of policy choices (i.e. crush/blocked outcomes as "what execution did"), then the alternative definition is:

- **Ex-ALT (outcome taxonomy):** Ex-ALT counts crush, blocked, and cap outcomes as execution *outcomes* (the result of executing the chosen action through the environment), regardless of conformance.
- **Distinguishability limit (why Ex-ALT cannot replace the conformance definition for causal separation):** Under Ex-ALT, a single crush step is simultaneously (a) a policy consequence (the policy placed the agent in D at a closed tick — a K/Dec pattern on earlier steps) and (b) an execution outcome (the crush happened). Counting it in both categories double-counts one event as two causes, and no Ex-ALT count can separate "policy caused the crush" from "execution caused the failure", because Ex-ALT does not compare observed to specified transition. Ex-ALT is therefore usable **only** as an outcome description ("N crush outcomes occurred"), labelled "Ex-ALT (outcome taxonomy, not causal)", and must never be presented as causal separation from knowledge/decision.
- **REVISION-02 position (PROPOSAL, for owner decision):** retain the conformance definition (§3.2) as the primary execution definition for causal purposes, and use Ex-ALT only if the owner explicitly adopts it for descriptive outcome reporting, with the label above. This revision does **not** adopt Ex-ALT, and no future report may use "execution failure" in the Ex-ALT sense without stating that it means outcome, not deviation.

## 4. Inevitability-linked aggregation (supersedes REV-01 §4.4 job-level rules in part, and REV-01 §4.5 as the aggregation link)

### 4.1 Slack and waste — exact quantities from V (definitions only; V is not computed in this revision)

For a conformant episode (all steps CONFORMANT, episode protocol-conformant, §3.2) with start_phase S and local steps s = 0, 1, …:

- **V_start** = V((0, 0), S mod 20) — the reference value of the episode's start state. (Start position is (0, 0) by protocol; if an episode's first position_before ≠ (0, 0), the episode is a protocol deviation, §3.2, and slack/waste are undefined for it.)
- **slack_start** = 200 − V_start. This is the number of wasted steps the episode can absorb before the goal becomes unreachable within the cap. If V_start > 200, slack_start < 0 and the episode is impossible from the start (Ex5-EXCLUSION, §3.3); its inevitability point is START, not a step, and its waste accounting does not apply (no step can be blamed for an already-impossible start).
- **Waste of a non-crush step s:** waste(s) = V(next_actual(s)) − V(s) + 1, where next_actual(s) = (position_after, phase_next) with phase_next = t_post mod 20, and V(goal, ·) = 0. *Identities (exact, from the Bellman definition):* if step s is OPTIMAL, V(next_actual) = V(s) − 1 (for non-goal next states; 0 for goal next states with V(s) = 1), so waste(s) = 0. If step s is SUBOPTIMAL, V(next_actual) ≥ V(s) (otherwise V(next_actual) + 1 = V(s) would make it optimal), so waste(s) ≥ 1. Waste is therefore an exact, threshold-free measure of how much slack a step consumed: optimal steps consume none, suboptimal steps consume at least one, and larger waste means the step moved the agent further from the goal (in V terms) or wasted more time.
- **Waste of a crush step s — crush-waste theorem (exact, from the Bellman definition):** waste_crush(s) = 0, always, for a conformant crush step. Proof: for a crush-forced state s = (D, closed phase), every action gives the same next state (start, phase_next), so V(s) = 1 + V((0, 0), phase_next) by the Bellman equation in REVISION-01 §3.2. Hence waste(s) = V((0, 0), phase_next) − V(s) + 1 = V(start) − (1 + V(start)) + 1 = 0. A crush step therefore consumes one tick but **no slack**: it returns the agent to the start state whose value already includes the cost of the crush step itself. The slack loss associated with a crush event lies entirely in the preceding placement steps (entry into D at a bad phase, stays in D that waste open ticks), whose waste is counted in their own pattern classes. Crush steps are consequently slack-neutral and can never be the earliest inevitability step f (§4.2) — a correction to REVISION-01 §4.5, which allowed for a "cap-fatal crush step" and a traceback to a placement step; under exact V arithmetic, no traceback is needed for waste accounting, because the crush step's waste is zero and the placement steps already carry the loss.
- **Slack evolution:** slack_after(s) = slack_before(s) − waste(s) (with waste(s) as defined above; crush waste = 0 by §4.1). Optimal steps preserve slack; suboptimal steps reduce it; crush steps preserve it. Slack_after(s) < 0 is exactly the condition V(next_actual) > remaining_after(s) (since remaining_after = 200 − (s + 1) and slack_after = remaining_after − V(next_actual)). Cap-fatality and slack-negativity are therefore the same condition, stated two ways.

### 4.2 Earliest inevitability point f, attributable step, and placement chain (supersedes REV-01 §4.5 inevitability definition in part)

For a failed (cap, reached = False) conformant episode with slack_start ≥ 0:

- **f (earliest inevitability step index)** = the smallest s such that slack_after(s) < 0, i.e. V(next_actual(s)) > 200 − (s + 1). *Existence (INFERENCE from the definitions, to be checked by any future aggregation code):* the episode runs 200 steps without reaching the goal; after the last step (s = 199), remaining_after = 0 and V(next_actual) ≥ 1 (agent not at goal), so slack_after(199) ≤ −1; slack starts ≥ 0 and decreases only on suboptimal steps (crush steps have waste 0, §4.1), so a first negative step exists and f ≤ 199. If no such s is found by aggregation code, the code's V, next_actual, or slack computation is wrong, and the episode's diagnostic is UNKNOWN (not a policy claim).
- **f is never a crush step (theorem, from §4.1):** because waste_crush = 0, a crush step preserves slack (slack_after = slack_before), so it cannot be the first step with slack_after < 0. This corrects REVISION-01 §4.5, which allowed for a "cap-fatal crush step" with a traceback to a placement step. Under exact V arithmetic, no such traceback is needed for waste accounting: if a crush follows a loss of recoverability, the loss occurred at an earlier non-crush step (the placement or stay step whose waste pushed slack negative, possibly several steps before the crush); if a crush does not follow a loss, slack is preserved across it and the episode remains recoverable (from the start state) after it. The attributable step is therefore simply **a = f**, always a non-crush step.
- **Placement chain (descriptive trace for crush events, not a waste bucket):** for any crush step c in an episode, the placement chain is the sequence from the most recent step before c that moved the agent into D (entry step: position_before = E, position_after = D), through any intervening D-occupancy steps at open phases, to c itself. The chain is reported descriptively (entry step, D steps with their pattern classes, crush step) so that a crush event can be traced to the steps that placed and kept the agent in D. For waste accounting, chain steps are ordinary eligible steps: their waste is counted in their own pattern classes (§4.3), the crush step contributes waste 0, and no separate chain bucket exists. If no entry step exists before a crush step in the episode (which should not occur, since the agent starts at (0, 0) and can only enter D by moving from E), the crush is reported as unplaceable and the episode flagged for protocol/recording investigation — its causal claims are INCONCLUSIVE until resolved.
- **Segments for reporting (per failed episode):**
  - **Pre:** steps s < f (eligible steps only; CRUSH-ND steps before f are counted in n_CRUSH-ND, not in pre eligible counts, and contribute waste 0). Pre steps are candidate contributors: their waste accumulated to bring slack down to the point where f's waste pushed it negative.
  - **At:** step f alone. At is the trigger step: the non-crush step that directly caused slack to become negative. Its pattern class is the trigger class for the episode.
  - **Post:** steps s > f. Post steps occur **after** the goal was already unreachable within the cap. They are reported descriptively only (counts, patterns, waste computed but labelled post-waste, never added to causal sums).
- **Successful episodes:** f is undefined (no inevitability point; the episode reached the goal). Pre/at/post segmentation does not apply. Successful episodes are **excluded** from failure-causal denominators (they did not fail, so their patterns cannot explain a failure). They may be reported descriptively as a comparison (e.g. "successful episodes had X pattern counts"), labelled as comparison, not as causal denominators.
- **Earliest deviation (retained, repositioned):** earliest deviation = first SUBOPTIMAL non-crush step in the episode (REVISION-01 §4.5, retained). It is reported alongside f, but it is **not** a causal claim by itself: a deviation with waste 1 on an episode with slack_start 150 does not explain the failure; only the waste accounting in §4.3 can link deviations to loss. Earliest deviation ≤ f always (f is itself a suboptimal non-crush step, so the first suboptimal non-crush step cannot be after f); if a future report finds earliest deviation > f, that is an aggregation error, not a diagnostic finding.

### 4.3 Causal-link condition — waste accounting (PROPOSAL; exact, no threshold invented)

For a stated pattern class P (one of SEL, HEAD, K3, or a stated set of classes for a joint claim — see below), a stated region, and a stated scope (a set of failed episodes, e.g. all failed episodes of a job, or a stated subset with the remainder reported separately), compute per episode ep in scope:

- waste_P(ep) = sum of waste(s) over steps s in ep's **pre segment** with class P, **plus** waste of the at step if the at step's class is P. (Crush steps contribute waste 0 by §4.1, so they add nothing to any waste_P sum; placement-chain steps that fall in pre/at are ordinary eligible steps counted in their own classes — no separate chain allocation is needed or permitted.)
- waste_protocol(ep) = sum of waste(s) over pre/at steps with class KBLANK or KNEVER (protocol buckets).
- waste_unattributed(ep) = sum of waste(s) over pre/at steps with class KUNK or SUBOPT-UNCLASS (attribution suspended or unavailable).
- waste_other(ep) = sum of waste(s) over pre/at steps with classes other than P (and other than protocol/unattributed, to avoid double-counting).
- slack_start(ep) as in §4.1.

**Crush steps in waste sums:** crush steps contribute waste 0 (§4.1) and are never assigned to a pattern waste bucket. Placement-chain steps (entry into D, D-occupancy steps at open phases) are eligible steps whose waste is counted in their own pattern classes if they fall in pre/at segments. There is no chain waste bucket and no chain allocation rule in this revision; any report that allocates crush waste to a pattern class is using a superseded rule and must be corrected (its crush waste is zero, so the allocation changes nothing numerically, but the labelling is wrong and must not be presented as an allocation choice).

**Sole-cause claim for pattern P (episode-level, then job-level):**
- *Episode-level sole:* waste_P(ep) ≥ slack_start(ep) + 1. Rationale (exact): P's waste alone exceeds the episode's total slack budget, so even without any other pattern's waste, the episode would have lost recoverability by f (waste accumulates monotonically; order does not affect the total). Note: waste_P(ep) here includes pre + at waste for P, as defined above.
- *Job-level sole:* episode-level sole holds for **every** failed episode in the stated scope. If it holds for some but not all failed episodes, the claim is **per-episode sole (stated subset)**, not job-level sole, and the remaining failed episodes must be reported separately (their causes are not established by this claim).
- *Unattributed blocker:* if, for an episode in scope, waste_unattributed(ep) ≥ slack_start(ep) + 1 and waste_P(ep) < slack_start(ep) + 1, then that episode's cause is INCONCLUSIVE (unattributed waste alone could explain the loss, so P cannot be claimed as the episode's sole cause even if P contributed). If waste_P(ep) ≥ slack_start(ep) + 1, the blocker does not apply to sole-sufficiency (P alone suffices regardless of unattributed waste), but unattributed waste must still be reported alongside, because it means the episode had multiple sufficient waste sources — the claim is "P sufficient", not "P the only contributor".

**Joint-cause claim for a stated pattern set {P, Q, …} (episode-level, then job-level):**
- *Episode-level joint:* (waste_P(ep) + waste_Q(ep) + …) ≥ slack_start(ep) + 1, AND no single pattern in the set is episode-level sole (otherwise the claim is sole, not joint — report the stronger claim), AND waste_unattributed(ep) < slack_start(ep) + 1 (otherwise unattributed alone could explain it and the joint claim is INCONCLUSIVE for that episode).
- *Job-level joint:* episode-level joint holds for every failed episode in scope, with the **same** pattern set (a set that changes per episode is not a job-level joint claim; it is a per-episode report).

**What waste accounting does NOT do:**
- It does not prove that pattern P *intended* or *mechanistically caused* a specific step's waste in a deeper sense than "steps of class P consumed enough slack to account for the loss". Waste is a V-arithmetic accounting, not a mechanistic intervention. A sole-P waste claim must therefore be labelled "P waste-sufficient for loss of recoverability (accounting claim)", not "P proved as the unique mechanistic cause", unless an intervention (forced-selector counterfactual or similar experiment, not authorised here) independently confirms it.
- It does not replace the frequency condition (§4.4): a pattern could be waste-sufficient in one episode (e.g. a single catastrophic step with huge waste — entering D at a fatal phase from a high-V state, or a long wandering sequence counted as one pattern) yet be rare across episodes. Waste-sufficiency + frequency together are required for a job-level causal claim; either alone is insufficient (see §4.4).
- It does not apply to non-conformant episodes (waste undefined, §3.2), to episodes with slack_start < 0 (impossible from start, §4.1), or to successful episodes (no f). Such episodes are reported as INCONCLUSIVE / N/A for causal purposes, not pooled into waste sums.

### 4.4 Frequency condition — necessary, not sufficient (supersedes REV-01 §4.4 job SUPPORTED conditions (b)–(c) as the *sole* causal requirement)

A job-level causal claim for pattern P (or pattern set, for joint claims) requires **all** of:

1. **Region and scope stated** (region from REVISION-01 §4.4; scope = failed episodes included, with successful episodes and deviation episodes listed separately and excluded from causal denominators).
2. **Pre-inevitability denominator stated:** n_pre = number of eligible steps (Population F, or Population Q where applicable — stated separately) in the region across failed episodes in scope, **pre segment only** (at steps reported separately as trigger counts, post steps excluded). Causal proportions are count_P_pre / n_pre. Trigger proportion (fraction of failed episodes whose at class is P) is reported separately and is **not** a causal proportion.
3. **Frequency threshold met:** count_P_pre / n_pre ≥ T, where T is a threshold **set by the owner before collection** (still unset — this revision does not set it, and no value may be chosen after seeing the data), AND episode spread ≥ Y (pattern P present in pre segments of at least Y failed episodes, Y also owner-set before collection, still unset). Until T and Y are set, condition 3 cannot be met, and any job-level causal claim is INCONCLUSIVE on frequency grounds, even if the waste condition in §4.3 is met.
4. **Waste-link condition met:** §4.3 sole or joint condition holds for the stated scope (per-episode, for every failed episode in scope).
5. **No blocker:** no failed episode in scope is INCONCLUSIVE by the unattributed blocker in §4.3 (for sole claims) or the joint blocker (for joint claims), unless that episode is removed from scope with the removal stated and the claim re-scoped to the remaining episodes.
6. **Shared mechanism caveat (if applicable):** for L-SHARED, SEL/HEAD pattern claims remain pattern-only (REVISION-01 rule, retained in §2.3); a Shared job-level causal claim for Dec1/K2 as separate mechanisms is INCONCLUSIVE regardless of frequency and waste, unless an intervention experiment (not authorised here) separates them.

**If any of conditions 1–6 fails, the job-level outcome is INCONCLUSIVE** (not SUPPORTED, not NOT_SUPPORTED, unless an exact exclusion in §4.5 applies). In particular: **frequency alone does not establish causation** — a pattern present in 100% of pre steps but with waste_P < slack + 1 in every episode (e.g. many small-waste deviations that never accumulate to the slack budget, with the loss actually driven by unattributed or protocol waste) is frequent but not waste-sufficient, and its causal claim is INCONCLUSIVE. Conversely, **waste alone does not establish a job-level claim** — a pattern waste-sufficient in one episode but absent in others is an episode-level finding, not a job-level one, until frequency/spread conditions are met.

### 4.5 Revised job-level exact exclusions (supersedes REV-01 §4.4 NOT_SUPPORTED list in part)

The following job-level assignments require **no** numeric threshold (they are exact, like the original crush exclusion):

- **Dec/K NOT_SUPPORTED for a region (failure must be elsewhere):** if, across failed episodes in scope, every pre/at eligible step in the region is OPT (i.e. the region's steps consumed zero waste), then Dec/K patterns in that region cannot explain the failure; the failure's waste must come from other regions, from protocol/unattributed buckets, or from deviation episodes (reported separately). This does **not** by itself give a job-level NOT_SUPPORTED for Dec/K overall, unless all regions satisfy it (see consistency check, §8.3).
- **K3 NOT_SUPPORTED for a region:** if, on timing-critical pre/at steps in the region, every step has K_rec = 1 with phase/remaining matching the true schedule (K-correct), then K3 cannot explain those steps' suboptimality (if any suboptimal steps exist, they are SEL/HEAD, not K3). If no timing-critical steps exist in the region, K3 is N/A for the region, not NOT_SUPPORTED (absence of timing steps is not evidence about schedule knowledge).
- **Ex1-EVENT-as-sole-cause NOT_SUPPORTED:** crushes_total = 0 for the job (REVISION-01 rule, relabelled per §3.3). This does not exclude Ex-DEV, policy causes, or other event patterns.
- **Ex-DEV NOT_SUPPORTED for a job:** if every step in every episode is CONFORMANT and every episode is protocol-conformant (§3.2), then Ex-DEV is NOT_SUPPORTED for that job (no deviation was observed). This is the **only** execution-related NOT_SUPPORTED available from a conformant collection, and it is an exact exclusion (zero mismatches), not a threshold.
- **Job-level INCONCLUSIVE (default):** any job-level causal claim where T/Y are unset, where n_pre = 0 for the stated region, where pre/at/post segments are not reported separately, where non-conformant episodes are pooled into causal denominators, or where the region/scope is not stated.

### 4.6 Descriptive prevalence vs causal reporting — two reports, never merged

Every future diagnostic report must contain **two** aggregation tables, labelled as such, and must not present one as the other:

- **Table D (descriptive prevalence):** pooled across all episodes (failed + successful, with successful flagged), all segments (pre + at + post), all populations shown separately (F/Q), with counts and proportions on the pooled denominator. Table D answers "how often did each pattern occur overall?" It must carry the label: "Descriptive prevalence only — pooled segments include post-inevitability patterns, which cannot have caused the failures they follow. Do not use Table D proportions for causal claims."
- **Table C (causal aggregation):** failed episodes only, conformant episodes only, with counts and proportions on the pre-segment denominator n_pre (§4.4 condition 2), at-segment trigger counts reported separately (not in the causal proportions), waste sums by class per episode (with per-episode sole/joint checks, §4.3), slack_start per episode, f and at class per episode, and frequency vs threshold status (T/Y set or unset). Table C is the **only** table that may support a job-level causal claim, and only if §4.4 conditions 1–6 all hold.

If a report contains only Table D, it contains **no** causal claim, regardless of how high any Table D proportion is. If a report's Table C and Table D proportions differ (they will, whenever post counts are non-zero), both must be shown with the difference explained by post-segment exclusion, not by adjusting one to match the other.

## 5. Estimate-availability provenance — job-order tuple replaces scalar tick (supersedes REV-01 §5 R-EST and REV-01 §3.4 K-UNKNOWN/K-BLANK-START definitions)

### 5.1 Why a scalar tick fails (restated as the defect, for auditability)

The world clock resets at every episode boundary: t_pre(s) = S_episode + s, where S_episode is that episode's start_phase (0..19). ScheduleModel estimates, by contrast, persist across episodes within a job (events/estimates persist; only the pending previous observation is cleared and the span marked gapped at new_episode — REVISION-01 §4.1, OBSERVED). A scalar "first_estimates_tick = 47" therefore does not identify a job position: it could be episode 2 step 27 (S = 20? — impossible, S ≤ 19, so tick 47 in an episode with S = 19 is step 28, while in an episode with S = 0 it is step 47), episode 0 step 47, or any other episode whose S + s = 47. Comparing two steps' world ticks to decide which came first in job order is invalid across episode resets. **Ordering steps by world tick alone is prohibited under this revision.** Job order is defined by (episode_id, local step) or by job_step_ordinal (§5.2), never by tick value.

### 5.2 Replacement fields (supersede R-EST; new fields are Essential, job-level + per-step)

| Field ID | Level | Definition (exact) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R-EST-EP | Job-level | **first_estimates_episode_id**: the episode index (0-based, in job execution order) of the first step at which ScheduleModel first had full estimates, or null if never. "Full estimates" = period, open_len, and last_opening all non-None in ScheduleModel state **after** processing the observe call at that step's encode (see §5.3 note on before/after). |
| R-EST-STEP | Job-level | **first_estimates_local_step**: the local step index s within R-EST-EP at which full estimates first became available (post-observe), or null if never. |
| R-EST-TICK | Job-level | **first_estimates_world_tick**: t_pre of the first-estimates step = S_{R-EST-EP} + R-EST-STEP, or null if never. This tick is recorded for cross-checking (it must equal the step's recorded t_pre), **not** for ordering. Ordering uses R-EST-ORD. |
| R-EST-ORD | Job-level | **first_estimates_ordinal**: job_step_ordinal (§5.2, per-step definition) of the first-estimates step, or null (NEVER) if full estimates never occur in the job. This is the ordering key for K classification. |
| R-ORD | Per-step (Essential) | **job_step_ordinal**: monotonic counter within a job, starting at 0 for the job's first episode's first step, incrementing by 1 per step in job execution order. Formally: ordinal(episode e, local step s) = (sum of actual lengths of episodes 0..e−1 in the job) + s, where actual lengths use the recorded episode lengths (goal episodes may be < 200 steps; cap episodes are 200 steps). Ordinal resets per job (it is not comparable across jobs). Ordinal must be recorded per step row; it must not be derived by assuming 200 steps per episode (that assumption fails for goal episodes and for any future protocol with variable episode lengths). |

**Old field status:** REV-01 field R-EST (scalar first_estimates_tick) is **deleted**. A future record that contains only a scalar first-estimates tick, without R-EST-EP/R-EST-STEP/R-EST-ORD, is **provenance-incomplete for K classification**: its KBLANK/KNEVER/KUNK assignments cannot be trusted across episodes, and any K claim based on it is INCONCLUSIVE until the tuple is supplied (from the job's execution order, not reconstructed from ticks alone — if the tuple cannot be recovered from the collection's own records, it must be labelled UNKNOWN, not inferred from tick values).

### 5.3 K assignment using execution order (supersedes REV-01 §3.4 K-UNKNOWN/K-BLANK-START paragraph)

For a step with ordinal o, first_estimates_ordinal f_ord (or NEVER), K_rec, mismatch flag, and timing-critical flag, the K classification for pattern purposes (i.e. for steps 2–5 of §2.3, which apply only to SUBOPTIMAL timing-critical steps; OPT steps and non-timing-critical steps carry K status as attributes only, §2.2) is:

| Condition (all must hold for the row) | K class (if SUBOPTIMAL + timing-critical) | Otherwise (OPT or non-timing-critical) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| K_rec = 1 AND mismatch | **K3** (with sub-attribute K3-PARTIAL if o < f_ord, K3-FULL if o ≥ f_ord or f_ord = NEVER with K_rec = 1 — see note below) | Attribute: K-mismatch present (not a K3 error at this step if OPT; attribute only if non-timing-critical). |
| K_rec = 1 AND no mismatch | Falls through to HEAD/SEL (K3 NOT_SUPPORTED for this step) | Attribute: K-correct. |
| K_rec = 0 AND f_ord exists AND o < f_ord | **KBLANK** (blank-start protocol) | Attribute: pre-estimates K = 0 (protocol, not a class). |
| K_rec = 0 AND f_ord = NEVER | **KNEVER** (estimates never available in job) | Attribute: never-estimated K = 0. |
| K_rec = 0 AND f_ord exists AND o ≥ f_ord | **KUNK** (unknown despite prior estimates) | Attribute: K unknown after estimates. |

**Notes (each is a definition, not an empirical claim):**
- **Before/after observe at the first-estimates step:** first estimates are defined **post-observe** at that step's encode. The encoder computes K/phase/remaining *after* ScheduleModel.observe(t_pre, door_open_pre) at that step (REVISION-01 §4.1). Therefore the first-estimates step itself may already have K_rec = 1 (if the observe completed the estimates and phase/remaining are both available). Its ordinal is f_ord, and steps with o = f_ord are **not** KBLANK (they are at-or-after first estimates). Steps with o < f_ord are before first estimates. This off-by-one distinction is part of the definition; any implementation that defines first estimates pre-observe must state so and shift the comparison accordingly, with the shift documented in R-CFG.
- **K3-PARTIAL vs K3-FULL:** if o < f_ord and K_rec = 1 with mismatch, the recorded features come from partial knowledge (estimates not yet full — e.g. a cleanly observed opening without a full period/open_len pair, per the ScheduleModel partial-knowledge path). Such a step is class K3 (schedule features were wrong at a timing-critical suboptimal step), but with sub-attribute PARTIAL, reported separately from K3-FULL. A job-level K3 claim that relies **solely** on K3-PARTIAL steps must be labelled "K3-PARTIAL-only (partial-knowledge schedule error)" and must not be presented as a full-knowledge K3 claim. Whether K3-PARTIAL steps count toward a K3 frequency threshold T is an **owner decision** (§8, decision 7) — this revision does not decide it by implication; until decided, K3 frequency must be reported as two numbers (K3-FULL proportion, K3 total proportion) with the threshold status stated for each.
- **Estimates lost after first appearance:** f_ord is the *first* appearance only; it is not recomputed if estimates are later lost (contradiction, reset, or otherwise). Later K_rec = 0 steps (o ≥ f_ord) are KUNK, not a new KBLANK. If a future ScheduleModel version re-establishes estimates after loss, the *first* tuple remains the provenance anchor; re-establishment may be recorded as an additional job-level field (R-EST-RE, optional, PROPOSAL) but must not overwrite R-EST-EP/STEP/ORD.
- **Never-estimated jobs:** if f_ord = NEVER, then *every* K_rec = 0 step in the job is KNEVER (for timing-critical suboptimal steps) or never-estimated attribute (otherwise). KNEVER must not be merged into KBLANK in aggregation: KBLANK implies estimates *did* appear later (so early blankness is a transient protocol phase), while KNEVER implies the job never achieved full estimates at all (so schedule ignorance is a job-level trajectory property, not a transient phase). Merging them would hide the difference between "agent started blank but later learned the schedule" and "agent never learned the schedule".

### 5.4 Aggregation update for K classes (consequential)

REVISION-01 aggregation fields n_P-K3 and n_P-KUNK are replaced by n_K3 (split n_K3-PARTIAL + n_K3-FULL), n_KBLANK, n_KNEVER, n_KUNK, each split by population (F/Q) and by segment (pre/at/post) as in §2.5/§4.6. A K report that contains only "K-unknown" without distinguishing BLANK/NEVER/UNK, or only a scalar first-estimates tick without the tuple in §5.2, is incomplete under this revision.

## 6. Time-indexed occupancy and movement targets (supersedes REV-01 §2.2 L3 definition in part, REV-01 §§4.1–4.3 position/target language in part, and REV-01 §5 R-POS/R-TARGET fields in part)

### 6.1 Before-occupancy vs after-occupancy — two counts, two timestamps, one union

For a step s in an episode with start_phase S, local step s, t_pre = S + s, t_post = t_pre + 1, phase_pre = t_pre mod 20, phase_next = t_post mod 20:

- **Before-occupancy:** the agent occupies position_before at tick t_pre (the encode tick). A step *before-occupies* cell C iff position_before = C. Its timestamp is **(t_pre, phase_pre)** and its job-order key is the step's ordinal o (the step's own ordinal). Before-occupancy is the occupancy relevant to *decisions* at step s (the agent's state when it chose its action) and to crush/blocked evaluation at step s (crush and blocked are evaluated on position_before at t_pre).
- **After-occupancy:** the agent occupies position_after at tick t_post (the tick at which the next step's encode will occur, if the episode continues). A step *after-occupies* cell C iff position_after = C. Its timestamp is **(t_post, phase_next)** and its job-order key is also the step's ordinal o (the step that produced the after-position), **not** the next step's ordinal. After-occupancy is the occupancy relevant to *what the step resulted in* (including entering D, reaching the goal side, or being reset to start by a crush).
- **Counts:** before-count(C) = number of steps in the job/episode with position_before = C; after-count(C) = number of steps with position_after = C. Both denominators are the number of steps in the job/episode (every step has exactly one before-position and one after-position, including crush steps (after = start) and goal steps (after = goal)). Before-count and after-count for the same cell will generally differ by at most the boundary effects (entry/exit steps, first/last steps, crush resets) — the difference is expected and must be reported, not smoothed away.
- **First-visit timestamps (per cell set, per job/episode):**
  - first_before(C) = (t_pre, phase_pre) of the earliest step (by ordinal) with position_before = C, or NONE if before-count = 0. Label: "first before-visit".
  - first_after(C) = (t_post, phase_next) of the earliest step (by ordinal) with position_after = C, or NONE if after-count = 0. Label: "first after-visit".
  - first_union(C) = the earlier (by ordinal) of first_before(C) and first_after(C), with its type (before/after), tick, and phase stated. Ordering is by **ordinal**, not by tick value (ticks reset across episodes; ordinals do not — §5.1). For the common case of entering D: the entry step s has position_after = D (after-visit, ordinal s) and the next step s+1 has position_before = D (before-visit, ordinal s+1, unless the episode ends or a crush intervenes), so first_union(D) is the after-visit, one ordinal before the before-visit. Reporting only the before-visit would misstate the first occupancy by one step.
- **Coverage levels restated (supersedes REV-01 §2.2 L0–L4 occupancy wording; geometry unchanged):**
  - L0 (never reached the wall): before-count(W) = 0 AND after-count(W) = 0 (either count > 0 means the wall was reached; report both counts).
  - L1 (reached wall, wrong row): (before-count(W) > 0 OR after-count(W) > 0) AND before-count(E) = 0 AND after-count(E) = 0.
  - L2 (reached entry cell): before-count(E) > 0 OR after-count(E) > 0 (report both counts and first_union(E) with type/tick/phase).
  - L3 (occupied door cell): before-count(D) > 0 OR after-count(D) > 0 (report both counts and first_union(D) with type/tick/phase). **A report that states only "D visited at tick t, phase p" without stating before/after is incomplete under this revision** and must be corrected to state the type and the corresponding tick/phase (t_pre/phase_pre for before, t_post/phase_next for after).
  - L4 (crossed to goal side): before-count(goal-side set) > 0 OR after-count(goal-side set) > 0 (report both; the after-visit of a crossing step is the first goal-side occupancy, one ordinal before the next step's before-visit).
  - Each level's counts must state their denominator (steps in job/episode) and their segment (all steps, or pre/at/post if the coverage is reported per failed episode — coverage for causal purposes uses pre/at segments only, §4.6, while descriptive coverage may use all steps with the label stated).

### 6.2 Movement targets — world target vs hypothetical target (supersedes REV-01 §5 R-TARGET for crush steps)

- **R-TARGET (world-computed target, Essential for non-crush steps):** for a non-crush step, R-TARGET = target_spec = clip(position_before + delta(a_taken), grid bounds), as computed by the world in its movement code (REVISION-01 §4.1 step 3). For a non-crush step, R-TARGET is also the target used for blocked evaluation (blocked_spec uses target_spec, §3.1).
- **R-TARGET on crush-forced steps = NOT_APPLICABLE (mandatory):** on a crush step, the world returns before movement code (see §6.3, code order OBSERVED). No world-computed target exists for that step. Recording any grid cell as R-TARGET on a crush step — whether the clipped target of the taken action, the start cell, or any other cell — is **incorrect** under this revision, because it misdescribes an analyst value as a world value. R-TARGET must be the literal value NOT_APPLICABLE on crush steps, and blocked_log must be False (no blocked event is logged on a crush step — the world returns before the blocked branch), and clipped must be NOT_APPLICABLE (clipping is part of movement code that did not run).
- **R-TARGET-HYP (hypothetical target, Optional, distinct field — PROPOSAL):** an analyst may, for completeness, compute a hypothetical target for a crush step as clip(position_before + delta(a_taken), grid bounds) *ignoring* the crush check (i.e. "what the target would have been if the crush check had not returned first"). If computed, it must be recorded **only** under the field name R-TARGET-HYP, labelled in every record and analysis as "hypothetical target, analyst-computed ignoring the crush check, NOT a target computed by the world", and it must **never** be used for blocked classification, for R(s) computation, for conformance comparison (§3.2 uses T_spec, which for crush steps has no target), or described in prose as "the action's target" or "the target the world computed". If R-TARGET-HYP is not computed, record it as OMITTED (distinct from NOT_APPLICABLE: OMITTED means the analyst did not compute it; NOT_APPLICABLE means the world did not compute it).
- **Position_after on crush steps:** R-POS-AFTER = (0, 0) on a crush step, labelled **reset-to-start** (by the world's crush reset), not "move to start" and not a movement target. Any analysis that treats a crush step's position_after as a move outcome (e.g. counting it as a move into the start cell for coverage of the start cell as a *move* destination) must label it as a reset, not a move. (Coverage of the start cell as an *occupancy* — after-occupancy of (0,0) on a crush step — is still counted in after-count((0,0)), with the reset label attached, so that reset occupancies are not mistaken for voluntary returns to start.)

### 6.3 Code-order evidence for §6.2 (OBSERVED, re-checked for this revision)

`Exp1World.step` in `TEMPLATE/world_exp1.py` (docstring and code, re-read 2026-10-29 for this revision) orders its operations as: (1) door log; (2) crush check — if agent in door_cells and not open, log "crushed", reset agent to start, tick += 1, **return**; (3) movement — compute dx/dy, clipped target, blocked evaluation, position update; (4) goal log. The `return` in step (2) means step (3) — including target computation — does not execute on a crush step. R-TARGET = NOT_APPLICABLE on crush steps is therefore a code fact, not a convention choice. Any future world implementation that computes a target before the crush check must version its schema (R-CFG) and state the different order explicitly; the NOT_APPLICABLE rule applies to the pilot's world code as specified here.

### 7.2 Replay labelling (mandatory, replaces any "original trajectory" language for new step data)

- Every new step-level dataset (whether aggregates match or not) is labelled **RECONSTRUCTION / REPLAY** in its collection identifier, its R-LINK record, and every analysis that uses it. The full label form is: "REPLAY of DEV-LPAIR-001:eval:<construction>:<train_world>:<seed>:<eval_world>, generated [date/version] from [weights/config as in (B)], aggregate check: [AGGREGATE-MATCH / AGGREGATE-MISMATCH / NOT-CHECKED]". The word "original" must not appear in the label for replayed steps, except in the phrase "original source job" (referring to the pilot job it links to, not to the steps themselves).
- **AGGREGATE-MATCH replay:** may be used for diagnostic analysis *of the replay* (patterns, reference, inevitability, waste — all computed on replay steps), with the caveat that conclusions are about the replay's trajectory, linked to the source job by (A) + (B), not about the original's (discarded) steps. Any sentence of the form "the original agent did X at step s" based on replay data must be rewritten as "the replay (linked to source job J, aggregate-match) did X at step s".
- **AGGREGATE-MISMATCH replay (DIVERGENT-REPLAY):** must be labelled DIVERGENT-REPLAY, must **not** be pooled with original aggregates, must **not** be used as if it were the original job's trajectory, and its diagnostic conclusions (if reported at all) must be labelled "diagnostic of a divergent replay, not of the original job". The mismatch fields must be listed. A divergent replay does not retroactively change the original job's aggregates or verdict; it indicates that the replay inputs (weights, code, protocol, or state evolution) differ from the original's in some way, to be investigated, not smoothed over by re-running until a match is obtained (re-running until match, without a stated rule, would be selection on the validation criterion and must not be done).
- **Weak-evidence note for zero-success jobs (important, often missed):** for held-out jobs with reached = 0, ticks_total = 4000, and crushes_total = 0 (the majority of completed held-out jobs in the pilot), aggregate consistency is **especially weak** evidence: a large set of different weights, values, and trajectories all produce 0/20, 4000 ticks, 0 crushes. An AGGREGATE-MATCH on such a job therefore provides very little discrimination between "same trajectory" and "different trajectory with the same zero outcome", and must not be presented as strong validation. Aggregate matching is most informative where aggregates are most specific (e.g. non-zero reached counts with specific act_steps distributions); even there, it remains aggregate consistency (A), not identity (D).

### 7.3 P-VAL status (unchanged in authorisation, narrowed in meaning)

P-VAL remains a **proposal requiring a separate owner decision** (identifier, scope, source, pooling rule, and now also: whether R-VAL is AGGREGATE-MATCH/MISMATCH/NOT-CHECKED, whether replay labelling in §7.2 is adopted as stated, and whether divergent replays are reported or discarded — each to be stated in the authorisation). P-VAL is **not executed** by this revision, and no R-VAL field is filled by this revision. If the owner does not adopt P-VAL, then any future replay must still carry the §7.2 replay label and must still not claim original-trajectory identity, because identity is unavailable regardless of P-VAL adoption.

## 8. Consistency check — partition, transition categories, and aggregation are mutually coherent (deliverable 3)

This section is a check that a future aggregation report must pass, stated as identities and forbidden states. It is not a report on data (no data exist to check); it is the checklist that any future report's arithmetic must satisfy before its conclusions are accepted. A future report that fails any identity below is invalid as an aggregation, regardless of its conclusions.

### 8.1 Partition identity (per region, per segment, per population)

- For Population F, per region/segment: n_F = n_OPT + n_K3 + n_KBLANK + n_KNEVER + n_KUNK + n_HEAD + n_SEL, with n_K3 = n_K3-PARTIAL + n_K3-FULL. No P-BOTH term, no "other" term, no double-counted step. (Dec3 is an attribute, not a summand — §2.3.)
- For Population Q, per region/segment: n_Q = n_OPT-Q + n_K3-Q + n_KBLANK-Q + n_KNEVER-Q + n_KUNK-Q + n_SUBOPT-UNCLASS. No SEL/HEAD terms in n_Q.
- Excluded counts are separate: n_CRUSH-ND, n_UNREACH, n_TERMINAL-DEV. Non-conformant steps are flagged NONCONF and excluded from n_F/n_Q causal denominators (§3.2); for the total-steps check they are counted once, in a separate n_NONCONF bucket, with their descriptive class (if computed) shown as an attribute, not as a summand in n_F/n_Q.
- **Total identity (per region/segment, all steps together) — single mandated form:** total_steps = n_F + n_Q + n_CRUSH-ND + n_UNREACH + n_TERMINAL-DEV + n_NONCONF, where n_F and n_Q are the **causal** counts (NONCONF steps excluded from n_F/n_Q and counted only in n_NONCONF), and a NONCONF step's descriptive pattern class (if computed) is shown as an attribute on the n_NONCONF row, never as a summand in n_F/n_Q. Counting a NONCONF step in both n_F (or n_Q) and n_NONCONF is a counting error. A report that uses a descriptive form (NONCONF steps included in F/Q counts) must convert to this mandated form before presenting causal denominators, by subtracting the NONCONF counts explicitly and showing the subtraction.

### 8.2 Transition-category identity (per step)

- transition_type ∈ {crush, blocked, stay, move} exactly one per step (REVISION-01 §4.3, retained, with §6.2 target rules added).
- If transition_type = crush: R-TARGET = NOT_APPLICABLE, blocked_log = False, clipped = NOT_APPLICABLE, position_after = (0, 0) labelled reset-to-start, crushed = True, and T_spec crush_spec must be True for a CONFORMANT crush (otherwise the crush step is NON-CONFORMANT with a crush-mismatch field).
- If transition_type = blocked: position_after = position_before, target_spec ≠ position_before, blocked_log = True, crushed = False.
- If transition_type = stay: taken action = stay, position_after = position_before, blocked_log = False, crushed = False.
- If transition_type = move: position_after = target_spec (including the edge-clip subcase, where target_spec = position_before and clipped = True, blocked_log = False — move, not blocked, REVISION-01 §4.3 retained).
- Conformance identity: a step is CONFORMANT iff all fields in §3.2 match; otherwise it is NON-CONFORMANT with at least one mismatching field listed. A step labelled CONFORMANT with a listed mismatch, or NON-CONFORMANT with no listed mismatch, is an invalid record.

### 8.3 Aggregation / inevitability identities (per failed conformant episode, then per job scope)

- **Slack/waste identity:** slack_after(f) < 0, slack_after(s) ≥ 0 for all s < f (by minimality of f), total waste pre+at = slack_start − slack_after(f) ≥ slack_start + 1. OPT waste = 0 for every OPT step, and crush waste = 0 for every crush step (checks: if any OPT step has waste ≠ 0, or any crush step has waste ≠ 0, the V table or next_actual computation is wrong). Total waste pre+at by class (OPT 0 + K3 + KBLANK + KNEVER + KUNK + HEAD + SEL, as applicable, with protocol/unattributed splits as in §4.3) = total waste pre+at. Any violation indicates a V, next_actual, class, or segment error.
- **Segment identity (eligible steps only):** for a failed episode, eligible_pre + eligible_at + eligible_post = total eligible steps in the episode. CRUSH-ND steps (including crush steps that belong to a placement chain descriptively) are **not** in any of pre/at/post eligible counts; they are counted in n_CRUSH-ND. Placement-chain entry/D-occupancy steps are ordinary eligible steps counted in pre/at/post by their position relative to f (an entry step before f is pre, an entry step that is f is at, and so on) — there is no separate chain segment in the identity, and creating one would double-count those steps.
- **Consistency check (REVISION-01 §4.4 check, retained without a crush-chain exception — none is needed):** if every eligible step in a failed episode is OPT, then the episode's diagnostic is UNKNOWN (optimal play must have reached the goal). This holds with crushes present as well: crush steps have waste 0 and preserve slack, so an all-OPT eligible-step episode with crushes still cannot cap-fail under a correct V — an all-OPT cap-failed episode indicates a V/reference error, a misclassified step, or a non-conformant step pooled as eligible, and must be investigated, not reported as a policy failure. (The earlier "crush-chain exception" idea is superseded by the crush-waste theorem: there is no case in which optimal eligible steps plus a crush produce a loss that optimal steps alone would not, because the crush adds zero waste.)
- **Frequency vs waste coherence:** a job-level causal claim must show both Table C frequency status (T/Y set or unset, §4.4) and waste status (§4.3 sole/joint per episode). A claim showing only frequency, only waste, only Table D prevalence, or only trigger proportions (at-class fractions) is incomplete and must be returned as INCONCLUSIVE, not upgraded by the reader.
- **Scope coherence:** causal denominators (n_pre, waste sums, sole/joint checks) include failed conformant episodes only. Successful episodes, deviation episodes, and post segments must not appear in those denominators. If a report's causal denominator includes any of them, its causal proportions and waste sums are invalid, even if the pattern counts themselves are correct.

**Answer: Yes, ready for owner approval as a specification — with the remaining limits stated below, which are limits of authorisation and measurement, not hidden inconsistencies. **What is now internally consistent (and was not, before this revision):**
- The step-pattern classification is now a genuine partition (§2, proved in §2.6): P-BOTH is removed, every eligible step receives exactly one class, excluded states (crush-forced, terminal, unreachable) and the Q-missing population are stated explicitly, and aggregation identities (§8.1) can be audited arithmetically. - Transition categories are now coherent with the reference (§3): conformance compares observed to specified transition on stated fields, expected events (crush/blocked/timing/loop/cap) are event patterns rather than execution failures, and the only execution-failure evidence class is Ex-DEV (deviation). The alternative Ex-ALT definition is stated with its distinguishability limit, rather than left as an implicit ambiguity that could be used to claim separation without a test.
- Causal aggregation is now linked to loss of recoverability (§4): failed episodes are split at earliest inevitability, post-inevitability patterns are excluded from causal denominators, and job-level causal claims require both an owner-set frequency condition (necessary, not sufficient) and an exact waste-based link (sole/joint, per episode, with unattributed blockers and the crush-waste theorem). Descriptive prevalence and causal aggregation are two separate tables that must not be merged.
- Estimate provenance is now order-correct (§5): first estimates are a job-order tuple plus a monotonic ordinal, KBLANK/KNEVER/KUNK are defined by ordinal comparison (never by tick comparison across resets), and partial-knowledge K3 is reported separately rather than merged silently into full-knowledge K3.
- Validation language is now honest (§7): aggregate consistency, model/configuration provenance, deterministic reproduction, and original-trajectory identity are four distinct claims with distinct evidence; identity is not established by any available evidence and must not be claimed; new step data are replays, labelled as such, with divergent replays handled explicitly rather than smoothed over.
- Occupancy and targets are now time-indexed correctly (§6): before- vs after-occupancy have their own ticks/phases and first-visit timestamps ordered by ordinal, and crush-step targets are NOT_APPLICABLE by code order, with hypothetical targets quarantined under a distinct field name.
