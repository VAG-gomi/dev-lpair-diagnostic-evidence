**What this revision changes, in one paragraph:**
REVISION-02 is a major improvement, but five of its rules do not cohere with its own definitions. First, it labelled a schedule mismatch K3-FULL even when full estimates never appeared — contradicting its own definition of "full", since the ScheduleModel's partial path can return both phase and remaining without a period. Second, it filed KBLANK/KNEVER waste under a "protocol" bucket, as if an availability condition (K flag off) were itself a protocol cause, when K = 0 does not even establish that no schedule information existed. Third, it defined step populations without conformance exclusions and then removed non-conformant steps later, so the partition identity and the causal denominators used different eligibility rules, and its conformance check did not explicitly require step-to-step continuity or target/transition-type agreement. Fourth, its frequency rule counted only pre-segment patterns, so a pattern that was the trigger in every failed episode yet never appeared before the trigger could never pass — or, if counted loosely, could be silently counted as a pre-step. Fifth, it used "sole cause" / "joint cause" language for waste results while elsewhere correctly stating that waste arithmetic does not prove a unique mechanism. REVISION-03 corrects all five: K subcategories now follow actual per-step estimate availability (with an explicit unresolved status when provenance is missing); availability-state waste is unassigned, never protocol-labelled, with every waste unit in exactly one bucket; recorded and causal populations are defined as two explicit levels with a reconciliation identity; contributor frequency, trigger frequency, and post frequency are kept distinct with an owner-prescribed combination rule; and "waste-sufficient" is reserved for the arithmetic result, with mechanism status kept separately and INCONCLUSIVE unless intervention evidence exists.

**Evidence labels, as in the original and earlier revisions:** OBSERVED, INFERENCE, HYPOTHESIS, PROPOSAL, UNKNOWN. New definitions in this revision are PROPOSAL until the owner approves them. Code-derived facts are OBSERVED from the code (ScheduleModel partial path and encoder K rule, re-checked for this revision — see §2.1).

## 2. Revised K classification — subcategories follow actual estimate availability (supersedes REV-02 §2.3 step 2 sub-attribute rule and REV-02 §5.3 in full)

### 2.1 Code facts that determine the rule (OBSERVED, re-checked for this revision)

- **Full estimates** = period, open_len, and last_opening_tick all non-None in ScheduleModel state after processing the observe call at the step's encode (wrapper.py `_has_estimates`, OBSERVED). Full estimates support periodic projection to any tick.
- **Partial knowledge** = open_len known **and** a cleanly observed opening (no gap since the opening, current tick at or after the opening), without a period (wrapper.py `_partial_span`, OBSERVED). On the partial path, `ticks_since_opening` returns elapsed ticks since the opening (not modulo-wrapped), and `open_ticks_remaining` returns open_len − elapsed while open, 0 while closed. Partial features are therefore exact *within the observed span* but are not periodic: elapsed ticks can exceed the true period, and no projection beyond the observed span is available.
- **Encoder K rule** = K = 1 iff phase and remaining are both available (non-None) after the encode's observe call; otherwise phase/remaining slots stay 0.0 (blanked) and K = 0 (lpair.py encode, OBSERVED; REV-01 blank-feature convention, retained). Consequence 1: **K = 1 does not imply full estimates** — partial-path phase + remaining also satisfy "both available". Consequence 2: **K = 0 does not imply no schedule information** — it implies only that phase and remaining were not *both* available; one of them, or an observed opening without an open_len, may still have been present, but the blanked slots do not say which.
- **Estimates can be lost after first appearing:** on a contradiction, ScheduleModel drops stale events and re-estimates (period/open_len cleared, last_opening reset to the latest opening if any — wrapper.py observe/re-estimate path, OBSERVED). First-appearance therefore does **not** imply current availability. Any rule that infers current full availability from first-appearance ordinal alone is invalid after a contradiction.

- (a) If the step's ordinal < first_estimates_ordinal (and the tuple is VERIFIED), then est_full_cur = False is certain (full estimates had not yet first appeared). K = 1 at such a step must be partial-path features.

### 2.3 K3 subcategory rule (replaces REV-02 §2.3 step 2 sub-attribute rule and REV-02 §5.3 note in full)

For a SUBOPTIMAL, timing-critical step with K_rec = 1 (so phase and remaining are both recorded, by the encoder rule), let true_phase = t_pre mod 20, true_remaining = (10 − true_phase) if true_phase < 10 else 0 (true periodic features, REV-01 §3.4, retained). Let partial expectations, when partial provenance is present (R-LAST-OPEN and R-OPEN-LEN both recorded and non-null, and the span since last opening is clean — clean span is implied by K_rec = 1 via the partial path, since a gapped span returns None for partial phase and would give K = 0 unless full estimates apply): partial_phase_exp = t_pre − last_opening_tick (elapsed ticks, **not** modulo-wrapped), partial_remaining_exp = max(0, open_len − partial_phase_exp) if door_open_pre is true, else 0 (following the partial-path remaining rule: open_len − elapsed while open, 0 while closed).

- **K3-FULL** iff est_full_cur = True AND (phase_rec ≠ true_phase OR remaining_rec ≠ true_remaining). *Meaning:* full periodic knowledge was currently available and was wrong at this step. This is the only case that may be labelled FULL.
- **K3-PARTIAL** iff est_full_cur = False AND partial provenance is present AND (phase_rec ≠ partial_phase_exp OR remaining_rec ≠ partial_remaining_exp). *Meaning:* partial-path features were currently available and were wrong against their own partial semantics. Note: a partial feature that differs from true periodic features but **matches** partial expectations is **not** a mismatch (see fall-through rule below) — the non-modulo elapsed value is correct partial knowledge, not an error, even though it differs numerically from true_phase mod 20 once elapsed ≥ 20.
- **K3-UNRESOLVED** iff (phase_rec ≠ true_phase OR remaining_rec ≠ true_remaining) AND est_full_cur = Unknown, OR est_full_cur = False AND partial provenance is missing (R-LAST-OPEN or R-OPEN-LEN not recorded / null for a reason other than genuinely none — genuinely-none partial provenance with K_rec = 1 and est_full_cur = False is itself contradictory, see below) so that neither the FULL comparison nor the PARTIAL comparison can be completed. *Meaning:* a difference from true periodic features was observed, but whether it is a genuine schedule error (FULL or PARTIAL sense) cannot be determined from the recorded provenance. K3-UNRESOLVED is a **separately defined unresolved status**, not a subcategory of FULL or PARTIAL, and its waste goes to the unassigned partition (§3.2), never to an assigned K3 bucket.
- **Fall-through (K3 NOT_SUPPORTED for this step)** iff recorded features match the applicable expectation: (est_full_cur = True AND phase_rec = true_phase AND remaining_rec = true_remaining) OR (est_full_cur = False AND partial provenance present AND phase_rec = partial_phase_exp AND remaining_rec = partial_remaining_exp) OR (est_full_cur = Unknown AND phase_rec = true_phase AND remaining_rec = true_remaining — matching true periodic features exonerates schedule at this step regardless of provenance, since correct features cannot explain the step's suboptimality). In fall-through cases the step proceeds to HEAD/SEL in the partition (§2.3 steps 6–7 of REV-02, retained), with a K-correct or partial-correct attribute recorded.
- **Contradiction case (genuinely-none partial provenance with K_rec = 1):** if est_full_cur = False, R-LAST-OPEN is genuinely none (not merely unrecorded), and K_rec = 1, the record is internally inconsistent (partial path requires a cleanly observed opening; full path requires last_opening non-None) — classify the step's K status as INVALID-K (see §4.2), exclude it from K denominators, and investigate the recording. Do not force it into PARTIAL or FULL.

## 3. Feature availability separated from attribution, and the revised waste partition (supersedes REV-02 §2.3 step 3 interpretation, REV-02 §4.3 waste buckets, and REV-02 §5.3 KBLANK/KNEVER descriptions)

### 3.1 Corrected descriptions — what each availability state does and does not establish

- **KBLANK (K_rec = 0, ordinal < first_estimates_ordinal, tuple VERIFIED):** establishes only that the encoder's K flag was off before full estimates first appeared. It does **not** establish that the model had no schedule information: partial information may have been present (an observed opening without open_len gives phase availability via the partial path only if open_len is also known for remaining — see R-FEAT fields; an opening observed without open_len gives neither phase nor remaining via the partial path as implemented, but the *opening event itself* is schedule information held in ScheduleModel state, even though it does not set K = 1). The correct statement for a KBLANK step is: "K flag off before first full estimates; individual phase/remaining availability: [recorded R-FEAT values, or UNKNOWN if not recorded]." It is not a statement about protocol, failure, or absence of all schedule information.
- **KNEVER (K_rec = 0, tuple VERIFIED NEVER):** establishes only that full estimates never appeared in the job and the K flag was off at this step. It is a **trajectory consequence**: the agent's observed (tick, door) sequence in this job never supplied the conditions for full estimates (two clean openings for a period, or an opening-to-closing pair for open_len, without contradiction clearing them — per ScheduleModel estimation rules). It does **not** establish that any protocol was violated, that the model failed to learn, or that schedule information was absent (partial information may have been present at various steps; see R-FEAT). The correct statement is: "K flag off; full estimates never achieved in this job; individual availability: [R-FEAT or UNKNOWN]."
- **KUNK (K_rec = 0, ordinal ≥ first_estimates_ordinal, tuple VERIFIED):** establishes only that the K flag was off at a step after full estimates had once appeared. Possible explanations include contradiction clearing estimates, a gapped span, or a recording error — the availability state alone does not distinguish them. Attribution is suspended (unassigned), not assigned to any cause.
- **Individual availability (R-FEAT-PHASE / R-FEAT-REM):** when recorded, these state per-step raw availability (True/False) before blanking. When not recorded, individual availability is UNKNOWN — it must not be inferred as "both unavailable" from K = 0 (K = 0 establishes only "not both available", i.e. at least one unavailable, by the encoder rule). Any future KBLANK/KNEVER analysis that claims "no phase information" or "no remaining information" without R-FEAT values is overclaiming and must be downgraded to UNKNOWN for that feature.

### 3.2 Revised waste partition — every waste unit in exactly one bucket, no protocol bucket (supersedes REV-02 §4.3 waste_protocol / waste_unattributed definitions)

For conformant, sequence-valid episodes in the causal population (§4), with waste(s) as defined in REV-02 §4.1 (OPT waste 0, crush waste 0 by theorem, suboptimal waste ≥ 1), partition pre/at waste (causal sums, §5) as follows. Each step's waste belongs to exactly one bucket, determined by the step's class (for pattern steps) or by its unassigned status:

**Assigned buckets (pattern waste — may support accounting-sufficient claims, §5.3):**
- SEL waste (Population F, class SEL, pre/at segments)
- HEAD waste (Population F, class HEAD, pre/at segments)
- K3-FULL waste (class K3-FULL, pre/at segments)
- K3-PARTIAL waste (class K3-PARTIAL, pre/at segments)

**Unassigned buckets (separately reported, never labelled as a cause — may block accounting claims as in §5.3, never support them):**
- K3-UNRESOLVED waste (class K3-UNRESOLVED, pre/at segments)
- KBLANK waste (class KBLANK, pre/at segments) — availability-state waste, **not** protocol waste
- KNEVER waste (class KNEVER, pre/at segments) — availability-state waste, **not** protocol waste
- KUNK waste (class KUNK, pre/at segments)
- SUBOPT-UNCLASS waste (Population Q, class SUBOPT-UNCLASS, pre/at segments)
- FIELD-MISSING waste (steps whose pattern class is FIELD-MISSING-K, K-STATUS-UNKNOWN, or otherwise unclassifiable due to missing fields, but whose waste is still computable from recorded positions/reference — pre/at segments only; if waste is not computable, the step contributes no waste unit and is counted separately as waste-undefined, see below)

**Non-causal / descriptive waste (never in causal sums):**
- Post-segment waste (any class, post segment) — descriptive only (§5.2)
- OPT waste (= 0, identity, no bucket needed) and crush waste (= 0, identity, no bucket needed) — zero contributions, stated for completeness so the partition sums correctly
- Non-conformant arithmetic waste: for non-conformant steps, the arithmetic quantity V(next_recorded) − V(s) + 1 may be computed descriptively from recorded positions, but it is **not** causal waste (the transition deviated, so the next state is not attributable to the pattern class in the causal sense). It goes to a descriptive NONCONF-ARITHMETIC bucket, never added to causal sums, and never counted as assigned or unassigned causal waste.
- Waste-undefined steps: steps whose waste cannot be computed (missing position_before/position_after/reference) contribute no waste unit to any partition; they are counted as n_waste-undefined (by reason) and excluded from waste sums with the exclusion stated. They must not be assigned waste 0 (zero is a computed value for OPT/crush steps, not a default for missing data).

**Partition identities (causal population, per episode, pre/at segments):**
- total_causal_waste(ep) = waste_SEL + waste_HEAD + waste_K3-FULL + waste_K3-PARTIAL + waste_unassigned_total, where waste_unassigned_total = waste_K3-UNRESOLVED + waste_KBLANK + waste_KNEVER + waste_KUNK + waste_SUBOPT-UNCLASS + waste_FIELD-MISSING (all pre/at, causal population).
- total_causal_waste(ep) must also equal slack_start(ep) − slack_after(f) for failed episodes (REV-02 §8.3 identity, retained) — any violation indicates a classification, segment, or V error.
- **No protocol bucket exists.** Any report containing a "protocol waste" bucket, or describing KBLANK/KNEVER waste as protocol-caused, is using superseded terminology and must be corrected to the unassigned partition above.

### 3.3 What would justify a protocol attribution in future (criterion, not a claim — owner decision)

A protocol attribution for availability-state waste would require, at minimum, all of the following, none of which is present in the current specification:
- (a) a stated protocol rule (owner-approved, before collection) that schedule features or full estimates *should* have been available by a stated step/ordinal/condition in the job (e.g. "full estimates should be available after the second clean opening in a job" — currently no such rule is stated; ScheduleModel estimation is descriptive, not prescriptive);
- (b) a demonstrated violation of that rule on the steps in question (e.g. estimates absent despite the stated conditions having been observed, as verified from recorded (tick, door) observations — not merely from K = 0);
- (c) a stated link from the violation to the waste (the steps' suboptimality depends on the missing features, per the K precedence and reference analysis — not merely co-occurrence).
Until (a)–(c) are supplied and approved, protocol attribution for KBLANK/KNEVER (or any availability state) is **not available** as a diagnostic category, and availability-state waste remains unassigned. This criterion is itself a PROPOSAL for owner decision (§7, decision 3); it does not create a protocol claim.

## 4. Two-level populations and record-integrity qualification (supersedes REV-02 §2.1 eligibility as the sole population definition, REV-02 §2.5/§8.1 identities as single-level identities, and REV-02 §3.2 conformance as the complete check set)

### 4.1 Level 1 — recorded population (all recorded steps, descriptive labels, no silent repair)

**Definition:** Level 1 = every step row recorded for a job/episode, in job execution order (by episode, then local step, then ordinal). Every Level 1 step receives exactly one descriptive label from the extended label set below, determined by the fields actually present in the record. No field is imputed, repaired, or inferred silently: if a required field is missing or contradictory, the label is INVALID/UNKNOWN for the affected analysis (see §4.2), with the missing/contradictory fields listed.

**Descriptive labels (Level 1, mutually exclusive by the same priority logic as REV-02 §2, extended with integrity labels — priority: integrity first, then state exclusions, then pattern classes):**
1. **INVALID-RECORD** — a field required for pattern classification or sequence ordering is missing or self-contradictory (see §4.2). Sub-labels by reason: MISSING-POSITION, MISSING-ACTION, MISSING-TICK, CONTRADICTORY-PHASE (phase ≠ t_pre mod 20). Missing Q does **not** make a record INVALID-RECORD (it routes the step to Population Q handling, SUBOPT-UNCLASS); missing K fields route to FIELD-MISSING-K (§2.4 row K-X3); target/transition-type contradictions do **not** make a record INVALID-RECORD (they make it transition-unqualified for Level 2-T, see qualification matrix §4.3, while pattern classification may still be computable).
2. **TERMINAL-DEV** — position_before is the goal (protocol deviation, REV-02 §2.1, retained).
3. **UNREACH** — reference V(s) = ∞ (REV-02 §2.1, retained).
4. **CRUSH-ND** — state-forced crush step: position_before ∈ D AND door_open_pre is false under the *true* schedule (phase < 10 check), regardless of whether a crush was logged. (Distinction from log-forced: if a crush is logged but the state is not crush-forced (door open, or position_before ∉ D), the step is **not** CRUSH-ND; it is a pattern-eligible step with a NONCONF crush-log mismatch, §4.3. If the state is crush-forced but no crush is logged, the step is still CRUSH-ND by state, with a NONCONF crush-log mismatch flagged for transition claims.)
5. **Pattern classes** — OPT, K3-FULL, K3-PARTIAL, K3-UNRESOLVED, KBLANK, KNEVER, KUNK, HEAD, SEL (Population F), SUBOPT-UNCLASS (Population Q), as in REV-02 §2 as corrected by §2 of this revision.
6. **K-STATUS-UNKNOWN / INVALID-K / FIELD-MISSING-K** — as in §2.4 rows K-X1/K-X2/K-X3 (K-status labels, not pattern classes; counted in Level 1 INVALID/UNKNOWN totals, not in pattern counts).

**Repair rule (mandatory, no silent repair):** if a record is corrected after initial recording (e.g. a tick recomputed from S + s, a target recomputed from code), the correction must be stored as a **separate corrected record** with the original record preserved, the corrected fields listed, the correction method stated, and the step flagged REPAIRED. Repaired steps are **excluded from Level 2 causal populations by default**; inclusion requires a separate owner decision stating the repair rule and its scope. A report that presents repaired values as originally recorded values, without the REPAIRED flag and original preservation, is invalid as a record-integrity matter, regardless of whether the repaired values are correct.

### 4.2 What counts as missing or contradictory (field requirements per analysis)

A step is INVALID/UNKNOWN **for a given analysis** (not necessarily for all analyses — see qualification matrix §4.3) if any field that analysis requires is missing or contradictory:

- **Pattern classification requires:** position_before (valid grid cell), phase (= t_pre mod 20, checkable from recorded t_pre and S), taken action, taken act (for SEL/HEAD split; if act missing but action present, SEL/HEAD split unavailable → SUBOPT-UNCLASS handling, not INVALID-RECORD), selector Q + full head Q (for SEL/HEAD split; if missing → Population Q handling, §2.4 of REV-02, retained), K features (K_rec, phase_rec, remaining_rec — if missing → FIELD-MISSING-K, §2.4 row K-X3), reference R(s)/V(s) (if uncomputable → UNREACH).
- **Waste/slack accounting requires (in addition to pattern fields):** position_after (valid grid cell), conformance of position_after/crush/reached/door/tick to T_spec (§4.3, Level 2-P checks), and sequence validity of the episode (§4.3). If position_after is missing, waste is undefined for that step (waste-undefined count, §3.2). If position_after is present but non-conformant, waste is NONCONF-ARITHMETIC (descriptive only, §3.2), not causal waste.
- **Transition-event classification requires:** recorded target (non-crush steps; NOT_APPLICABLE on crush steps, REV-02 §6.2, retained), recorded transition_type, recorded blocked_log, recorded crushed, recorded reached, recorded door_open_pre, recorded t_pre/t_post. If any is missing, the step's transition-event class is UNKNOWN for that field's claim (e.g. missing target → blocked/target claim UNKNOWN, even if pattern class is computable).
- **Coverage/first-visit claims require:** position_before, position_after, t_pre/t_post (for before/after timestamps, REV-02 §6.1, retained), and ordinal/episode order. If ordinal is missing, first-visit ordering across episodes is UNKNOWN (ticks alone cannot order across resets, REV-02 §5.1, retained).
- **Contradiction types (each is a record-integrity failure, flagged with the field pair):** phase ≠ t_pre mod 20; door_open_pre ≠ (phase < 10) under the true schedule (unless door_open_pre is recorded from a different schedule definition, in which case the definition difference must be stated in R-CFG — otherwise it is a contradiction); position_before(s) ≠ position_after(s−1) (continuity, §4.3); recorded target ≠ target_spec on a non-crush step; recorded transition_type ≠ type implied by T_spec + log semantics (§4.3); recorded blocked_log ≠ blocked_spec; recorded crushed ≠ crush_spec; recorded reached ≠ reached_spec; t_pre(s) ≠ t_pre(s−1) + 1 or t_pre(0) ≠ S. A step with any contradiction is flagged with all contradictory field pairs listed; it is not "repaired" by choosing one field over another without the repair rule in §4.1.

### 4.3 Qualification checks and the per-claim Level 2 populations (deliverable 4, part 1)

**Checks (all must be stated per step/episode in a future collection's qualification report):**
- **C-TRANS (step transition conformance, REV-02 §3.2, retained):** position_after, crushed, blocked_log, reached, next t_pre, door_open_pre all match T_spec(s, a_taken).
- **C-TARGET (new, explicit — work-order requirement):** on non-crush steps, recorded R-TARGET = target_spec = clip(position_before + delta(a_taken), grid bounds); on crush steps, recorded R-TARGET = NOT_APPLICABLE. (Target_spec is computed from recorded position_before and action, not from the recorded target field — otherwise the check would be circular.)
- **C-TYPE (new, explicit — work-order requirement):** recorded transition_type = type derived from T_spec + log semantics: crush iff crush_spec; else blocked iff blocked_spec; else stay iff a_taken = stay; else move. (Edge-clip moves: target_spec = position_before with clipped = True, type = move, not blocked — REV-02 §4.3, retained.) Recorded blocked_log must equal blocked_spec, recorded crushed must equal crush_spec, recorded reached must equal reached_spec, as in C-TRANS (listed here so that C-TYPE is checkable as a unit).
- **C-CONT (new, explicit — work-order requirement, episode-level):** continuity within an episode: position_before(0) = (0, 0); for s > 0, position_before(s) = position_after(s−1); t_pre(0) = S; for s > 0, t_pre(s) = t_pre(s−1) + 1. C-CONT is an episode-level check: a failure at step s breaks the sequence chain for the episode (see consequence rule below).
- **C-PROTO (episode/job protocol, REV-02 §3.2, retained):** episode length ≤ 200, ends after a reached step or at exactly 200 steps, does not continue after a reached step, start_phase S ∈ 0..19, episodes in a job occur in the stated order with the stated start_phases (pilot eval: 0..19 in order).

### 4.4 Total-count and partition identities at both levels (deliverable 4, part 2)

**Level 2-P partition identity (per region/segment, causal population only — must hold exactly):**
n_causal-P = n_OPT-P + n_K3-FULL-P + n_K3-PARTIAL-P + n_K3-UNRESOLVED-P + n_KBLANK-P + n_KNEVER-P + n_KUNK-P + n_HEAD-P + n_SEL-P + n_SUBOPT-UNCLASS-P,

**Reconciliation identity (the bridge between levels — must be shown in every future report that makes a causal claim):**
For each pattern class C (and for the total), the report must show:
n_recorded(C) − n_excluded_NONCONF(C) − n_excluded_SEQUENCE(C) − n_excluded_PROTOCOL(C) − n_excluded_MISSING(C) = n_causal-P(C),
where each exclusion count is by reason code (NONCONF = C-TRANS failure; SEQUENCE = C-CONT failure affecting the episode; PROTOCOL = C-PROTO failure; MISSING = required field absent for Level 2-P). A step excluded for multiple reasons must be counted under exactly one reason code by the stated precedence (order: PROTOCOL (episode) > SEQUENCE (episode) > NONCONF (step) > MISSING (step) — precedence must be stated in the report; if a different precedence is used, it must be stated and applied consistently, and the reconciliation must still sum exactly). **No step is counted in both n_causal-P(C) and any exclusion count for the same claim type.** The same reconciliation form applies, mutatis mutandis, to Level 2-T and Level 2-C (with their own reason codes from §4.3).

                                   unless intervention evidence is separately supplied

## 5. Revised causal aggregation — contributors, triggers, and post observations kept distinct (supersedes REV-02 §§4.4–4.6 frequency and Table C rules in part; REV-02 §4.1–§4.3 waste/slack definitions retained except for bucket and terminology changes in §§3, 6)

### 5.1 Terminology for this section (accounting terms — mechanism terms are in §6)

- **Inevitability step (trigger observation):** the step f in a failed episode (REV-02 §4.2, renamed from "attributable step" per §6). "Trigger" in this revision always means "trigger observation" (the step at which slack first becomes negative), never "proved trigger cause".
- **Contributor:** a pattern class occurring on pre-segment steps (s < f) in failed episodes. Contributors are candidate waste sources *before* the loss point.
- **Accounting-sufficient (sole/joint):** the waste arithmetic in §5.3 (renamed from REV-02 "sole cause" / "joint cause" per §6). Accounting-sufficient ≠ mechanism-established.

### 5.2 Three frequencies, three denominators — never merged (supersedes REV-02 §4.4 condition 2 and §4.6 Table C frequency reporting)

For a stated pattern class P (or, for K3, a stated subcategory or the K3 family with subcounts shown — see §5.4 note on K3 frequency), a stated region, and a stated scope (failed, conformant, sequence-valid episodes in the causal population, Level 2-P), compute all three:

- **Contributor frequency (pre):** contrib_freq(P) = count_P_pre / n_pre, where count_P_pre = number of Level 2-P steps in the region with class P in pre segments (s < f) across failed episodes in scope, and n_pre = total Level 2-P steps in the region in pre segments across the same episodes. Contributor episode spread: contrib_spread(P) = (# failed episodes in scope with ≥ 1 P step in pre) / (# failed episodes in scope). Contributor frequency answers: "among pre-loss steps, how prevalent was P?"
- **Trigger frequency (at):** trigger_freq(P) = (# failed episodes in scope whose inevitability step f has class P) / (# failed episodes in scope). The denominator is **episodes**, not steps (each failed episode has exactly one f). Trigger frequency answers: "in how many failed episodes was P the class at the loss point?" The at step is **never** counted in count_P_pre or n_pre, and pre steps are **never** counted in trigger_freq's numerator. A pattern that appears both as a contributor and as a trigger in the same episode contributes to both quantities (different denominators, different questions) — that is not double-counting within either quantity, but the report must state both quantities separately so the reader does not add them.
- **Post frequency (descriptive only):** post_freq(P) = count_P_post / n_post, where count_P_post / n_post are Level 2-P steps in post segments (s > f). Post frequency answers only: "after loss, how prevalent was P?" It must be labelled descriptive only and must never enter contributor or trigger thresholds, waste sums, or causal proportions.

**Trigger-only pattern case (the defect in REV-02, stated as the worked rule):** suppose P is the at-step class in every failed episode (trigger_freq = 1.0) but has zero pre occurrences (contrib_freq = 0, contrib_spread = 0). Then:
- Contributor condition (if it applies, §5.4) **fails** for P — correctly, because P contributed nothing before the loss point.
- Trigger condition (if it applies, §5.4) **passes** for P on frequency grounds — correctly, because P was at the loss point in every episode.
- Waste accounting (§5.3) still governs: P's waste per episode = pre waste (0) + at-step waste (waste(f) for that episode). Sole accounting-sufficiency for P requires at-step waste alone ≥ slack_start + 1 per episode. In most episodes at-step waste is small (the loss is usually cumulative: pre waste from many patterns already reduced slack to near zero, and f's waste is merely the final unit) — in that case P is **not** sole accounting-sufficient despite trigger_freq = 1.0, and P can at most join a joint accounting set with the pre contributors whose waste actually accumulated. Conversely, if at-step waste alone ≥ slack_start + 1 in every episode (a single catastrophic step, e.g. a fatal-phase entry from a high-V state with huge V jump), then P **is** sole accounting-sufficient for those episodes despite zero pre occurrences. Trigger frequency alone decides neither outcome; waste decides sufficiency, frequency (as prescribed, §5.4) decides job-level generality. **A trigger-only pattern is therefore neither automatically causal nor automatically excluded** — its status is determined by the prescribed combination rule (§5.4) plus waste (§5.3), stated explicitly per pattern.

### 5.3 Waste accounting with the revised buckets (supersedes REV-02 §4.3 bucket definitions; slack/waste arithmetic retained)

Per episode ep in scope (failed, conformant, sequence-valid, Level 2-P), with slack_start(ep) as in REV-02 §4.1:

- waste_P(ep) for an assigned pattern P ∈ {SEL, HEAD, K3-FULL, K3-PARTIAL} = sum of waste(s) over pre steps with class P + waste of the at step if its class is P. (Crush steps contribute 0, REV-02 crush-waste theorem, retained. OPT steps contribute 0.)
- waste_unassigned_total(ep) = sum of waste over pre/at steps in unassigned buckets (§3.2: K3-UNRESOLVED + KBLANK + KNEVER + KUNK + SUBOPT-UNCLASS + FIELD-MISSING). This merged total replaces REV-02's separate waste_protocol / waste_unattributed quantities in blocker rules (see below). Sub-bucket values must still be shown separately in the report (so that, e.g., a large KBLANK sub-bucket is visible as availability-state waste, not hidden inside a merged total).
- waste_other(ep) for a stated P = sum of waste over pre/at steps in assigned buckets other than P (SEL/HEAD/K3-FULL/K3-PARTIAL excluding P). (Unassigned waste is **not** in waste_other; it is in waste_unassigned_total. This keeps the three-way split — assigned-P / assigned-other / unassigned — exact: total pre/at causal waste = waste_P + waste_other + waste_unassigned_total, for any P, with OPT/crush zeros contributing nothing.)

**Sole waste-sufficient (accounting) — episode-level, then job-level (renamed from REV-02 "sole cause"):**
- *Episode-level sole accounting-sufficient:* waste_P(ep) ≥ slack_start(ep) + 1.
- *Job-level sole accounting-sufficient:* episode-level sole holds for **every** failed episode in the stated scope (REV-02 per-episode-all rule, retained; alternative job-level rules remain an owner decision, REV-02 §9 decision 4, carried forward to §7 decision 4). If it holds for a stated subset only, the claim is **per-episode sole accounting-sufficient (stated subset)**, not job-level.
- *Unassigned blocker:* if waste_unassigned_total(ep) ≥ slack_start(ep) + 1 and waste_P(ep) < slack_start(ep) + 1, then episode ep's accounting claim for P is INCONCLUSIVE (unassigned waste alone could account for the loss). If waste_P(ep) ≥ slack_start(ep) + 1, the blocker does not defeat sole accounting-sufficiency (P alone suffices arithmetically regardless of unassigned waste), but unassigned waste must still be reported alongside, because the episode then has multiple sufficient waste sources — the claim is "P waste-sufficient", not "P the only waste source". (This corrects REV-02's blocker wording, which spoke of "cause" and "only contributor" in mechanism terms; the accounting version is exact and makes no mechanism claim.)

**Joint waste-sufficient (accounting) — episode-level, then job-level (renamed from REV-02 "joint cause"):**
- *Episode-level joint accounting-sufficient* for a stated assigned-pattern set S (subset of {SEL, HEAD, K3-FULL, K3-PARTIAL}; K3-family sets must state subcategory composition — see §5.4 note): sum_{P∈S} waste_P(ep) ≥ slack_start(ep) + 1, AND no single P ∈ S is episode-level sole (otherwise report sole, not joint), AND waste_unassigned_total(ep) < slack_start(ep) + 1 (otherwise unassigned alone could account for it and the joint claim is INCONCLUSIVE for that episode).
- *Job-level joint accounting-sufficient:* episode-level joint holds for every failed episode in scope, with the **same** set S (a set varying per episode is a per-episode report, not a job-level claim — REV-02 rule, retained).

**K3-family accounting note:** a "K3" accounting claim must state its subcategory composition. Permitted forms: (a) K3-FULL sole/joint (using K3-FULL waste only); (b) K3-PARTIAL sole/joint (using K3-PARTIAL waste only); (c) K3-assigned-family sole/joint (using K3-FULL + K3-PARTIAL waste, with both sub-buckets shown and the claim labelled "K3-family (FULL+PARTIAL), not FULL-only"). K3-UNRESOLVED waste must **never** be included in any assigned K3 sum (it is unassigned, §3.2); a K3 claim that needs unresolved waste to reach slack + 1 is INCONCLUSIVE for the assigned claim (unassigned blocker applies).

### 5.4 Frequency thresholds — owner-prescribed combination rule (supersedes REV-02 §4.4 condition 3 as a single pre-only threshold)

A job-level accounting-sufficient claim (sole or joint, §5.3) requires, in addition to waste accounting, a **frequency condition** whose form must be prescribed by the owner **before collection**. The owner must state, in the owner's own words, one of the following combination rules (this revision prescribes the *form* of the choice, not the choice itself, and not the threshold values):

- **Rule C (contributor-only):** thresholds apply to contributor frequency and spread only: contrib_freq(P) ≥ T_pre AND contrib_spread(P) ≥ Y_pre (for joint sets, contributor frequency/spread of the set's combined pre steps, stated explicitly). Trigger frequency is reported separately and does not affect pass/fail under Rule C (a trigger-only pattern therefore fails Rule C on contributor grounds, but its trigger finding remains reported as an episode-level trigger observation, §5.2).
- **Rule T (trigger-only):** thresholds apply to trigger frequency only: trigger_freq(P) ≥ T_trig (episode fraction; T_trig is a separate threshold value from T_pre, not the same number reused across denominators). Contributor frequency is reported separately and does not affect pass/fail under Rule T. Note: Rule T + waste (§5.3) together still require waste sufficiency — trigger frequency alone never establishes accounting-sufficiency (see §5.2 trigger-only worked rule).
- **Rule CT (combined, prescribed Boolean form):** thresholds apply to a stated combination, e.g. "(contributor condition AND trigger condition)" or "(contributor condition OR trigger condition)" or "(contributor condition AND (trigger condition OR contributor-spread condition))" — the exact Boolean form, and the threshold values T_pre, Y_pre, T_trig as applicable, must be stated verbatim in the authorisation. Any other combination (e.g. a weighted sum of contributor and trigger frequencies across different denominators) must be defined explicitly with its denominators stated; an undefined or post-hoc combination is invalid.

**Until a combination rule (C, T, or CT with its form) and its threshold values are prescribed before collection, job-level accounting-sufficient claims are INCONCLUSIVE on frequency grounds, even if waste accounting (§5.3) is satisfied and even if all three frequencies are computed.** Computed frequencies without a prescribed rule are descriptive only. Threshold values themselves (T_pre, Y_pre, T_trig) remain owner-set and unset by this revision — no value is invented here, and no value may be chosen after seeing the data.

**Job-level accounting-sufficient claim — full condition list (replaces REV-02 §4.4 conditions 1–6 as the claim rule, with terminology corrected per §6):**
1. Region and scope stated (failed, conformant, sequence-valid episodes, Level 2-P; successful, deviation, and non-conformant episodes listed separately and excluded from causal denominators).
2. Segment denominators stated: n_pre (contributor denominator), # failed episodes in scope (trigger denominator), n_post (descriptive denominator, post only). At steps not counted in n_pre; pre steps not counted in trigger numerator.
3. Frequency condition met under the owner-prescribed combination rule (§5.4, Rule C/T/CT) with threshold values set before collection.
4. Waste condition met (§5.3 sole or joint, per episode, for every failed episode in scope) with unassigned blocker checked per episode.
5. Shared mechanism caveat (if applicable): for L-SHARED, SEL/HEAD pattern claims remain pattern-only (REV-02 rule, retained); a Shared accounting-sufficient claim for Dec1/K2 as separate mechanisms is INCONCLUSIVE regardless of frequency and waste, unless intervention evidence (not authorised here) separates them.
6. Mechanism status stated separately (§6): every accounting-sufficient claim carries mechanism status INCONCLUSIVE (accounting only) unless intervention evidence is separately supplied and linked. An accounting-sufficient claim without a mechanism-status line is incomplete.

If any of conditions 1–6 fails, the job-level outcome is INCONCLUSIVE for the accounting-sufficient claim (not SUPPORTED, not NOT_SUPPORTED, unless an exact exclusion in §5.6 applies). In particular, frequency alone, waste alone, trigger frequency alone, and Table D prevalence alone each fail to establish a job-level accounting-sufficient claim.

### 5.5 Table C restructured (supersedes REV-02 §4.6 Table C)

Every future diagnostic report that makes a job-level claim must contain **Table C (causal aggregation)** with, per pattern class (and per K3 subcategory, separately) and per region:
- n_pre, count_P_pre, contrib_freq, contrib_spread (contributor quantities, §5.2);
- # failed episodes in scope, # with at-step class P, trigger_freq (trigger quantities, §5.2);
- waste_P per episode (pre+at, assigned buckets) and waste_unassigned_total per episode (with sub-buckets shown), slack_start per episode, f and at class per episode;
- frequency rule applied (C / T / CT with form and threshold values, or "NOT PRESCRIBED — claim INCONCLUSIVE on frequency grounds");
- accounting-sufficient status per episode (sole / joint / neither / blocked) and job-level status (sole / joint / INCONCLUSIVE, with scope stated);
- mechanism status line (§6): INCONCLUSIVE (accounting only) unless intervention evidence is listed.
Table D (descriptive prevalence, REV-02 §4.6, retained) must still be shown separately, labelled descriptive only, with post frequencies included only there (post frequency may also be shown in Table C as a clearly labelled descriptive row, never as a causal input).

### 5.6 Exact exclusions (retained from REV-02 §4.5, terminology corrected)

- Dec/K NOT_SUPPORTED for a region: every pre/at eligible step in the region is OPT (region waste zero) — failure's waste must come from elsewhere (other regions, unassigned buckets, deviation episodes reported separately). Job-level NOT_SUPPORTED only if all regions satisfy it (REV-02 rule, retained).
- K3 NOT_SUPPORTED for a region: every timing-critical pre/at step in the region is K-correct or partial-correct (fall-through, §2.3) — schedule cannot explain those steps' suboptimality (if any, they are HEAD/SEL). No timing-critical steps in the region ⇒ K3 N/A for the region, not NOT_SUPPORTED.
- Ex1-EVENT-as-sole-accounting-source NOT_SUPPORTED: crushes_total = 0 (REV-02 rule, relabelled: "sole cause" → "sole accounting source"; the exclusion is about crush events, not about mechanism). This does not exclude Ex-DEV, policy patterns, or other event patterns.
- Ex-DEV NOT_SUPPORTED for a job: every step CONFORMANT and every episode protocol-conformant (exact zero mismatches, REV-02 rule, retained).
- Job-level INCONCLUSIVE (default) for accounting-sufficient claims where any §5.4 condition fails, n_pre = 0, segments not reported separately, non-conformant/sequence-invalid episodes pooled into causal denominators, or region/scope not stated.

## 8. Bounded conclusion — what this revision fixes, and what remains unresolved (deliverable 6)

**Answer: REVISION-03 corrects the five identified internal inconsistencies and is ready for owner approval as a specification — with the remaining limits stated below, which are limits of authorisation, measurement, and mechanism evidence, not hidden inconsistencies in the corrected rules. This conclusion is about the specification's readiness, not about any diagnostic having succeeded. No diagnostic has succeeded, no mechanism has been established, and no trajectory has been collected under this revision.**

**What is now internally consistent (and was not, before this revision):**
- K subcategories now follow the specification's own definition of full estimates (§2): FULL requires currently-available full estimates and a true-periodic mismatch; PARTIAL requires currently-available partial features and a partial-semantics mismatch (including the NEVER case, corrected from REV-02's FULL label); UNRESOLVED covers provenance-missing cases as a separately defined status, never as an inferred FULL. The revised K table (§2.4) covers full, partial, never-available, and insufficient-provenance cases, plus K = 0 cases (BLANK/NEVER/UNK/STATUS-UNKNOWN) and field-missing/inconsistent cases, with waste buckets stated per row.
- Availability is no longer mistaken for attribution (§3): KBLANK/KNEVER are availability states (with individual feature availability recorded via R-FEAT or UNKNOWN), no bucket is labelled protocol, and every waste unit sits in exactly one bucket (assigned: SEL/HEAD/K3-FULL/K3-PARTIAL; unassigned: K3-UNRESOLVED/KBLANK/KNEVER/KUNK/SUBOPT-UNCLASS/FIELD-MISSING; descriptive: post, NONCONF-ARITHMETIC, waste-undefined counts). A future protocol claim must meet the three-part criterion in §3.3 and be owner-approved; it cannot be read off an availability flag.
- Populations and accounting reconcile at two levels (§4): Level 1 (all recorded steps, descriptive labels, no silent repair) and Level 2 per claim type (pattern/waste, transition-event, coverage) with explicit continuity, target, transition-type, blocked/crush/reached/door/tick, and protocol checks, a per-claim qualification matrix, and reconciliation identities (recorded − exclusions by reason code = causal, with a stated precedence for multiply-excluded steps). A future report that cannot fill in the reconciliation is incomplete for causal purposes, visibly, rather than silently dropping steps between definition and denominator.
- Contributor and trigger frequencies are distinct quantities with distinct denominators (§5.2), and the frequency rule is an owner-prescribed combination (Rule C / T / CT with form verbatim) rather than a single pre-only threshold that could exclude or silently absorb triggers (§5.4). A trigger-only pattern's status is now determined explicitly: contributor frequency 0, trigger frequency as computed, waste sufficiency checked per episode (at-step waste alone vs slack + 1), and job-level outcome governed by the prescribed rule — neither automatic exclusion nor automatic causation.
- Language is consistent (§6): "waste-sufficient" means the arithmetic result only; "sole/joint waste-sufficient (accounting)" replace "sole/joint cause" for waste results; "inevitability step (trigger observation)" replaces "attributable step" and causal uses of "trigger"; and every accounting-sufficient claim carries mechanism status INCONCLUSIVE (accounting only) unless intervention evidence is separately supplied. The audit rule in §6 makes this checkable in future conclusions, not merely aspirational.

**Remaining limits — stated explicitly, so that "ready for owner approval" is not misread as "ready for collection", "diagnosis complete", or "mechanism proved":**
1. **Thresholds, combination rule, and several definitions are still unset or unapproved** (decisions 1–8, 12 in §7).
